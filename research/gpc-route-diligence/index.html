Great Plains: named routes, endpoints and rights

Prioritize OPTK Omaha–Denver capacity, Illinois–Indiana enterprise access and a priced Michigan handoff. The evidence now reaches named facilities; connection rights and route economics are the next gate.

Public-source review as of September 20, 2026. Exchange and facility listings are dated observations, not certified live circuits. Proposed structures and rankings are analytical judgments. Residential FCC availability is not used as proof of enterprise route reach.

Start with three commercial tests

The first screen is a priced service or rights transaction at exact endpoints. A whole-company acquisition becomes attractive only if it adds transferable customer margin or network control beyond the same-endpoint wholesale alternative. These are proposed diligence priorities, not indications of seller willingness or buyer mandate.

The evidence needed before paying an ownership premium

  • Exact A/Z buildings, meet-me rooms and accepted demarcations; live circuit identifiers, capacity, LOA/CFA and current local-versus-remote exchange arrangements.
  • As-built route and shared-risk groups: ducts, bridges, crossings, splice points, regeneration, entrances, power and underlying third-party carriers. A different corporate owner does not prove diversity.
  • Cable, conduit, easement, lease and IRU ownership or use rights by span; remaining terms, maintenance obligations, grant constraints, liens and assignment consents.
  • Signed enterprise demand, avoidable off-net cost, recurring contribution after every leased gap, connection capex and replacement cost. Price wholesale, IRU and ownership at the same endpoints and service level.
  • A proposed route fails the investment test if useful rights cannot be conveyed, required gap capex consumes the margin, or a contract captures the same benefit at lower total cost.
