Hamilton Telecommunications / Nebraska Central Telephone Company

Independent operator screen | P1: investigate

Current ownership

Nedelco-controlled, with material Nelson family interests and a charitable holder in the 2021 FCC cap table. Nedelco then held 20% of NebraskaLink. Private, not cooperative.

Commercial fit (inference)

[I] Best Nebraska control target: central-state FTTH density plus an OPTK stake.

Possible transaction or partnership

Acquire or recapitalize Hamilton Telephone, NCTC, Hamilton.net and the OPTK stake with family rollover. Exclude relay, NG911 and other national services if separable.

Constraints

Mixed holding-company perimeter; ILEC/ETC approvals; Family, charitable and OPTK consents

Countercase

A telecom carve-out may strand shared systems; buying all Nedelco adds noncore operations.

Geographic clusters

  • Aurora
  • Grand Island
  • Gibbon
  • Central City
  • Burwell
  • 21 central Nebraska exchanges

Construction evidence

  • Hamilton’s September19,2025 notice lists20exchanges with FTTH deployed before planned copper retirement on or afterDecember30,2025. The notice does not verify retirement completion.

Reported scale and date

  • 3,272 service-area square miles added in 2021
  • approximately 3,850 access lines in the 2021 FCC filing
  • 20% of NebraskaLink held by Nedelco in 2021; current stake unknown

Socket / IdeaTek Midwest platform

Scaled strategic platform | P2: selective

Current ownership

Oak Hill and Pamlico-backed Midwest platform; TPG Peppertree reinvested alongside them in 2025. Socket and IdeaTek retain distinct brands and local management. Socket acquired Fastwyre Missouri in April2026.

Commercial fit (inference)

The Missouri-Kansas platform offers residential scale and a hypothetical combination with Omni. Physical connection, incremental customers and enterprise corridor synergies require route-level diligence; state presence does not establish them.

Possible transaction or partnership

Monitor for sponsor-level strategic combination, selected market swaps or reciprocal wholesale. Near-term whole-company acquisition is unlikely while Oak Hill and Pamlico fund growth and integrate Fastwyre.

Constraints

Active sponsor-backed consolidator, current Fastwyre integration, potentially meaningful overlap, and a stated 2028 build target. Exact Missouri passings, subscribers, churn and fiber-versus-legacy plant for acquired exchanges are undisclosed. IdeaTek is already part of the Socket platform, not an independent Kansas-owned seller.

Countercase

Socket may be a better-funded Missouri competitor than a seller, and buying overlapping FTTH could destroy value if penetration is low or upgrade capital remains high.

Geographic clusters

  • Columbia
  • Jefferson City
  • Fulton and Callaway County
  • Moberly and Mexico
  • Hannibal and Kirksville
  • St. Louis western suburbs
  • Sedalia and Warrensburg
  • Nevada
  • Hickory County
  • Webb City and southwest Missouri
  • central Kansas
  • Wichita region
  • Dodge City
  • Garden City
  • statewide Freestate corridor

Construction evidence

  • Socket passed more than 100,000 premises as of 2025-07-08 and announced expansion into Webb City, Odessa, Eureka, Nevada and Harrisonville. The announced Fastwyre Missouri perimeter added 16 markets; current Socket pages list former Fastwyre towns as Socket service areas.
  • IdeaTek: Reports 8,500-plus fiber miles and 120-plus communities on an undated current company page accessed 2026-09-20; route-versus-strand basis is not disclosed.
  • IdeaTek: Partner on the planned state-owned Freestate open-access network, described by IdeaTek on 2025-06-06 and by NTIA's current award page accessed 2026-09-20.

Reported scale and date

  • Socket reported more than 100,000 premises passed on 2025-07-08. Socket and IdeaTek target more than 500,000 homes passed by 2028. The acquired Fastwyre Missouri business was described at signing as 16 markets serving more than 25,000 households; this is not a verified subscriber count.
  • IdeaTek: 75,000-plus customers, current page accessed 2026-09-20
  • IdeaTek: 8,500-plus fiber miles, undated current company page accessed 2026-09-20; route-versus-strand basis unspecified.
  • Kansas state-owned Freestate project: planned 682-mile open-access middle-mile project, described by IdeaTek on 2025-06-06 and on NTIA's current award page accessed 2026-09-20; $42.5 million federal award and $69.7 million total project cost; separate public asset, not IdeaTek-owned mileage.

ALLO Communications

Scaled strategic platform | P2: selective

Current ownership

Private with SDC, Nelnet and founder/management. Nelnet held 27% economic and 20% voting rights at 2025 year-end; SDC percentage undisclosed.

Commercial fit (inference)

[I] Sponsor combination, market swap or leased-network partner, not bolt-on.

Possible transaction or partnership

Sponsor-to-sponsor merger, Nebraska market swap, joint leased-network build or minority recap.

Constraints

Leverage; Nebraska overlap; Multiple investors; No proven Kansas residential FTTH cluster

Countercase

Large debt and duplicate Nebraska plant can make a premium transaction dilutive.

Geographic clusters

  • 40 Nebraska communities
  • Lincoln
  • Omaha suburbs
  • Grand Island
  • Kearney
  • Kansas City business market
  • Colorado: ten communities reported on June 12, 2025, concentrated in Weld, Adams, Morgan, Boulder and Summit counties; Greeley construction completed in June 2025 and Erie construction remained active after a July 2025 stop-work order was lifted.
  • Arizona: City of Yuma retail FTTP plus Yuma County middle mile. The county owns the middle-mile network and ALLO operates and markets it to carriers.
  • Boulder, Colorado: planned citywide last mile is no longer a build cluster. ALLO withdrew on July 30, 2026 after beginning limited work in 2025.

Construction evidence

  • Completed 342 Nebraska fiber miles to more than 1,450 addresses over 18 months, announced 2026-03-17.
  • Greeley's citywide all-fiber build, begun August 2021, was declared complete June 12, 2025.
  • Erie construction began October 2022; the Town lifted its latest stop-work order on July 8, 2025.
  • Yuma County reports completed western phases and an eastern middle-mile segment at 76% overall on the current page, but the table has no visible update date.
  • Boulder reported ALLO would not perform the 2025 lease requiring 80% coverage by 2028 and 97% by 2030.

Reported scale and date

  • approximately 157,000 residential customers and 74,000 business lines at 2025-12-31
  • approximately $260 million 2025 revenue
  • $1.41 billion debt at 2025-12-31
  • 157,000 residential customers and approximately 74,000 business lines nationwide at December 31, 2025.
  • $260 million nationwide revenue for calendar 2025 and $1.41 billion debt outstanding at December 31, 2025.
  • $20.7 million of Yuma County ARPA funding for phase one and a $10 million Arizona broadband grant for phase two; these fund the county-owned middle-mile perimeter, not ALLO corporate equity or all Arizona FTTP.
  • $500 million gross financing completed in June 2025 at ALLO Holdings.

Fastwyre Broadband Nebraska business

Committed or integrated platform | P3: partner / monitor

Current ownership

GPC signed a definitive agreement on May19,2026. Nebraska approved C-5709 on August18, and FCC international authority was granted September18, but the separate domestic application remained identified as pending; legal close is unverified.

Commercial fit (inference)

[I] Adjacency and upgrade value are already contracted; remaining issue is closing and integration.

Possible transaction or partnership

No origination. Track C-5709, confirm close, inventory fiber versus copper, then stage migration and capex.

Constraints

Do not treat Nebraska approval or an international authorization as proof of closing. Domestic authorization and consummation must be confirmed.

Countercase

A copper-heavy perimeter could require more upgrade capital than the customer base supports.

Geographic clusters

  • 26 named communities including Bellevue, Blair, Fremont, Wayne and Winnebago

Construction evidence

  • GPC committed to upgrade and integrate after closing; this is a target, not completed construction.

Reported scale and date

  • 26 communities in announcement
  • more than two dozen employees expected to transfer
  • passings, subscribers, fiber miles and financials undisclosed
