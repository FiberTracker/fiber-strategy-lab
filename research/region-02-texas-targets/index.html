Southwest Arkansas Telephone Cooperative (SWAT First)

Cooperative / municipal partner | P2: selective

Current ownership

Member-owned cooperative; current company materials identify ILEC/CLEC operations in Arkansas and Texas. No announced sale or change of control found as of 2026-09-20.

Commercial fit (inference)

Priority 1 commercial partner. Cass County and the Texarkana headquarters make this the tightest physical adjacency to Rightfiber's existing Texas node. It can improve rural reach and redundancy without requiring immediate change of control.

Possible transaction or partnership

Propose reciprocal wholesale/off-net access, NNI and redundant transport around Texarkana, followed by a co-build or operating-services agreement for Cass County. An acquisition should be framed only as a member-benefit strategic combination after commercial proof, not as a conventional sponsor sale.

Constraints

The 2023 bylaws require approval by at least three-quarters of all cooperative members for covered fundamental proposals. Federal or state funding, ETC duties, patronage interests and rural service commitments would need perimeter-specific diligence.

Countercase

Small rural density and member governance can outweigh adjacency. Current revenue, EBITDA, fiber miles, homes passed and unencumbered asset perimeter are unknown.

Geographic clusters

  • Texarkana
  • Cass County, Texas
  • Miller, Hempstead, Howard and Columbia counties, Arkansas

Construction evidence

  • Current company FAQ, accessed 2026-09-20, says SWAT provides fiber internet and telephone service across five counties in two states over a 100% fiber backbone.
  • USDA announced a $10.412 million telecommunications infrastructure loan on 2020-06-24 to upgrade portions of the Arkansas and Texas network to FTTH; the project target was 4,612 subscribers, not a reported current subscriber count.

Reported scale and date

  • 5 counties served (current page accessed 2026-09-20; Cass TX plus four Arkansas counties)
  • 4,612 target subscribers (2020-06-24; USDA-funded upgrade; target, not actual)

Peoples Telephone Cooperative / Peoples Communications

Cooperative / municipal partner | P2: selective

Current ownership

Member-owned Texas cooperative with cooperative-controlled operating activities; no announced sale or change of control found as of 2026-09-20.

Commercial fit (inference)

Priority 2. Peoples offers the broadest disclosed Northeast Texas commercial network among the cooperatives and a current 400G multi-core network, making wholesale, mobile backhaul and enterprise handoff more credible than a pure residential tuck-in.

Possible transaction or partnership

Lead with transport/IRU, mobile backhaul and enterprise referral arrangements between the Rightfiber build cluster and Peoples' East Texas backbone. Explore a jointly funded extension or a subsidiary-level investment only after mapping grants, ILEC assets and member territory.

Constraints

The March 2025 bylaws require two-thirds of all members for a substantial property sale, with a narrower cooperative-to-cooperative path still requiring member approval. Mixed fiber and fixed-wireless connections cannot be treated as FTTH passings.

Countercase

The network is strategically useful but may remain permanently held for member service. Current financials, take rate, FTTH passings, debt and grant completion are not public.

Geographic clusters

  • Quitman and Wood County
  • 19 East Texas counties
  • I-30 and east-of-Dallas rural corridor

Construction evidence

  • Current company page, accessed 2026-09-20, reports more than 26,000 connections across 19 East Texas counties and offers fiber and fixed wireless.
  • Company history says a 2010 federal grant funded more than 600 miles of East Texas fiber backbone across 13 counties. This is historical route mileage, not current FTTH passings.
  • USDA awarded $3.024604 million on 2020-02-28 for FTTP to 914 households and two critical facilities in Titus, Camp and Wood counties; award and target are not completion evidence.

Reported scale and date

  • 26,000 connections (current page accessed 2026-09-20; all technologies across 19 East Texas counties; not subscribers or fiber passings)
  • 600 route miles of historical backbone (grant received 2010-08-18; East Texas backbone across 13 counties)

Etex Telephone Cooperative and Etex Communications

Cooperative / municipal partner | P2: selective

Current ownership

Etex Telephone Cooperative is member-owned; Etex Communications, LLC is its affiliate/subsidiary. No announced sale or change of control found as of 2026-09-20. Do not confuse Etex with Eastex Telephone Cooperative.

Commercial fit (inference)

Priority 3. Etex fills the Gilmer-to-Marshall/Cass County band south of I-30 and has demonstrated willingness to use public-private partnerships. The Etex Communications affiliate may provide a more practical commercial perimeter than the regulated cooperative.

Possible transaction or partnership

Pursue reciprocal transport, school/enterprise fiber, construction services and co-funded extensions. Test whether Etex Communications can accept minority capital, contribute off-footprint assets to a JV or sell selected competitive assets while the cooperative retains ILEC/member control.

Constraints

Etex bylaws require at least two-thirds of all members to approve sale or encumbrance of a substantial portion of property. The disclosed 12,600 count mixes members and customers, and current fiber miles, passings and capital needs are unknown.

Countercase

Strong local franchise and cooperative mission reduce sale probability. Some current plans remain copper-based, so the whole perimeter is not fiber.

Geographic clusters

  • Gilmer and Upshur County
  • Cass, Harrison, Marion, Morris and surrounding Northeast Texas counties
  • Harleton, Center and Palestine partnership markets

Construction evidence

  • Current company page, accessed 2026-09-20, reports more than 12,600 members and customers across 710 square miles and identifies both DSL and fiber service.
  • Etex's press archive reports public-private fiber partnerships in Palestine on 2020-06-01, Center on 2023-12-01 and Harleton on 2024-11-01. These announcements do not establish completed passings.

Reported scale and date

  • 12,600 members and customers (current page accessed 2026-09-20; combined cooperative/customer count; not fiber subscribers)
  • 710 square miles of service area (current page accessed 2026-09-20; rural East Texas)

Lyte Fiber

Scaled strategic platform | P2: selective

Current ownership

Growth platform formed with SDC Capital Partners; exact equity split is not public. No announced sale found as of 2026-09-20. Lyte closed a $175 million construction facility on 2026-05-12.

Commercial fit (inference)

Priority 4. Marshall and Marion County are attractive south-of-Texarkana adjacencies, but Lyte is an active sponsor-backed builder and direct competitor. A merger or targeted East Texas asset partnership has more logic than a small bolt-on purchase.

Possible transaction or partnership

Open sponsor-to-sponsor dialogue around an East Texas joint venture, reciprocal transport or a broader combination. Alternatively, test a future carve-out if SDC prioritizes other Texas clusters. Rightfiber's $1.6 billion facility gives it funding capacity, but Lyte has no disclosed immediate capital shortfall.

Constraints

New-build ramp, state-award obligations, construction facility covenants, integration risk and sponsor valuation expectations. Announced BSLs and target passings must not be treated as live service.

Countercase

The 2026 debt raise and stated acquisition capacity are evidence Lyte intends to grow independently. It may be a competitor for the same communities rather than a partner.

Geographic clusters

  • Marshall and Harrison County
  • Jefferson and Marion County
  • Navarro County
  • other noncontiguous Texas markets

Construction evidence

  • Lyte connected its first Marshall customers on 2025-10-13 and targeted more than 8,000 homes and businesses with more than 120 route miles; completion was expected in early 2026, but full completion was not independently confirmed.
  • Texas' September 2025 report lists Lyte awards of $10.142583 million for 2,253 BSLs in Marion County and $30.400781 million for 4,488 BSLs in Navarro County. Awards and BSLs are not completed construction or passings.
  • The $175 million 2026 facility, with an accordion, was stated to fund organic builds, government-supported projects and acquisitions.

Reported scale and date

  • 8,000 target homes and businesses (2025-10-13; initial Marshall phase; target)
  • 120 target route miles (2025-10-13; Marshall build; target)
  • 175,000,000 USD construction loan facility (2026-05-12; companywide; closed financing)

Nextlink Internet (AMG Technology Holdings / Investment Group)

Scaled strategic platform | P2: selective

Current ownership

Founder and CEO Bill Baker was identified as majority owner in the 2024 financing announcement. Cable One held approximately 22% as of 2026-03-31. No announced sale found as of 2026-09-20.

Commercial fit (inference)

Priority 5. Nextlink has the strongest disclosed overlap with Rightfiber's North Texas build and Northeast Texas corridor, but it is a large hybrid wireless/fiber platform. It is more likely a bilateral recap, partnership or selected-asset discussion than a conventional bolt-on.

Possible transaction or partnership

Approach founder and Cable One together for a strategic combination, minority recap or a ring-fenced Texas fiber contribution. A commercial alternative is reciprocal off-net access and transport in overlapping or adjoining towns while avoiding duplicative construction.

Constraints

Multistate operations, fixed-wireless assets, federal/state award obligations, minority rights, debt and shared network systems complicate a carve-out. Current revenue, EBITDA, fiber-only passings and leverage are not public.

Countercase

Nextlink has repeatedly raised growth capital and describes itself as a national rural broadband builder. Direct overlap may produce competition rather than partnership, and the scale may exceed a bolt-on mandate.

Geographic clusters

  • Seagoville, Ennis, Forney and Mabank overlap
  • Canton, Grand Saline, Sulphur Springs and Naples
  • Morris County
  • broader rural Texas

Construction evidence

  • Current company page, accessed 2026-09-20, reports more than 100,000 homes and businesses served across 12 states and more than $1 billion invested. The service count mixes fixed wireless and fiber.
  • On 2024-08-15 Nextlink announced more than $100 million of new capital, including $20 million equity from Cable One and $80 million debt led by CanAm, targeting more than 50,000 additional rural and small-town fiber locations.
  • Texas' September 2025 report lists a $17.843383 million Morris County award for 1,903 BSLs. The current Texas service page also lists several Rightfiber target cities, but it does not establish that every listed city is a fiber market.

Reported scale and date

  • 100,000 homes and businesses served (current page accessed 2026-09-20; 12 states, mixed fixed wireless and fiber)
  • 100,000,000 USD-plus capital raise (2024-08-15; companywide; announced infusion)
  • 1,903 funded BSLs (Texas report 2025-09; Morris County award; not passings)

Vexus Fiber, part of the Metronet network platform

Committed or integrated platform | P3: partner / monitor

Current ownership

Not independent. FCC approval on 2025-07-09 covered Vexus in the transfer to MetroNet Systems, a joint venture with 50% voting/equity held by T-Mobile and 50% voting and approximately 49.69% equity held by a KKR vehicle, with management holding the remainder. T-Mobile reported the Metronet joint acquisition closed on 2025-07-24.

Commercial fit (inference)

Priority 6. Tyler, Nacogdoches and Denison provide regional relevance, but Vexus is locked inside a much larger T-Mobile/KKR fiber strategy. Treat it as a wholesale, interconnection or selective enterprise-transport counterparty only.

Possible transaction or partnership

Route any discussion through Metronet's network/wholesale organization and T-Mobile for customer-facing matters. Test NNIs, carrier transport or non-residential dark fiber. Do not approach Vexus as an independent whole-company seller.

Constraints

Joint-venture governance, T-Mobile ownership of residential customer relationships, KKR rights and a national 2030 build plan. Sub-platform disposal would be strategically inconsistent absent a non-core asset package.

Countercase

The corporate structure and growth mandate eliminate the simple seller thesis. Local overlap alone is not enough to overcome control and strategic constraints.

Geographic clusters

  • Tyler and Nacogdoches
  • Denison
  • other Texas Vexus markets

Construction evidence

  • Vexus reported on 2024-06-12 that 1,500 Denison homes and businesses had service available and targeted more than 12,000 by year-end, against a $20 million investment. Availability is not subscribers.
  • T-Mobile's 2025 Form 10-K confirms the Metronet joint acquisition closed on 2025-07-24 and that T-Mobile owns the customer relationships while the JV focuses on network build and installation.

Reported scale and date

  • 1,500 homes and businesses with service available (2024-06-12; Denison actual availability at announcement)
  • 12,000 target homes and businesses (target by 2024-12-31; Denison target, not confirmed actual)

Ezee Fiber, including acquired Tachus Fiber Internet

Scaled strategic platform | P2: selective

Current ownership

Ezee is wholly owned by I Squared Capital. It announced an agreement to acquire Tachus from Crosstimbers Capital Group on 2025-07-07 and reported the acquisition closed by 2026-08-04. Tachus is no longer an independent seller.

Commercial fit (inference)

Priority 7. Ezee/Tachus has substantial scale but is centered on Houston, not the Texarkana or east/southeast Dallas clusters. It is a sponsor-backed consolidator and geographic countercase, not a Rightfiber bolt-on.

Possible transaction or partnership

No near-term acquisition route. Consider carrier transport or roaming/off-net cooperation only if Rightfiber develops a statewide Texas backbone or needs Houston interconnection. Any strategic dialogue belongs at I Squared/Ezee level.

Constraints

Nonadjacent footprint, recent integration, six-state growth program and I Squared control. The platform reports no debt and has its own acquisition agenda, limiting an obvious financing need.

Countercase

Scale is attractive in isolation, but buying it would change the geographic mandate and transaction size. Treating Tachus separately would misstate the closed 2025-2026 deal sequence.

Geographic clusters

  • Greater Houston
  • The Woodlands and northern Houston
  • Dayton, Texas

Construction evidence

  • At announcement on 2025-07-07, Tachus had more than 60,000 residential and business subscribers and the combined company expected more than 600,000 fiber passings across Houston, New Mexico and Washington by year-end 2025.
  • Ezee reported on 2026-08-04 that the Tachus acquisition had closed, that it had surpassed 600,000 FTTH passings by year-end 2025, and that it remained debt-free. These are company-reported actuals, not independently audited figures.

Reported scale and date

  • 60,000 Tachus residential and business subscribers (2025-07-07 announcement; Greater Houston; more than 60,000)
  • 600,000 FTTH passings (actual reported for 2025-12-31 on 2026-08-04; combined multistate Ezee platform; more than 600,000)
