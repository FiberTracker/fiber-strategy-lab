Lumos

Committed or integrated platform | P3: partner / monitor

Current ownership

Acquired on April 1, 2025 by a joint venture owned 50% by T-Mobile and 50% by EQT Infrastructure VI. Lumos now operates as a wholesale network platform, with T-Mobile the anchor tenant and owner of residential and small-business customer relationships.

Commercial fit (inference)

[I] Strategically important infrastructure partner, but low-priority acquisition target because the control transaction closed recently and committed capital is already in place. Distance penalty is high as expansion now spans numerous states.

Possible transaction or partnership

[I] Pursue wholesale, carrier, build-partner or local tuck-in opportunities, not a near-term control sale. Any financing dialogue should recognize T-Mobile's disclosed additional capital commitment for 2027 to 2028.

Constraints

Closed 50/50 strategic JV with an anchor tenant and defined wholesale model.; Public build figures mix homes reached, customers, route miles and forward targets; none are interchangeable.

Countercase

[I] The platform's capital plan, strategic anchor and recent acquisition materially reduce near-term advisory openness despite its scale.

Geographic clusters

  • Triad and central NC legacy footprint
  • Durham and Orange counties, NC
  • Johnston and Harnett counties, NC
  • New Hanover County, NC
  • Greenville, Spartanburg, Richland and Lexington counties, SC
  • Hampton Roads
  • Roanoke-Lynchburg corridor
  • Shenandoah Valley and Charlottesville
  • Alleghany Highlands

Construction evidence

  • Announced and under construction: Lumos announced nearly $100 million and 775 miles for Greenville County in October 2023; announced $56 million and 655 miles in New Hanover County in April 2023, then said construction was beginning in February 2024.
  • Awarded for 2026 delivery, not verified complete: NCDIT awarded Lumos $3.965221 million for 402 Orange County locations in March 2026.
  • Target, not actual: the JV stated a goal of reaching 3.5 million homes by year-end 2028.
  • The current Virginia service page lists operating or marketed communities including Virginia Beach, Chesapeake, Portsmouth, Roanoke, Lynchburg, Bedford, Charlottesville, Harrisonburg, Staunton and Waynesboro.
  • The Botetourt County VATI project reported 1,901 of 1,901 project passings with access and 269.73 fiber miles through August 2025. This is a grant-project perimeter, not the Virginia or company footprint.

Reported scale and date

  • 475,000 homes with connectivity and 7,500 network miles at the joint-venture close on April1,2025, companywide.
  • Approximately97,000 fiber customers transferred to T-Mobile at the April1,2025 close, companywide.
  • 3.5million homes passed by end-2028 is an announced target, not an actual.

Glo Fiber / Horizon, owned by Shenandoah Telecommunications Company

Public-company operating brand | P3: partner / monitor

Current ownership

Public-company owned. Nasdaq-listed Shentel completed its acquisition of Horizon Telcom on 2024-04-01 and said it would rebrand Horizon's commercial and residential fiber businesses to Glo Fiber.

Commercial fit (inference)

Top strategic alternative because Horizon supplies both Ohio commercial fiber and FTTH beachheads. It can compete for enterprise, carrier, dark-fiber, and residential economics, so Omni should not pitch itself as uniquely carrier-ready without proving routes, buildings, and contracted wholesale demand.

Possible transaction or partnership

Potential strategic buyer, interconnection, or wholesale counterparty inference. Shentel is public and already executed the Horizon acquisition; no evidence shows it is seeking Omni or divesting Ohio.

Constraints

Latest SEC scale is consolidated across eight states. Ohio build announcements are targets or community-level counts and are not reconciled to the 2026 SEC passings table by state.

Countercase

Glo's principal Ohio clusters are south-central and southeast of much of Omni's northern build, so overlap may be lower than the statewide map suggests. Public-company leverage and growth targets may also constrain another large acquisition.

Geographic clusters

  • Ross County incumbent market
  • Chillicothe-Circleville-Lancaster-Washington Court House
  • Zanesville-Hillsboro-Jackson-Johnstown-Greenfield expansion corridor
  • Shenandoah Valley
  • Central Virginia and Campbell County
  • Virginia expansion markets within an eight-state company footprint

Construction evidence

  • On 2024-04-17, Glo announced engineering work for more than 40,000 additional Ohio homes and businesses in Zanesville, Hillsboro, Jackson, Johnstown, and Greenfield, while reporting service in Chillicothe, Circleville, Lancaster, and Washington Court House.
  • On 2025-02-03, Glo said more than 4,000 Lancaster homes and businesses already had service and construction would add approximately 10,000 more, with expected completion in the second half of 2025. Completion was not independently verified here.
  • Shentel reported completion of a more than $20 million Campbell County project reaching more than 4,100 previously unserved homes on 2026-05-19.
  • The 2025 Form 10-K reports a $5 million Virginia FTTH business acquisition closed on 2025-07-09, adding approximately 1,500 passings and 700 customers. This was an acquired retail operating asset, not a cooperative operating partnership.

Reported scale and date

  • 475,677 Glo Fiber expansion-market homes and businesses passed as of 2026-06-30 across the multi-state Shentel perimeter.
  • 19,847 fiber route miles as of 2026-06-30 across Shentel's integrated multi-state network.
  • 100,155 Glo Fiber expansion-market Residential and SMB RGUs as of 2026-06-30; this is not Ohio-only and should not be treated as subscribers.
  • 475,677 Glo expansion passings and 100,155 Glo RGUs at 2026-06-30, companywide.
  • 728,736 total company passings and 19,847 route miles at 2026-06-30 across eight states.
  • Glo construction substantially complete by end-2026 is management guidance, not an actual.

All Points Broadband

Scaled strategic platform | P2: selective

Current ownership

Privately held and Searchlight Capital Partners-backed. Searchlight is identified as the lead equity sponsor; the reviewed public sources do not disclose current voting ownership percentages.

Commercial fit (inference)

Best sponsor-to-sponsor dialogue in the set. All Points combines rural retail operations with electric-utility and county partnerships, but its Virginia clusters are dispersed. Pulaski is the clearest RightFiber adjacency; most northern and central clusters are remote from RightFiber's retail footprint and noncontiguous with Omni's current markets.

Possible transaction or partnership

Sponsor recapitalization, growth equity, county-project financing or selected operating partnership. No public evidence shows the company is for sale.

Constraints

mixed legacy fiber and fixed-wireless operations; make-ready exposure; dispersed county clusters; grant and utility dependencies

Countercase

Completed Northern Neck, King William and Middlesex projects prove delivery, but slow Loudoun and Northern Shenandoah progress and Augusta rescoping show that awarded locations do not equal constructed passings.

Geographic clusters

  • Northern Neck and Middle Peninsula
  • Loudoun and Hanover
  • Northern Shenandoah Valley and Augusta
  • Pulaski and New River Valley

Construction evidence

  • Completed August 2025 VATI ledgers included 3,411 of 3,411 Northern Neck project passings, 2,236 of 2,236 King William passings and 970 of 970 Middlesex passings with access.
  • The same ledger showed Hanover at 1,363 of 6,198, Loudoun at 249 of 8,629 and the Northern Shenandoah Valley project at 193 of 32,238; the latter was flagged high risk due to make-ready delays.
  • Pulaski's more than $55 million project broke ground on 2025-09-29 with a stated target of 7,800 homes, businesses and community buildings. That is a planned project perimeter, not completed passings.
  • Augusta County reported in April 2026 that some residents were online, construction was continuing and a portion of the project had been rescoped after higher make-ready and other costs.

Reported scale and date

  • Eleven Virginia counties with construction completed or underway as stated on 2025-09-29.
  • The 32,238-location Northern Shenandoah Valley and 7,800-location Pulaski figures are project targets, not companywide actual passings.
  • No current companywide FTTH passing or subscriber total was found in a primary public source.

RiverStreet

Cooperative / municipal partner | P2: selective

Current ownership

Member-owned cooperative rooted in Wilkes Telephone Membership Corporation. RiverStreet is the unified operating name for Wilkes Communications and RiverStreet Networks.

Commercial fit (inference)

Consider project-specific completion capital or operating transfers, subject to cooperative and public-partner consent. The West Piedmont project lost RiverStreet as provider in August2026 following a funding shortfall; this is not proof of a statewide exit or company sale.

Possible transaction or partnership

[I] Cooperative merger, adjacent rural-system acquisition, RUS or bank debt, grant bridge financing and shared-services combinations are more credible than a sponsor buyout.

Constraints

Cooperative governance, documented project-level financing shortfall and public obligations. The status and remaining cost of each retained project must be verified separately.

Countercase

[I] The operating dispersion may outweigh scale benefits for a buyer seeking a compact Carolinas platform.

Geographic clusters

  • Wilkes and neighboring western NC counties
  • Stokes County, NC
  • Beaufort, Hyde and Washington counties, NC
  • Camden and Currituck project areas, NC
  • multiple rural Virginia counties
  • Southside Virginia
  • West Piedmont historical award area
  • Dinwiddie and eastern project counties

Construction evidence

  • Current build: RiverStreet says its multi-phase Stokes County plan is designed to reach more than 6,770 homes and businesses, with areas available, under construction and in future engineering as of the page reviewed.
  • Awarded, not completed: NCDIT announced a 2025 CAB award for 921 Camden County homes and businesses.
  • Awarded for 2026 delivery, not verified complete: NCDIT awarded $959,828.18 for 306 locations across Camden, Currituck, Stokes and Wilkes counties in March 2026.
  • The August 2025 VATI ledger showed 8,167 of 24,641 East-project passings, 31 of 10,056 West-project passings and zero of 5,009 Dinwiddie project passings with access; all three were flagged high risk.
  • RiverStreet's current project page says BEAD expansion required redesign and refinancing and acknowledges delays since March 2025.
  • On 2026-08-05, the West Piedmont commission terminated RiverStreet's West-project role after it could not secure remaining capital and contracted with ZiTEL for the remaining scope.

Reported scale and date

  • Roughly 30,000 customers across rural North Carolina and Virginia, current company page accessed 2026-09-20
  • More than 6,770 planned Stokes County homes and businesses, page dated 2025-11-23
  • 17 North Carolina serviceable counties listed on the current company site
  • RiverStreet's November 2024 Virginia BEAD letter claimed approximately 14,300 Virginia customers and 36,900 North Carolina customers. These are applicant-reported customers, not independently audited state counts.
  • The same letter described more than 6,300 planned Virginia route miles and more than 74,500 target locations across nine counties. These are build-plan figures, not completed network scale.

Firefly Fiber Broadband / Central Virginia Electric Cooperative

Cooperative / municipal partner | P2: selective

Current ownership

Firefly is a wholly owned subsidiary of member-owned Central Virginia Electric Cooperative. CVEC owns the core electric-cooperative network; Firefly operates the ISP service and works through utility and county partnerships.

Commercial fit (inference)

High-quality operating benchmark and possible wholesale or service partner. Low M&A fit because member-owned governance, CVEC network ownership and utility partnerships split the economic and asset perimeters.

Possible transaction or partnership

Lease, interconnection, managed service or project-specific partnership. A conventional acquisition of Firefly would not automatically convey the CVEC core network or partner utility assets.

Constraints

cooperative governance; CVEC ownership of core network; partner-owned infrastructure; grant and make-ready dependencies

Countercase

Strong connection growth proves operating traction, but it does not convert the cooperative network into an available retail asset.

Geographic clusters

  • Central Virginia
  • CVEC member territory
  • RISE partnership area across 13 counties

Construction evidence

  • CVEC completed its approximately $130 million, 4,500-mile core network in 2022 to make service available to roughly 39,000 members; the network is owned by CVEC.
  • The August 2025 VATI ledger showed Firefly's Thomas Jefferson project at 24,162 of 36,225 project passings with access and 2,668 fiber miles; it was flagged high risk due to make-ready, railroad and right-of-way delays.
  • Firefly identifies Rappahannock Electric Cooperative, Dominion Energy and Appalachian Power as network or infrastructure partners across the expansion perimeter.

Reported scale and date

  • More than 48,000 service connections as of 2026-08-06, actual company connections.
  • The 13-county RISE footprint is a partnership perimeter, not ownership of every underlying route or pole asset.

Point Broadband / Clearwave Fiber

Scaled strategic platform | P2: selective

Current ownership

Point Broadband and Clearwave Fiber closed their combination on 2026-05-04. FCC approval describes joint control by GTCR and Berkshire Partners after closing. The operating platform announced a further Visionary Broadband combination on 2026-09-08, but that transaction was signed, not reported closed, by the screen date.

Commercial fit (inference)

Best geographic match to RightFiber because Southwest Virginia sits next to RightFiber's Tennessee and Kentucky retail states. However, Point is already a sponsor-controlled consolidator executing two combinations in 2026, so it is a peer or interconnection counterparty rather than a plausible tuck-in. Omni's current footprint is more distant and noncontiguous.

Possible transaction or partnership

Carrier interconnection, reciprocal route access or broader strategic combination. Near-term sale to Omni or RightFiber is inconsistent with the active Clearwave and Visionary consolidation path.

Constraints

active integration; joint sponsor control; pending Visionary transaction; large multi-state perimeter

Countercase

Southwest Virginia adjacency is strategically compelling for RightFiber, but geography alone does not overcome Point's current buyer and consolidator posture.

Geographic clusters

  • Southwest Virginia
  • Mount Rogers region
  • Cumberland Plateau
  • Bristol and former OptiNet/Sunset markets

Construction evidence

  • The August 2025 VATI ledger showed the Mount Rogers project at 26,390 of 27,551 project passings with access and 1,652.5 fiber miles, and Cumberland Plateau at 5,113 of 5,659 with 350.93 fiber miles.
  • Point's Virginia retail base includes acquired operations: the company funded the 2018 purchase of Bristol Virginia Utilities OptiNet and Sunset Digital's Duffield-area operations, then rebranded the retail operations as Point in 2019.

Reported scale and date

  • More than 500,000 homes and businesses reached across 12 states at the 2026-05-04 Point-Clearwave close, companywide.
  • The announced Point-Clearwave-Visionary combination would reach approximately 700,000 homes and businesses across 17 states. This was a target perimeter for a signed transaction as of 2026-09-08, not a closed platform actual.

ZiTEL

Independent operator screen | P2: selective

Current ownership

Privately held Virginia limited liability company. Ultimate equity ownership and sponsor status were not identified in the reviewed primary public sources.

Commercial fit (inference)

Most actionable local alternative after All Points. ZiTEL has proven Bedford delivery and won a material replacement mandate, but the new scope creates execution and capital needs. RightFiber has better geographic logic through nearby Tennessee and Kentucky; Omni would be entering a noncontiguous state.

Possible transaction or partnership

Growth capital, construction financing, operating partnership or staged acquisition contingent on ownership diligence and West Piedmont milestones.

Constraints

ownership opacity; large newly assumed delivery obligation; limited public subscriber disclosure; county and grant dependencies

Countercase

The Bedford record is positive, but Franklin and Carroll were still at zero project passings in the August 2025 state ledger. The West Piedmont award increases relevance and execution risk at the same time.

Geographic clusters

  • Bedford
  • Franklin
  • Roanoke
  • Campbell
  • new West Piedmont scope in Franklin, Henry and Patrick

Construction evidence

  • The August 2025 VATI ledger showed ZiTEL's Bedford project at 5,593 of 5,644 project passings with access and 539.12 fiber miles, a 99% completion measure within that grant perimeter.
  • The same ledger showed zero of 2,543 Franklin and zero of 2,214 Carroll project passings with access at that cutoff, although both were then classified not at risk.
  • On 2026-08-05, the West Piedmont commission selected ZiTEL to replace RiverStreet and scheduled 14,161 connections across Franklin, Henry and Patrick counties by end-2027. This is a contracted target, not completed scale.

Reported scale and date

  • Company website claim of 750 fiber miles and more than 18,000 homes and businesses passed, accessed 2026-09-20; the page does not provide a measurement date.
  • 14,161 West Piedmont connections by end-2027 is a government-announced project target, not actual passings or subscribers.
