123NET

Same-sponsor portfolio | P1: investigate

Current ownership

Grain-controlled. FCC approval described a 65% interest held by a Grain fund vehicle and 35% retained by carryover owners at the July 2024 close. It is a sister platform to Grain-backed Rightfiber, not a third-party target.

Commercial fit (inference)

[I] Highest strategic relevance for Rightfiber because shared Grain control removes external seller risk and 123NET supplies the Michigan enterprise, data-center and middle-mile anchor that Rightfiber lacks. It can also be Omni's wholesale/backhaul counterparty, but Omni is Oak Hill-backed and remains external.

Possible transaction or partnership

Rightfiber: internal portfolio integration, commercial coordination, or eventual legal combination subject to fund, minority-owner, debt and regulatory constraints. Omni: wholesale transport, interconnection and selective open-access cooperation rather than ownership overlap.

Constraints

Minority rollover ownership remains material.; Enterprise, data-center, middle-mile and residential assets must be separated in valuation.; IMPACC and county grant obligations constrain use and open-access terms.; Route-mile figures changed from 3,100 at the 2024 transaction to nearly 5,000 in May 2026; reconcile definitions and completed additions.

Countercase

An immediate 123NET/Rightfiber legal merger may create limited value if fund, systems, capital structures or minority rights make commercial coordination cheaper. It is not evidence of an arm's-length Michigan acquisition opportunity.

Geographic clusters

  • Detroit and southeast Michigan enterprise metro
  • I-75 corridor from Southfield through Genesee and Saginaw to Bay City
  • Grand Rapids/Kent
  • Allegan County FTTH
  • Ottawa County FTTH under construction
  • IMPACC middle-mile routes through southwest and eastern Michigan

Construction evidence

  • Completed about 1,100 fiber miles in Allegan County by 2026-08-19, with about 17,000 addresses able to be served.
  • Ottawa County build was 25% complete on 2026-08-19, targeting 325 miles and more than 5,000 residents by year-end 2026.
  • Completed a 130-mile Southfield-to-Bay City middle-mile route with PFN in February 2025.

Reported scale and date

  • Nearly 5,000 route miles of long-haul and metro fiber as of 2026-05-22, company-reported.
  • Allegan: about 1,100 route miles and about 17,000 serviceable addresses as of 2026-08-19.
  • Michigan BEAD final-proposal allocation: 19,332 locations as of the 2025 final proposal; not constructed passings.

D & P Communications

Independent operator screen | P2: selective

Current ownership

Locally operated private company. Current beneficial ownership and cap table were not disclosed in the primary sources reviewed; do not label sponsor-free as confirmed.

Commercial fit (inference)

[I] Best independent adjacency for Omni. D&P sits inside and immediately west of Omni's Monroe County build and could reduce duplicate construction while extending Omni toward Adrian and Tecumseh. It is a smaller southeast Michigan entry option for Rightfiber/123NET.

Possible transaction or partnership

Start with an ownership and asset-perimeter review, then test tuck-in acquisition, reciprocal transport, or market partition/co-build. Confirm whether the fiber, ILEC, CLEC, cable and TC3 assets share ownership and debt.

Constraints

No current passings or subscriber disclosure.; The 550-mile figure is network mileage, not FTTH passings.; Mixed fiber and fixed-wireless service means a service-area map is not a pure FTTH footprint.; No verified 2025-2026 project-scale announcement was found.

Countercase

The business may be too small, locally held or operationally mixed to justify integration, and Omni may prefer organic infill where it has already mobilized crews.

Geographic clusters

  • Lenawee County
  • western Monroe County
  • Adrian
  • Tecumseh
  • Blissfield
  • Dundee
  • Petersburg
  • Milan area

Construction evidence

  • Company states it has built an extensive fiber network across Lenawee and western Monroe since the 1990s.
  • Current consumer pages advertise 2 Gig fiber and up to 4 Gig in named communities, while a separate rural internet product uses fixed wireless; address-level diligence is required.

Reported scale and date

  • More than 550 fiber miles installed, company-reported and undated as of 2026-09-20.
  • Passings, subscribers, revenue, EBITDA and 2025-2026 construction miles are unknown.

Peninsula Fiber Network

Independent operator screen | P2: selective

Current ownership

Private network-infrastructure company founded in 2006 through a collaboration between Baraga Telephone Company and Hiawatha Communications. Current equity percentages were not disclosed in the reviewed sources.

Commercial fit (inference)

[I] Highest-value neutral infrastructure partner for both platforms. PFN can supply route diversity, rural handoff and Chicago connectivity without either buyer duplicating long-haul construction. Its existing 123NET relationship makes it especially actionable for Rightfiber.

Possible transaction or partnership

Wholesale, IRU, dark-fiber, open-access, landing-station or joint-build agreement. Treat a control transaction as a separate and lower-probability question because PFN's utility is middle-mile and mission-critical infrastructure, not retail subscriber consolidation.

Constraints

Not a demonstrated mass-market FTTH platform.; Grant-funded routes carry open-access and nondiscrimination obligations.; Current ownership split and change-of-control rights are unknown.; The IMPACC performance report is evidence of an active award, not completion of all 535 miles.

Countercase

A buyer seeking immediate FTTH customers may get little near-term revenue synergy from PFN relative to a retail tuck-in, while assuming construction and grant-compliance execution risk.

Geographic clusters

  • Upper Peninsula and northern Lower Peninsula
  • Southfield-to-Bay City/I-75 corridor
  • Southfield-to-Lansing corridor
  • Byron Center-to-Benton Harbor/St. Joseph
  • Gulliver-Beaver Island-Charlevoix-Gaylord
  • Port Huron-to-Flint

Construction evidence

  • IMPACC is active and contemplates about 535 new middle-mile route miles, including two Lake Michigan crossings and terrestrial routes.
  • PFN and 123NET completed an approximately 130-mile Southfield-to-Bay City route in February 2025.
  • MPSC reports 2026 permits in Bellaire, Ellsworth and Delta Township, which evidence activity but not retail service.

Reported scale and date

  • More than 6,400 route miles across the Great Lakes region, company-reported as of 2026 access.
  • IMPACC: $61.257 million federal award, $87.510 million total project cost, about 535 planned route miles over a five-year performance period.
  • Statewide NG911 transport reaches all 83 Michigan counties; this is not a retail FTTH count.

Surf Internet

Scaled strategic platform | P2: selective

Current ownership

Private sponsor-backed platform. Macquarie Capital led a January 2026 investment alongside Bain Capital and Future Standard. Public materials do not disclose control percentages.

Commercial fit (inference)

Commercial partner or large strategic combination, not a conventional bolt-on.

Possible transaction or partnership

Transport, dark-fiber, off-net last mile or enterprise channel partnership. Consider a future sponsor-led combination only after route and passing overlap is measured.

Constraints

Substantial sponsor capital, secured ABS structure, three-state build plan, likely high valuation and potentially significant but unquantified duplicate markets.

Countercase

Surf is funded to keep building and is positioned as a scaled super-regional platform. It is more likely to compete with or partner with Rightfiber than sell as a small add-on.

Geographic clusters

  • northern and central Indiana
  • northern Illinois
  • Michigan
  • Livingston and Ingham counties
  • Fowlerville and rural Livingston townships
  • Hartford/South Haven in Van Buren County
  • Sparta/Kent County
  • Emmet and Charlevoix counties

Construction evidence

  • Surf reported service available to more than 300,000 homes and businesses across the three-state region on May 26, 2026. Its January plan targeted more than 330,000 total passings during 2026; the target is not an actual. It closed a $407 million ABS on March 4, 2026 to support continued expansion.
  • Livingston ROBIN build was 50% complete as of 2025-08-05 and was expected to reach about 4,500 unserved homes by mid-2026; separate Fowlerville build targeted about 960 locations.
  • An $11.4 million privately funded northern Michigan project announced 2025-10-21 targeted 8,965 homes and businesses in Charlevoix, Petoskey, Boyne City, East Jordan and Harbor Springs.
  • Hartford construction targeted nearly 1,000 locations by December 2025.

Reported scale and date

  • More than 300,000 serviceable homes and businesses as of May 26, 2026, across Indiana, Illinois and Michigan; more than 330,000 passings is a 2026 target. More than 150 school systems served. These are not subscriber counts.
  • More than 200,000 serviceable fiber passings across three states as of 2025-05-13, actual company milestone.
  • Target to surpass 330,000 total passings in 2026, announced 2026-01-28; target, not actual.
  • $407 million ABS closed 2026-03-04 through a wholly owned financing subsidiary.

Bloomingdale Communications

Independent operator screen | P2: selective

Current ownership

Company describes itself as community-owned and operated. Public records use both Bloomingdale Telephone Company, Inc. dba Bloomingdale Communications and Southwest Michigan Communications, Inc. for network awards and permits; the exact parent-affiliate chain is unresolved and should be confirmed before treating them as one transaction perimeter.

Commercial fit (inference)

[I] Best small southwest Michigan tuck-in or operating partnership for a 123NET/Rightfiber strategy because it sits near 123NET's Allegan network and the IMPACC Benton Harbor corridor. It offers Omni a greenfield entry but is less contiguous with Omni's southeast cluster.

Possible transaction or partnership

Confirm legal entities and cooperative/community governance, then test local combination, management partnership, shared backhaul or grant-completion financing. A 123NET interconnect is the clearest first commercial route.

Constraints

Community ownership and RUS debt may restrict change of control.; No current passings, subscribers, revenue or EBITDA were found.; Historical USDA funded households are not current serviceable addresses.; The $7.5 million Thornapple figure comes from the Barry County Chamber/Economic Development Alliance release, not audited company disclosure.

Countercase

Small scale, public-funding obligations and community control may make a sale uneconomic. MEC and Surf also operate or build in adjacent southwest Michigan markets.

Geographic clusters

  • Van Buren County
  • Allegan County
  • Barry County/Thornapple Township

Construction evidence

  • Current company site describes a Van Buren and Allegan footprint and expansion into Barry County.
  • A Thornapple Township FTTH project broke ground in June 2026; a local economic-development release describes about $7.5 million for roughly 380 addresses.
  • USDA's 2020 ReConnect award provided $10.720 million of grant and loan funding to Southwest Michigan Communications for FTTP to 2,864 households in Van Buren and Allegan counties.

Reported scale and date

  • Thornapple: roughly 380 planned addresses and about $7.5 million project value as announced 2026-07-20; under construction, not completed.
  • 2019 ReConnect perimeter: 2,864 funded households in Van Buren and Allegan; historical funded households, not current passings.
  • Michigan BEAD final-proposal data assigns 19 locations to Southwest Michigan Communications; award obligation, not current retail scale.

Midwest Energy & Communications

Cooperative / municipal partner | P2: selective

Current ownership

Member-owned cooperative. Members voted in 2026 to admit fiber customers as cooperative members effective July 2026, reinforcing cooperative rather than sponsor ownership.

Commercial fit (inference)

[I] Strong operating partner for both platforms and especially Omni because MEC's Lenawee, Monroe and Washtenaw construction touches Omni's southeast Michigan markets. For Rightfiber, MEC can connect 123NET's southwest and southeast corridors without a control deal.

Possible transaction or partnership

Wholesale/backhaul, roaming-style operational support, reciprocal construction, or grant-area subcontracting. A control acquisition is lower probability and would require member, lender and regulatory analysis.

Constraints

Cooperative governance materially limits conventional M&A.; The 84,000 figure is cumulative addresses offered service, not subscribers.; ROBIN and BEAD locations are funded obligations, not actual passings.; Multi-utility operations require separation of fiber economics from electric and propane businesses.

Countercase

MEC may have little reason to sell or cede customer control, and direct FTTH overlap could make collaboration narrower than the geographic fit suggests.

Geographic clusters

  • Berrien
  • Cass
  • St. Joseph
  • Van Buren
  • Kalamazoo
  • Lenawee
  • Monroe
  • Washtenaw
  • Jackson
  • Ingham

Construction evidence

  • Started more than 300 route miles of construction in early 2026 for more than 3,000 rural addresses near Benton Harbor, Britton, Carleton, Coloma, Constantine, Covert, Hartford, Maybee, Milan and Schoolcraft.
  • Completed construction to more than 7,600 rural addresses across nine southern Michigan counties in the second half of 2025.
  • ROBIN award covers 15,846 locations in Berrien, Cass, St. Joseph and Van Buren counties.

Reported scale and date

  • More than 84,000 residences and businesses offered a fiber option since rollout began in 2015, company-reported as of 2026-02-12.
  • FY2025 fiber-service revenue: $32.776 million, audited cooperative financial statements.
  • Michigan BEAD final-proposal allocation: 10,617 locations; award obligation, not constructed passings.

Cherry Capital Connection / CCCFiber

Independent operator screen | P2: selective

Current ownership

Owner-managed private company at the 2019 MPSC application, which named Timothy Maylone as managing member and CEO. No current sponsor or later control transaction was found; current cap table remains unverified.

Commercial fit (inference)

[I] Better independent northern Michigan name than another sponsor-backed platform. It could give Rightfiber/123NET or Omni a local construction team, grant book and northern entry at smaller scale than Surf, but only if fiber assets can be separated from legacy fixed wireless.

Possible transaction or partnership

Founder/owner-led tuck-in, minority growth investment, or program-management partnership. Make the first diligence item an address-level split of FTTH, fixed wireless, funded-but-unbuilt locations and owned transport.

Constraints

Hybrid wireless/fiber perimeter.; No current subscriber, revenue, EBITDA, fiber-mile or completed-passings disclosure.; 2021 square-mile claims are stale and unsuitable for valuation.; ROBIN, RDOF and BEAD figures overlap in time and may not be additive.

Countercase

Execution risk and a small operating base may outweigh the value of grant awards. Surf and Truestream have larger northern Michigan systems, while PFN can provide transport without acquiring a hybrid ISP.

Geographic clusters

  • Benzie County
  • Grand Traverse and Leelanau area
  • northern Lower Peninsula north of M-55
  • selected Upper Peninsula activity including Rudyard Township

Construction evidence

  • Company states it won 2,791 RDOF locations across 25 census block groups in eight counties and planned $8.25 million of infrastructure investment over six years.
  • Michigan ROBIN awarded 1,428 Benzie County locations with a $2.858 million grant.
  • MPSC permit records show activity in Lake Ann, Crystal Lake Township, Leelanau Township, Forest Township and Rudyard Township; permits do not prove service.

Reported scale and date

  • Company-reported hybrid footprint in 2021: 11 counties, about 2,900 square miles of fixed wireless territory and about 2,500 square miles described as FTTH coverage; square miles are not passings.
  • RDOF: 2,791 awarded locations and $8.25 million planned investment, announced 2021.
  • Michigan BEAD final-proposal allocation: 3,915 locations; award obligation, not current availability.
