Omni / Lit / Citizens capital and ownership perimeter

As of 2026-09-20

Conclusion

The clean financing conclusion is narrower than the headline dollars suggest. Oak Hill made two separate pre-merger sponsor commitments: $250 million to Omni in July 2022 and $150 million of primary capital to Lit in May 2023. Omni later closed two distinct $150 million term-debt rounds, in July 2024 and December 2025. Those debt rounds support a $300 million announced facility-principal subtotal because the second was explicitly incremental and described as following the first. That subtotal is not cash funded or current debt outstanding. A March 31, 2026 SEC fund filing shows an unfunded portion within one OHA fund's slice of the 2025 incremental delayed-draw loan. The separate $10 million working-capital facility may also be undrawn.

Do not add the $250 million Omni commitment, $150 million Lit commitment, $300 million debt subtotal, $50 million incremental 2025 Oak Hill equity, $10 million working-capital facility, and more than $500 million cumulative invested. They mix sponsor commitments, equity, facility principal, potentially undrawn capacity, and deployed construction capital. Their company perimeters also change after the Lit merger and the proposed Citizens transfer.

Normalized chronology

  • July 22, 2022: Oak Hill formed Omni as majority investor and committed $250 million from Fund VI for new construction in Ohio, Pennsylvania, and Michigan. This was a commitment, not an actual-invested disclosure. S01

  • May 4, 2023: Oak Hill committed $150 million of primary capital to Lit and became majority investor. Stephens, TPO, and Lit management remained shareholders. Lit reported networks reaching approximately 20,000 homes and businesses and targeted more than 200,000 locations over the next few years. The first figure was actual company-reported reach; the second was a target. S02

  • July 16, 2024: Omni closed $150 million of debt from Stonepeak Credit. Omni reported more than $250 million invested, over 25 communities serving customers, over 15 under construction, and a target of approximately 200,000 locations by year-end 2024. S03

  • November 20, 2024: Omni and Lit announced a merger. Both were majority-owned by Oak Hill, while Stephens and TPO remained shareholders. The release promised completion of network and systems integration in early 2025 so Lit customers could receive speeds up to 10 Gbps. It did not disclose exchange ratios, consideration, a legal close date, ownership percentages, assumed debt, or the complete Lit Communities contract perimeter. S04

  • December 31, 2024 measurement: Stonepeak's 2025 mid-year credit presentation reported 110,000 homes passed in less than two years. The presentation states figures are as of December 31, 2024 unless otherwise noted. This is material counterevidence to Omni's 200,000-location target, but it is not a clean 90,000-location miss. “Homes passed” and “locations” may differ, and the Stonepeak slide describes Ohio, Michigan, and Pennsylvania without establishing whether Lit or Texas was included. S05

  • May to September 2025: Brownsville officially launched Omni-branded service on May 27. Brownsville's FY2025 financial report later stated that Lit had merged into Omni, Omni was the surviving legal and operating entity, and the city's counterparty name changed without changing contractual obligations. This proves meaningful legal and customer-facing integration by September 30, 2025. It does not prove that every network, billing, OSS/BSS, product, or customer migration was completed in early 2025. [S06, S07]

  • December 1, 2025: Omni closed $150 million of incremental debt from Stonepeak and OHA, $50 million of incremental Oak Hill equity, and a separate $10 million Republic Bank working-capital facility. It reported more than $400 million invested, approximately 60 communities serving customers, approximately 25 communities under construction, and a target of approximately 340,000 locations by year-end 2025. No actual location count was provided. S08

  • March 31, 2026: An SEC-filed OHA fund report showed Omni first-lien loans and a 2025 incremental delayed-draw commitment with an unfunded portion. This confirms that “closed $150 million debt financing” should not automatically be read as fully funded cash. The filing covers one fund's holdings, not Omni's total debt. S09

  • June 1 to July 11, 2026: Omni announced the Citizens acquisition and said Citizens served Westmoreland County, including Latrobe, Greensburg, Mt. Pleasant, Acme, Pleasant Unity, Mammoth, Youngstown, Norvelt, and surrounding areas. Omni also reported more than $500 million invested in fiber construction and a plan to add nearly 200,000 locations across six western Pennsylvania counties. That plan is not Citizens' acquired passings. On July 11, Pennsylvania published a notice that Omni and three Citizens legal entities were seeking transfer-of-control approval, with protests due July 27. No later final order or consummation notice was located. The status should remain announced and transitioning, with regulatory close unresolved. [S10, S11]

  • September 20, 2026: Citizens still operates a standalone consumer site, support channel, and bill-pay path. Omni's current service-area page separately labels western Pennsylvania markets as available, partially available, or coming soon. These pages show operating transition, not legal close or acquired scale. [S12, S13, S14]

Ownership and perimeter

Omni is the surviving platform publicly associated with Oak Hill. Post-merger ownership percentages are unknown. Lit's operating brand and Brownsville counterparty moved under Omni, but no public schedule proves that every Lit Communities planning, advisory, or P3 contract transferred. Citizens' regulatory application names Citizens Telephone Company of Kecksburg, Citizens Telecom Solutions, LLC, and Citizens Telecommunication Technologies, Inc. That legal perimeter is more precise than the Citizens Fiber brand, but ownership should not be called finally transferred without the PUC disposition.

Actuals still required

The unresolved diligence list is decisive: actual year-end 2024 and 2025 passings or ready-for-service locations on a consistent perimeter; subscribers and take rate; revenue, EBITDA, and free cash flow; amount funded and deployed under both sponsor commitments; debt drawn, repaid, and outstanding by facility; Lit merger consideration and contract schedule; systems-integration completion; Citizens final approval and consummation; and Citizens purchase price, passings, subscribers, route miles, and financial contribution.

Until those data exist, more than $500 million invested establishes scale of deployment, not capital efficiency or target achievement.
