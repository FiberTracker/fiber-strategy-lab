Omni/Lit MDU economics and wireless-carrier pitch framework

As of: 2026-09-20

Decision

Build the MDU channel, but underwrite two separate businesses: property broadband and a wireless overlay. The broadband case should clear the investment threshold on retail, bulk or hybrid economics. Assign zero revenue and zero reimbursement to wireless until a carrier signs a site order or binding commitment.

Omni publicly markets a community solution to property owners and residents, but discloses no signed MDU properties, units, penetration, revenue share, capex, revenue or EBITDA [P]S1. Omni announced the Lit merger on November 20, 2024; the release contemplated early-2025 integration but did not state a legal closing date [P]S2. Later Omni materials treat Lit as part of the platform, but the underwrite should confirm contract succession and avoid counting Lit twice.

Omni's December 1, 2025 financing release said it had invested more than $400 million and was on track to reach about 340,000 locations by year-end [P]S4. This target is company-wide, not MDU units, actual locations, subscribers or earnings.

Contract structures

  1. Retail access: Omni bills residents. The property supplies access, risers, telecom rooms, marketing coordination and unit-entry rules. Omni bears penetration, churn, bad-debt and installation-conversion risk.
  2. Bulk: The property or HOA pays a monthly rate for contract-defined units. This can create a revenue floor, but the denominator must follow the billing clause, including vacancies, outages and added units. It is not automatically every contracted unit.
  3. Hybrid: The property buys a base service or common-area network, and residents buy upgrades. Model the two revenue streams and their costs separately.
  4. Wireless overlay: A carrier, neutral-host customer or property pays for backhaul or in-building coverage. Keep this outside the base MDU case until the carrier order, RF design and capital responsibilities are signed.

AT&T's current multifamily offer validates the menu: bulk, access and marketing agreements, pre-installed gateways and property-wide Wi-Fi [P]S5. It does not validate Omni pricing or margins. Optimum's 2025 10-K provides a useful counting convention: each subscribing apartment unit in a bulk residential arrangement is one customer, while the master account is not; passings are units serviceable without extending transmission lines [P]S13. Its company-wide penetration is not an Omni MDU benchmark.

Metric perimeter

The following six counts are analytical definitions, not disclosed Omni metrics. Date each at the property level:

  • Contracted properties: effective signed agreements, excluding proposals and LOIs.
  • Contracted units, Uc: dwelling units in the agreement perimeter.
  • Accessible units, Ua: units with secured entry, pathway, riser and installation rights.
  • Ready-for-service units, Ur: accessible units serviceable through a standard installation without material additional distribution construction.
  • Bulk-billed units, Ub: units the master customer must pay for during the period under the contract's vacancy and outage rules.
  • Retail subscribers, S: active, directly billed unit accounts. Exclude pre-installed equipment and the master account.

Retail penetration is p = S / Ur. If occupancy-adjusted penetration is useful, report it separately as S / occupied Ur; never silently change the denominator. FCC BDC availability is not a subscriber count. FCC guidance includes potential customers that can receive a standard installation, excludes buildings where owner restrictions block access, and can assign reporting to the facilities-based provider even when a landlord pays [P]S12.

Underwrite

Contract inputs are the owner entity, address, state, unit schedule, dates, renewal and termination rights, physical access and equipment rights, existing providers, wiring ownership and marketing clauses.

Commercial inputs are retail plan mix, net ARPU, discounts, credits, installation revenue, churn and bad debt; bulk MRC and billable-unit definition; escalators and service levels; fixed property payments or flat shares; upgrades; property-service fees; and executed carrier terms.

Capex inputs are offsite feeder, entrance, make-ready, riser, lateral, unit drop, ONT, gateway, common-area Wi-Fi, design, permits, fire-stopping, remediation, revisits and contingency. For wireless, add radios, antennas, cabling, power, cooling, backhaul and integration. Every line needs a payor, owner, timing, useful life, maintenance duty and removal obligation.

Core formulas:

S_t = Ur_t * p_t
Retail revenue_t = 12 * S_t * net ARPU_t
Bulk revenue_t = 12 * Ub_t * bulk MRC_t
Upgrade revenue_t = 12 * upgrade subscribers_t * upgrade net ARPU_t
Property payment_t = fixed payment_t + flat rate_t * eligible retail revenue_t
Wireless recurring_t = 12 * contracted circuits_t * carrier MRC_t
Net capex_0 = gross capex_0 - cash property contribution_0 - cash carrier contribution_0
Contribution_t = all contracted revenue - variable COGS - property payment - property opex
UFCF_t = contribution_t - maintenance capex_t - change in NWC_t - cash taxes_t
NPV = -net capex_0 + sum(UFCF_t / (1+r)^t) + residual value_n / (1+r)^n

Deduct only contributions received or contractually due. Do not net speculative carrier funding. For break-even penetration, annualize net capex with CRF(r,n) = r*(1+r)^n / ((1+r)^n-1), then calculate:

p_be = (fixed opex + net capex * CRF - bulk contribution - contracted wireless contribution)
       / (12 * Ur * retail contribution per subscriber month)

Show capex per Uc, Ua, Ur and activation; contribution by stream; UFCF, NPV, IRR and payback; and input-driven sensitivities. Do not pre-populate Omni economics.

Wireless-carrier pitch

Pitch a geocoded, diligence-ready portfolio, not a headline unit count. For each building show signed rights, ready fiber routes, telecom space, power, measured indoor coverage gaps, diversity, demarcation and launch timing. Offer distinct products: lit or dark-fiber backhaul, small-cell transport, carrier-specific in-building coverage, or multi-carrier neutral host.

Carrier capex is not automatic. T-Mobile's recommended BYOC 2.0 says the customer owns and manages the solution and funds 5G-ready radios and backhaul. Its BYOC 1.0 says T-Mobile supplies radios and backhaul but can take 6 to 18 months [P]S8. Verizon says it uses small cells, in-building solutions and DAS to densify its network and acquired Starry in January 2026 to strengthen MDU broadband capabilities [P]S7. This shows strategic interest, not an Omni contract.

Omni Mobile launched with Reach in August 2025. Reach supplies the platform while Omni controls the brand and customer relationship [P]S3. This supports a convergence and retention story, but it is not evidence that a host carrier will fund an indoor system, buy backhaul or share retail revenue.

The carrier ask should be a portfolio MSA plus property-specific orders with minimum MRC, construction reimbursement, RF approval deadlines, SLA, equipment ownership and termination protection. Until an order is signed, all related revenue and reimbursement remain zero.

FCC diligence constraints, not legal advice

For covered common carriers, 47 CFR 64.2500 prohibits contracts that restrict an MTE owner's right to admit another carrier, graduated owner compensation, and an exclusive right to compensate the owner for access [P]S9. Exclusive marketing is not the same as exclusive access, but written tenant marketing must clearly disclose that alternatives may be available [P]S9. FCC 22-12 also clarified that cable inside-wiring sale-and-leaseback structures cannot block alternative providers [P]S10.

The FCC did not prohibit bulk billing in the 2022 order. On January 24, 2025, the Chairman ended consideration of the later bulk-billing proposal [P]S11. The 2022 order targeted covered telecommunications carriers and covered MVPDs and did not then extend every requirement to broadband-only providers [P]S10. Do not assume Omni is outside or inside a rule based on the brand. Counsel should classify the contracting entity and services and review state and local access, landlord-tenant billing, consumer-protection, tax and permitting law.

A flat, non-graduated, non-exclusive property payment may be structurally different from the arrangements the FCC banned, but it still requires legal review. Do not offer penetration tiers or prevent the owner from accepting consideration from competitors.

Pros and countercase

The case works when density lowers external plant per Ur, rights are secure, activation is simple, a creditworthy bulk payer creates a floor, and carrier orders add contracted use of the same fiber. AT&T identifies subscriber counts, revenue per user, capital and acquisition cost per subscriber, and ongoing operating costs as core valuation inputs [P]S6.

The countercase is decisive: contracts may cover inaccessible units; risers, unit entry, fire-stopping, environmental remediation, power and repeat visits can absorb the density benefit; competitive access can pressure retail penetration; bulk exposes the operator to property credit, vacancy, renewal and resident-value risk; and carriers may require owner-funded radios and backhaul. A property or carrier pipeline is not underwritten value until perimeter, serviceability, pricing, capex allocation and enforceable payment are documented.
