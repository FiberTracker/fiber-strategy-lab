Pelican Broadband / Northeast Telephone Services / CP-TEL

Independent operator screen | P1: investigate

Current ownership

Independent, locally and family owned. NortheastTel acquired CP-TEL in 2024 and rebranded the combined operations as Pelican Broadband.

Commercial fit (inference)

Best Louisiana bolt-on. Its north Louisiana cluster is adjacent to Rightfiber's Ruston entry and overlaps the same rural operating model. The legal-entity perimeter and subscriber count are small enough for a true tuck-in, while the current grant provides funded expansion.

Possible transaction or partnership

Approach as a whole-company acquisition or majority recapitalization with family rollover. Preserve local brand and management during BEAD construction. First diligence item is a location-level overlap and interconnection study around Ruston, Natchitoches, and Monroe.

Constraints

BEAD grant and ETC/change-of-control consents; two legacy operating perimeters; company page lists Ruston business services, so some overlap may already exist; exact FTTP BSLs and grant match obligations are not yet reconciled.

Countercase

The 4,000 figure is subscribers across residential and business products, not FTTH passings. Parts of the footprint may be legacy voice, DSL, managed IT, or enterprise services. A small subscriber base can still carry material regulated and grant compliance complexity.

Geographic clusters

  • Morehouse
  • Ouachita
  • Lincoln/Ruston business services
  • Natchitoches
  • Sabine
  • DeSoto
  • Red River
  • Caldwell
  • Richland
  • Rapides

Construction evidence

  • Current company pages report FTTH service and a signed GUMBO 2.0 program covering Coushatta, Hagewood, and Provencal. The FCC D25 catalog includes Northeast Louisiana Telephone Co. provider 130939 and CP-Tel Holdings provider 130362 with technology code 50.

Reported scale and date

  • More than 4,000 residential and business subscribers, current company disclosure accessed 2026-09-20. A $5,363,585 GUMBO 2.0 award covers 1,232 locations and a stated plan to reach about 1,300 residents and businesses during 2026-2028. Do not add residents/businesses to locations.

Cajun Broadband

Independent operator screen | P1: investigate

Current ownership

Independent, locally owned and founder-led by Chris Disher and Jimmy Lewis; no sponsor ownership was identified in reviewed primary sources.

Commercial fit (inference)

Second-best target and the better southern Louisiana operating beachhead. Founder-led execution and a meaningful customer base create a credible tuck-in, but the geography is less contiguous with Ruston than Pelican.

Possible transaction or partnership

Whole-company acquisition or founder recapitalization with milestone-based consideration tied to current grant construction, acceptance, and reimbursement. Keep the local construction and customer-service team.

Constraints

Current BEAD obligations, coastal construction and storm-hardening exposure, possible FWA-to-fiber mix, and limited public financial disclosure. Grant award amounts and build locations changed during the 2025 federal reset and require final schedule review.

Countercase

South Louisiana distance from Rightfiber's current Ruston cluster reduces immediate network synergy. The company may prefer to compound independently using grant capital rather than sell after winning funded expansion.

Geographic clusters

  • Acadiana
  • St. Martin
  • Iberia
  • Vermilion
  • St. Mary
  • Assumption
  • Jefferson Davis
  • Plaquemines planned build

Construction evidence

  • The company reports completing Louisiana's first GUMBO project, 90,000 feet of fiber to more than 500 previously underserved homes and businesses in St. Martin Parish. ConnectLA listed Cajun among providers with fully executed agreements and initial BEAD disbursement on 2026-02-16. FCC D25 lists Cajun Broadband provider 360013 with technology 50 and 70.

Reported scale and date

  • Nearly 10,000 customers across seven Louisiana parishes, current company disclosure accessed 2026-09-20. The company announced a $26.7 million preliminary GUMBO 2.0 grant for 6,239 homes and businesses on 2024-11-19; the exact post-reset award perimeter was not re-reconciled to the 2026 executed agreement.

PhireLink, including Arkwest

Scaled strategic platform | P2: selective

Current ownership

Management-controlled PhireLink platform. Arkwest is an acquired operating brand, not an independent target. Ultimate controllers identified in FCC filings include Glen Post III, James Davidson and Benjamin Friedman.

Commercial fit (inference)

Arkwest adds a central and western Arkansas operating base near Rightfiber counties. Physical interconnection and incremental locations remain unmeasured. A platform-level partnership is more plausible than treating the recently acquired brand as a free-standing seller.

Possible transaction or partnership

Owner-to-owner interconnection, transport, wholesale, referral, or joint-build discussion. Treat M&A only as a future PhireLink-level combination.

Constraints

Recent control change; PhireLink growth plan; E-ACAM/ETC duties; duplicate integration; ILEC approvals; likely premium.

Countercase

A near-term resale conflicts with PhireLink's stated Arkansas expansion strategy; both owners may target the same River Valley corridor.

Geographic clusters

  • Yell 5,210; Perry 340; Scott 131 D25 FTTP availability locations.
  • St. Tammany and Slidell, Louisiana
  • Yell, Scott, and Perry Counties, Arkansas via Arkwest
  • southeastern Colorado
  • western Kansas

Construction evidence

  • Deal materials describe a 100% FTTP, third-generation GPON network exceeding 900 route miles, built from internal cash flow; current pages say the entire network is fiber. No post-close Arkansas build count was located.
  • FCC records identify Camellia City Fiber and Tammany Wireless as Louisiana affiliates and granted the Arkwest transfer on 2025-07-03. PhireLink announced Arkwest's more than 900-route-mile, 100% FTTP network on 2025-04-29. FCC D25 lists Camellia City Fiber provider 450023 with technology 50.

Reported scale and date

  • 5,681 unique FCC FTTP availability locations, not passings or subscribers (D25 2025-12-31; revised 2026-09-15)
  • More than 900 route miles applies only to the announced Arkwest Arkansas perimeter as of 2025-04-29. Louisiana route miles, passings, and subscribers remain undisclosed. PhireLink's 2025-01-06 BEAD announcement disclosed no dollar amount or location count.

REV

Independent operator screen | P2: selective

Current ownership

Independent, privately held, locally owned Louisiana platform formed from Reserve Telephone, EATEL, and Vision Communications. No financial sponsor control was found in reviewed primary sources.

Commercial fit (inference)

A scaled merger candidate or Louisiana partnership, not a bolt-on. A combination would create immediate state relevance and operating density but would be a major corporate transaction relative to Rightfiber's single disclosed Louisiana entry at Ruston.

Possible transaction or partnership

Begin with a strategic combination or statewide wholesale and grant-construction partnership. Preserve local ownership identity through rollover equity and local operating governance if M&A is pursued.

Constraints

Large size, local identity, active BEAD obligations, possible overlap, and an existing consortium relationship with Macquarie-backed Swyft and T-Mobile. Consortium commercial terms are undisclosed.

Countercase

REV has demonstrated its own acquisition capability and may see Rightfiber as a competitor. The 5,000 miles and customer figures are company-reported and not a transaction perimeter or valuation basis.

Geographic clusters

  • Baton Rouge and Ascension
  • Lafourche and Thibodaux
  • St. John the Baptist and River Parishes
  • Livingston
  • Grand Isle
  • Southeastern MDU portfolio

Construction evidence

  • REV joined Swyft and T-Mobile in the Louisiana Local Fiber Consortium in August 2024 and was included in Louisiana's executed BEAD provider list by 2026-02-16. FCC D25 includes EATEL provider 130432 and Reserve Holdings provider 131103 with technology 50 among their technology sets.

Reported scale and date

  • REV disclosed more than 5,000 fiber miles and more than 63,000 customers on 2024-08-01. Its current service-area page, accessed 2026-09-20, states more than 68,000 customers. Customer counts are not passings and include homes and workplaces.

Volt Broadband

Cooperative / municipal partner | P2: selective

Current ownership

Wholly owned subsidiary of Northeast Louisiana Power Cooperative, a member-owned electric cooperative.

Commercial fit (inference)

Best wholesale and operating partner because it is close to Ruston and uses a rural FTTH model, but a weak acquisition target because the broadband company is tied to cooperative ownership and electric infrastructure.

Possible transaction or partnership

Pursue IRUs, backhaul, middle-mile interconnection, carrier services, shared NOC/procurement, or an operating-services arrangement. Any equity route would need cooperative governance and member-benefit support.

Constraints

Cooperative mission and governance; shared pole and electric infrastructure; public-funding obligations; subscriber count not disclosed; sale may be legally or politically unattractive.

Countercase

A partnership can improve transport economics but may not transfer retail customers or control. Rightfiber should not assume the 7,000 connected locations equal active subscribers.

Geographic clusters

  • Franklin
  • Madison
  • Richland
  • Tensas
  • East Carroll
  • West Carroll
  • Morehouse

Construction evidence

  • The co-op selected Conexon in 2022 to design and manage an FTTH system using cooperative infrastructure. ConnectLA announced completion of a GUMBO-funded expansion on 2026-07-16. FCC D25 lists Volt Broadband provider 480104 with technology 50 only.

Reported scale and date

  • Nearly 7,000 connected locations in northeast Louisiana as of 2026-07-16. The original 2022 plan targeted up to 11,000 homes and businesses across a seven-parish co-op region. These figures are locations or target premises, not subscribers.

Swyft Fiber, including acquired Fastwyre LA / TX / AL assets

Committed or integrated platform | P3: partner / monitor

Current ownership

Sponsor platform controlled through CSM Holding by Macquarie-affiliated MIP VI Outlier. The FCC reported a 57.35% direct equity interest, 50% direct voting interest, and four of eight board seats as of 2026-02-11, with continuing Hunt Group minority ownership.

Commercial fit (inference)

Swyft and its acquired Fastwyre assets form an existing Macquarie-backed platform. Screen selective interconnection, wholesale or boundary exchanges; a new sponsor-level transaction is unproven.

Possible transaction or partnership

Engage at sponsor and operating-company level for interconnection, transport, construction coordination, or a future portfolio-level combination. Do not pursue the already acquired Fastwyre assets as a separate seller process.

Constraints

Macquarie sponsorship, current BEAD and RDOF obligations, ongoing Fastwyre integration, competitive Louisiana overlap, and an existing relationship with REV and T-Mobile.

Countercase

A broad strategic partnership may help grants and transport, but it could strengthen a direct competitor. No reviewed source disclosed the LLFC economics or any obligation for T-Mobile to buy capacity from Swyft or REV.

Geographic clusters

  • Rural Louisiana statewide grant markets
  • Arkansas
  • Mississippi
  • southwest Louisiana and southeast Texas Fastwyre markets
  • Moundville, Alabama
  • Cameron, Calcasieu, Beauregard, Allen, and Vernon areas in Louisiana
  • High Island, Gilchrist, and Nome in Texas
  • Moundville area in Alabama

Construction evidence

  • FCC approval in 2024 established the Macquarie relationship, and a 2026 FCC public notice confirmed CSM Holding remained majority owned and controlled by MIP VI Outlier. Swyft is a Louisiana BEAD agreement holder as of 2026-02-16 and a member of the Swyft/REV/T-Mobile consortium. It finalized the Fastwyre Louisiana/Texas/Alabama acquisition in July 2026. FCC D25 lists CableSouth provider 370020 with technology codes 40 and 50.
  • FCC records identify Cameron Communications, Cameron Telephone, Elizabeth Telephone, LBH, Moundville Telephone, and MTC in the transferred regulated perimeter. Fastwyre customer pages state that the Alabama, Louisiana, and Texas business was sold to Swyft.

Reported scale and date

  • FCC records on 2024-01-03 cited $152.9 million of RDOF support over ten years for 57,387 locations across Arkansas, Louisiana, and Mississippi. Those are subsidized deployment obligations, not passings, customers, or Louisiana-only scale. Acquired Fastwyre route miles and customers were not disclosed.
  • No transaction consideration, acquired route miles, passings, or customers were publicly disclosed in reviewed primary sources. FCC D25 lists American Broadband Holding Company provider 131226 with technology codes 10 and 50, but the catalog record count is not used as scale.
