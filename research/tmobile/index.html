T-Mobile challenge: Omni / Lit

As of: 2026-09-20

Citizens status as of September 20, 2026. Omni announced the transaction on June 1, 2026. A July 11 Pennsylvania transfer-of-control notice shows applications for approval; no final approval or consummation notice was located. Final legal closing remains unresolved in the reviewed public record.

Decision

T-Mobile is credible for a staged commercial partnership, but an immediate second Oak Hill whole-platform JV is not the base case. The best proposal is a selected-market wholesale/customer transfer or greenfield BuildCo, coupled with a priced option to contribute validated Omni cohorts to the pending Oak Hill JV after the GoNetspeed/Greenlight transaction closes and performs.

[P] T-Mobile's repeat fiber model is consistent: it owns eligible customer relationships and retail revenue, while the fiber company builds, installs, operates the network, and charges wholesale access. That model is live at Lumos and Metronet and is proposed for i3 and GoNetspeed/Greenlight. T-Mobile Q2 2026 10-Q

[Inference] Strategic fit is not the same as deal need. T-Mobile already has two closed JVs, two pending JVs, unnamed wholesale partners, and at least $3.2 billion of specifically disclosed future or closing fiber funding before Omni: $500 million for Lumos in 2027 to 2028, $700 million for i3 at closing, and $2.0 billion for GoNetspeed/Greenlight at closing. The last two amounts remain conditional on closing.

What Omni / Lit actually is

[P] Omni and Lit are not two current platforms. Omni and Lit announced their merger on 2024-11-20; that announcement did not state an exact legal close date. Later public materials treat Lit as integrated into Omni. Both were majority-owned by Oak Hill. Stephens Capital Partners and The Pritzker Organization remained shareholders at announcement, but current percentages and rights are unknown. Lit contributed its Medina County, Ohio network and Brownsville, Texas build. Omni/Lit merger

The company also closed $150 million of incremental debt, $50 million of Oak Hill equity, and a $10 million working-capital facility, following $150 million of Stonepeak debt announced in 2024. MDU and convergence test

[P] Omni publishes two blank right-of-entry forms with different duration terms. The July-path form contemplates perpetual access and express successor assignment. The September-path form contemplates a 20-year initial term followed by one-year renewals subject to notice. Neither blank form proves executed property rights or a universal current standard. Executed property agreements control, and the later URL does not prove that earlier executed agreements were replaced.

[P] Omni also markets a mobile product with nationwide 4G/5G coverage. That establishes convergence intent, not economics. The public page does not identify the host network, subscribers, attach rate, churn, margin, or change-of-control cost. Omni Mobile

[Inference] T-Mobile can plausibly improve customer acquisition and cross-sell. It has not publicly demonstrated an Omni-specific wireless-fiber attach case or an MDU premium. Assign no premium until executed ROEs, activated units, property payments, wiring ownership, install capital, take rate, churn, and assignability are produced.

Three feasible proposal structures

  1. Post-close Oak Hill JV drop-down option. Add an option to the pending GoNetspeed/Greenlight JV documents. Exercise only after that JV closes and completes an agreed operating period. T-Mobile buys 50% of specified Omni NetworkCo cohorts and eligible residential customers under a pre-agreed valuation formula. Debt, minority holders, business customers, and legacy products are resolved before contribution. This best matches T-Mobile's model but avoids a simultaneous second full Oak Hill transaction.

  2. Selected-market customer transfer plus wholesale MSA. T-Mobile buys eligible residential internet relationships only in exact-location non-overlap clusters. Omni keeps the network, construction, installation, and wholesale revenue. Business, TV, phone, and mobile are carved out or governed separately. Markets activate only after ready-for-service acceptance, with customer churn true-ups and a later equity call option.

  3. Greenfield and MDU BuildCo. Ring-fence new communities or executed MDU properties outside all T-Mobile partner availability. Omni contributes development and operations. T-Mobile funds agreed customer acquisition and an agreed share of network capital only at construction milestones, owns new residential customers, and pays wholesale access. A later 50/50 conversion requires passing, activation, service-quality, and return hurdles.

Kill tests

  • Incrementality: Exact FCC location intersections must isolate an economic perimeter outside Lumos, Metronet/Vexus, GoNetspeed/Greenlight, i3, and unnamed T-Mobile wholesale partners. State or county adjacency fails.
  • Customer transfer: Subscriber schedules must separate residential internet from business, phone, TV, mobile, grant, municipal, and MDU obligations. Material nonassignability or forced legacy products kills the customer-book trade.
  • Cohort economics: Actual ready-for-service locations, connections, ARPU, churn, install cost, network opex, and remaining capex must support returns comparable with T-Mobile's dated public references of about 20% year-one penetration and around 20% all-in JV IRR. Announced communities and location targets fail.
  • Capital and consents: Stonepeak Credit, Oak Hill Advisors, Republic Bank, Oak Hill fund entities, Stephens, Pritzker, Citizens, and Ohio Telecom rights must be mapped. Unfinanceable make-wholes, cross-defaults, or vetoes kill a whole-platform structure.
  • MDU: Executed and assignable property agreements, controlled units, activated units, economics, and wiring rights are required. A landing page and blank form earn zero premium.
  • Oak Hill sequencing: The GoNetspeed/Greenlight JV must close and migrate customers on plan. Delay, repricing, or operating strain should defer Omni.
