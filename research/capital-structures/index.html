Capital options: Rightfiber bolt-ons and Omni carrier packaging

As of: 2026-09-20
Evidence: Public company, government, and SEC sources only. Public fact is marked [P], analytical inference [I], and unresolved information [U]. No private transcripts or relationship records were reviewed.

Decision

Rightfiber: Lead with cash plus optional seller rollover for a small, coherent retail bolt-on such as Dragonfly. Do not size it from Rightfiber's announced $1.6 billion facility. [P] Drawn debt, availability, covenants, and committed build capex are undisclosed. [I] For Ubiquity/FiberFirst and Gigabit Fiber, pursue a selected-market carveout, wholesale agreement, or ring-fenced JV before whole-company M&A. [S01, S04-S06]

Omni: Start with a selected-market wholesale anchor pilot. Omni retains the network; the carrier owns retail. Require minimum revenue, take-or-pay, prepayment, or explicit capex contribution before granting exclusivity. A 50/50 JV follows only after whitespace, conversion, installation, and MDU rights are proven. [U] No public evidence establishes an installed MDU base or wholesale economics. [S05, S10, S16]

Precedents without false multiples

T-Mobile/Oak Hill, announced not closed. [P] On April 28, 2026, T-Mobile announced an expected approximately $2.0 billion investment at a 1H 2027 closing for a 50% interest in a GoNetspeed/Greenlight JV and substantially all residential customers. The disclosure does not allocate network equity, customer-book value, seller proceeds, or primary capital. It is not a whole-company valuation or per-passing precedent. S12

Metronet, closed. [P] The announcement said Oak Hill and the founder would retain minority stakes, the network would become wholesale, and residential customers would transfer to T-Mobile. T-Mobile's 2025 Form 10-K records $4.6 billion invested for a 50% JV interest and 713,000 customers, without allocating equity, customer, funding, debt, or fee value. This supports customer/network separation and rollover, not an implied multiple. [S13-S14]

Lumos, closed. [P] The April2025 Lumos closing release described $950million invested at close. The later 2025 Form10-K reports $932million for the 50% JV interest and acquired customer relationships; use that later filed amount for the historical investment. A further $500million planned for2027-2028 is future expansion funding. T-Mobile 2025 Form10-K The network JV and T-Mobile customer ownership are explicit. The staged funding shows why future capex cannot be treated as seller proceeds. S15

Verizon/Eaton, commercial agreement. [P] Eaton funds and builds the network and handles maintenance and installation. Verizon has residential retail exclusivity for the build and an additional period and handles sales, marketing, and end-user service. Economics, duration, and minimum commitments are not public. [I] Omni should borrow the role clarity, but negotiate stronger capex protection if it is expected to fund the network. S16

AT&T/Lumen, closed asset carveout. [P] AT&T paid $5.75 billion cash for substantially all of Lumen's 11-state mass-markets FTTH business, including more than one million subscribers and four million fiber locations. Lumen retained backbone assets and provided an IRU, transition services, and long-term network services. Define customers, local assets, retained backbone, shared services, and liabilities before price. The headline is not a Rightfiber multiple. [S17-S18]

Rightfiber route by target

Dragonfly: cash plus optional rollover. [P] Dragonfly reported more than 20,000 homes covered in March 2026 across mixed wireless and fiber, not subscribers or FTTH passings. Alabama's January 2026 BEAD schedule assigns SP Broadband 7,884 fiber BSLs; awards are not construction. [I] Control fits only if customer cohorts, assets, grant match, completion capex, Florida separation, and adjacency work. Protect grant compliance through escrow, indemnity, or milestones. [S02-S03]

Ubiquity/FiberFirst: carveout or partnership. [P] Ubiquity disclosed $420 million of facilities in December 2024, not draws, and AT&T announced wholesale access plus targeted MDU/HOA and exclusive greenfield builds. [I] Tenant rights, financing, the owned retail layer, and Omaha/Texas overlap argue for a market carveout, reciprocal wholesale arrangement, or JV with lender and tenant consents. [S04-S05]

Gigabit Fiber: route-level structure. [P] Blue Owl acquired a majority position in September 2025; management retained material equity. Gigabit reported approximately 500 conduit route miles and more than 50 data centers, not fiber miles or FTTH locations. [I] Open with interconnection, route carveout, or project JV after continuity, capacity, utilization, concentration, and capex are verified. S06

Omni package and MDU test

[P] Omni publishes two blank right-of-entry forms with different duration terms. The July-path form contemplates perpetual access and express successor assignment. The September-path form contemplates a 20-year initial term followed by one-year renewals subject to notice. Neither blank form proves executed property rights or a universal current standard. Executed property agreements control, and the later URL does not prove that earlier executed agreements were replaced.

The wholesale term sheet should specify address eligibility, installation acceptance, build milestones, minimum revenue or take-or-pay, exclusivity by address and time, second-tenant rights, service credits, customer data, migration, termination value, and residual network use. The structure flips to a JV only if the carrier contributes separately stated primary capital and customer-book value at terms better than the risk-adjusted wholesale case. It flips to an asset sale only if carrier control value exceeds Omni's retained network NPV plus separation and stranded-cost burdens.

Transparent formulas

These are modeling conventions, not estimates of any company's undisclosed financial results. Keep the acquisition's economic cost separate from the cash needed at closing.

  1. Incremental serviceable locations = unique target ready-for-service locations excluding the union of buyer overlap, non-serviceable locations, and locations without transferable rights. Remove duplicates before counting and exclude each location only once. Planned and grant-awarded BSLs remain a separate completion cohort.

  2. Build investment schedule includes make-ready, feeder/distribution, electronics, drops/CPE/install, customer acquisition, attributable overhead, contingency and working capital. Schedule grants and landlord/carrier contributions when collectible. Apply delay through the timing of cash flows; do not add a second delay penalty for the same deferred cash flows.

  3. Gross cash uses at closing = cash purchase consideration to sellers after rollover + debt actually repaid/refinanced + transaction fees + funded integration/completion reserves + required opening cash. Include consideration paid into seller escrow or remitted on sellers' behalf within purchase consideration. Include only reserves actually funded at closing; separately schedule unfunded future capex. This is a cash bridge, not enterprise value or total lifetime acquisition cost.

  4. Buyer equity funding = gross cash uses at closing - new funded debt - usable acquired cash - third-party primary equity - grant cash actually available at closing. Count each source once. Seller rollover has already reduced cash purchase consideration and is not deducted again. Debt left in place is neither a cash funding source nor a payoff use; include it in post-close debt and leverage. Future grant reimbursements are not cash available at closing. Cash already netted in the purchase consideration cannot also be a funding source. The $1.6 billion facility is not evidence of funded debt or available headroom.

Illustrative closing bridge: gross seller equity value 100 less rollover 20 gives cash purchase consideration 80. With no other uses, usable acquired cash of 10 and new funded debt of 30, buyer equity funding is 40. These arbitrary units are not company financials. Adding 5 of fees makes the funding requirement 45. Retained debt does not create closing cash.

  1. Unlevered build-versus-buy economics: compare the same eligible asset perimeter, ownership share, forecast horizon, discount rate, tax convention and residual-value method. For a 100% asset comparison, NPV cost of buying = agreed debt-free, cash-free enterprise value + incremental acquisition costs at close - PV of future unlevered free cash flow - PV of terminal value. Future unlevered free cash flow includes after-tax operations, completion/replacement capex, integration spending, working capital and grants exactly once. Do not also add those same future costs to initial acquisition cost. Rollover and retained debt affect financing, not the 100% enterprise purchase cost. Acquired excess cash is excluded from both the debt-free, cash-free enterprise value and operating cash flows.

For building, NPV cost of building = initial project investment - PV of future unlevered free cash flow - PV of terminal value, with all subsequent construction spending and receipts in the future cash flows. Do not subtract grant cash twice or count initial investment again in future cash flows. Restrict terminal value to cash flows beyond the explicit forecast. Lower risk-adjusted NPV cost is preferable, subject to execution capacity and control. Show cost per incremental serviceable location and months to ready-for-service. A buyer-equity return analysis is a separate calculation using the buyer's actual ownership, debt service, distributions and exit equity proceeds; never pair a reduced buyer equity check with 100% of enterprise cash flow.

  1. Seller proceeds: gross seller equity value = agreed enterprise value - debt/debt-like items + excess cash +/- normalized working-capital adjustment. Cash delivered directly to sellers = gross seller equity value - rollover value - seller expenses - escrow/holdback - taxes/withholding. Rollover is non-cash. Escrow and seller-directed payments remain uses of purchase consideration in the closing bridge, even though sellers do not receive that cash directly on closing day.

  2. Carrier JV cash commitment = customer-book purchase price + secondary equity purchase + primary JV equity + separately funded capex reserve + carrier-paid fees, with each component mutually exclusive. If primary equity funds the capex reserve, do not add that reserve again. Identify the legal recipient of each payment: customer-book consideration goes to its seller, secondary consideration to selling shareholders, and primary capital stays in the JV. A headline commitment is not all sponsor proceeds.

  3. MDU annual wholesale contribution = activated units x [12 x monthly wholesale fee - annual variable network opex per active unit - annual property payment per active unit] - fixed building/network opex. Activated units = contracted ready-for-service units x penetration. Convert any revenue-share percentage to dollars on its contractual revenue base; put fixed property charges with fixed expenses. Project NPV deducts entrance, riser/common-area, electronics, drops/CPE/install, launch and replacement capex and models activation and churn cohorts.

What would flip the recommendations

  • Rightfiber moves from cash to rollover if seller management is essential, the seller accepts proportional downside and capital calls, and minority rights do not block integration.
  • Rightfiber moves from whole-company to carveout if grants, wireless assets, states, debt, tenant contracts, or shared systems cannot transfer cleanly.
  • Omni moves from wholesale to JV if the carrier funds meaningful primary capital, separately values customers, accepts clear future-capex rules, and address-level conversion validates the channel.
  • Omni retains retail if risk-adjusted retail contribution after sales, care, billing, content, bad debt, and churn exceeds contracted wholesale contribution.
  • Omni sells assets only if the carrier requires ownership and consideration exceeds retained network value plus separation costs, lost second-tenant option, and stranded overhead.

Unresolved diligence

Rightfiber liquidity and covenants; target debt, EBITDA and capex-to-complete; exact route/location overlap; grant and contract change-of-control; Omni property schedules and unit cohorts; wholesale minimums, exclusivity, SLA and termination economics; and the undisclosed allocation of precedent carrier cheques. These are unknown, not zero.
