Armstrong / MCTV transition

Independent operator screen | P2: selective

Current ownership

Family owned and operated within Armstrong Group. No sale process was identified in the reviewed primary sources.

Commercial fit (inference)

Strategically important Omni-adjacent competitor and potential boundary-rationalization or partnership counterparty. Full-company M&A would be much larger and less actionable than a small tuck-in, with no public seller signal found.

Possible transaction or partnership

Senior family-owner dialogue; test appetite for asset swaps, edge-market partnerships, grant-build financing or a broader strategic combination only after exact overlap and corporate perimeter work.

Constraints

Scale, family control, six-state perimeter, mixed legacy cable and FTTH definitions, and likely competitive overlap with Omni. BEAD award is provisional and should not be treated as constructed locations. MCTV should not be screened as an unencumbered independent seller. Customer pages show integration; exact legal closing date is not established by the reviewed releases.

Countercase

Armstrong is investing and building at meaningful scale. Public evidence supports an active operator, not a motivated seller.

Geographic clusters

  • Butler County: completed southern-county FTTH upgrade in Cranberry, Gibsonia, Mars, Seven Fields and Valencia; Zelienople was a 2025 target.
  • Fayette County: Connellsville project announced for more than 4,500 homes and businesses, slated for early 2025; completion was not verified.
  • Western Pennsylvania overlap: current local pages and the Omni plan indicate competitive adjacency across Butler, Fayette, Lawrence, Mercer and Westmoreland, but this screen did not calculate location overlap.
  • Medina County
  • Mahoning County and Poland Township
  • Ashland and Richland-area communities
  • eastern and northeastern Ohio
  • Massillon and Stark County
  • Wooster and Wayne County
  • eastern Ohio and Ohio-West Virginia border communities

Construction evidence

  • Completed and activated 970 miles of fiber for more than 44,000 homes and businesses in southern Butler County as of 2025-01-10.
  • Announced Connellsville expansion to more than 4,500 homes and businesses on 2025-02-04; target status only.
  • Provisionally awarded 3,425 BEAD fiber locations and $19,898,209 as of 2026-04-27; county allocation and contract execution were not shown in the statewide summary.
  • The Supreme Court of Ohio reporter published a 2025-07-21 appellate decision stating that Armstrong's 2024 Poland Township, Mahoning County project was a multi-million-dollar transition to fiber. The court record says Armstrong had completed more than 50% of the permitted work by September 2024.
  • Armstrong's current company page says the broader six-state network reaches more than 400,000 homes. It does not say all are Ohio FTTH passings.
  • The 2026-02-17 transaction announcement said MCTV had made significant FTTH investments and was nearing completion of its network transformation.
  • MCTV's current Excellerate page says FTTH is being rolled out across its service area on a neighborhood-by-neighborhood basis. The page does not publish county completion percentages.

Reported scale and date

  • More than 400,000 homes reached across six states, company live page accessed 2026-09-20.
  • 970 fiber miles activated and more than 44,000 homes and businesses addressed in the southern Butler project, 2025-01-10.
  • More than $150 million invested in FTTH, company statement dated 2025-01-10.
  • More than 400,000 homes reached across six states on the current company page accessed 2026-09-20; technology mix and Ohio share are undisclosed.
  • More than 50% of the permitted Poland Township fiber upgrade completed by September 2024, according to a court decision dated 2025-07-21; no passing count was disclosed.
  • More than 96,000 passings across 13 counties in eastern Ohio and West Virginia, disclosed in the 2026-02-17 transaction announcement.
  • More than 60,000 homes and businesses served in Ohio and West Virginia on MCTV's current service-area page accessed 2026-09-20. This company phrasing is not labeled subscribers.

Greenlight operating brands: FastBridge / Loop

Committed or integrated platform | P3: partner / monitor

Current ownership

FastBridge closed into Greenlight on July6,2026; Loop closed into Greenlight in October2025. Greenlight is in the announced Oak Hill / T-Mobile JV, expected to close in the first half of2027; current T-Mobile control is not established.

Commercial fit (inference)

Pennsylvania competitive and wholesale context for Omni. These are acquired operating brands, not independent sellers.

Possible transaction or partnership

Track the Greenlight platform and proposed carrier JV; evaluate specific network access or interconnection if commercially needed.

Constraints

Already acquired brands and a pending parent-platform transaction; retailer, network ownership and customer-transfer rights must remain separate.

Countercase

A standalone FastBridge sale thesis is obsolete after the July 2026 close.

Geographic clusters

  • Berks County: Spring and Lower Heidelberg townships, Wyomissing and Sinking Spring.
  • Columbia, Montour and Lycoming counties: Bloomsburg, Danville and Williamsport.
  • Erie and Luzerne counties: Erie and Hazleton construction/market presence.
  • Lackawanna County: Scranton and surrounding communities.
  • Luzerne County: Wilkes-Barre, Kingston, Nanticoke, Drums, Hazleton and Mountain Top.

Construction evidence

  • Berks construction covered more than 7,000 homes plus hundreds of businesses on the company page updated 2025-07-23.
  • At the 2026-07-06 acquisition close, Greenlight said construction was underway and tens of thousands more passings were expected by year-end. This is a target, not actual completion.
  • Greenlight reported more than $24 million of 2026 Northeast Pennsylvania expansion capital and construction progressing across seven named communities as of 2026-03-12.
  • Loop was fully integrated into Greenlight by 2026-07-23.

Reported scale and date

  • More than 65,000 household passings added to Greenlight across the acquired Pennsylvania and New York perimeter, 2026-07-06. No state split disclosed.
  • 120 FastBridge employees joined Greenlight, 2026-07-06.
  • Nearly 36,000 passings added through Loop, reported 2026-07-23.
  • More than 30,000 additional passings targeted through the rest of 2026; target, not actual.
  • Northeast Pennsylvania network had nearly 42,500 households and businesses able to connect as of 2026-03-12; broader Greenlight regional perimeter, not Loop-only.

Hickory Telephone Company / Aurora by HickoryTel / Advanced Telephone

Independent operator screen | P2: selective

Current ownership

Local ILEC/CLEC group. Beneficial ownership and public/private status were not disclosed in the reviewed primary sources. Advanced Telephone is an affiliated operating name used with Hickory/Aurora in Washington County records.

Commercial fit (inference)

High-quality Omni-adjacent tuck-in or operating-partnership candidate because Omni identifies Washington County as a planned market and Hickory brings an established rural ILEC base plus expansion experience.

Possible transaction or partnership

Direct owner/management discussion, or Washington County public-private partnership dialogue, focused on capital needs, succession and integration with Omni's western Pennsylvania ring.

Constraints

Unknown subscriber and financial scale, undisclosed beneficial ownership, regulated ILEC perimeter, grant obligations and potentially noncontiguous rural routes.

Countercase

The entire base was already FTTH-capable by 2020 and Hickory won county work, which may support independence rather than sale urgency.

Geographic clusters

  • Washington County: Hickory base and expansion into Avella, Jefferson Township, Canton Township and Taylorstown.

Construction evidence

  • Hickory says it began replacing lines with FTTH in 2012 and completed Aurora availability to its entire subscriber base in 2020.
  • Washington County awarded Aurora/Hickory and Advanced Telephone a 47-mile, 383-target project in Jefferson Township, Canton Township and Taylorstown for $4,878,797 on 2023-03-17.
  • Advanced Telephone received a provisional 2026 BEAD award for 12 fiber locations and $17,229; county was not disclosed.

Reported scale and date

  • 47 fiber route miles and 383 residential/commercial targets in the county award, 2023-03-17.
  • Entire legacy subscriber base FTTH-capable by 2020, company statement; subscriber count unknown.

QCOL, Inc.

Independent operator screen | P2: selective

Current ownership

Local operating company. Beneficial ownership and public/private status are unknown. QCOL's site carries a VitaLink copyright and links to a VitaLink careers page, suggesting affiliation, but legal ownership was not established.

Commercial fit (inference)

Potentially the cleanest small Omni tuck-in because it is based in Fayette County, one of Omni's stated nearly 200,000-location expansion counties. The thesis is geographic, not yet financial.

Possible transaction or partnership

Resolve QCOL/VitaLink ownership and network perimeter first, then approach local management on tuck-in M&A, construction capital or wholesale integration with Omni's Fayette build.

Constraints

No public passings, subscribers, miles or financials; QCOL/VitaLink perimeter uncertainty; high rural build costs; existing enforceable commitments.

Countercase

The FCC found in 2022 that QCOL defaulted on three Pennsylvania RDOF census block groups and assessed a $9,000 forfeiture. That is historical, not proof of current weakness, but it raises execution-diligence needs.

Geographic clusters

  • Fayette County: Markleysburg base and 2024 state challenge evidence for FTTP/enforceable commitments. Pennsylvania footprint outside Fayette was not established in this bounded review.

Construction evidence

  • QCOL says it has offered FTTH since 2004 and its live sign-up page says it is extending fiber as fast as it can; neither statement supplies miles or passings.
  • Pennsylvania's July 2024 BEAD challenge summary records QCOL FTTP enforceable-commitment locations in Fayette County with advertised 8 Gbps symmetrical service.
  • VitaLink received 188 provisional 2026 BEAD fiber locations and $1,727,845, but the award cannot be attributed to QCOL without corporate-perimeter proof.

Reported scale and date

  • Passings, subscribers and fiber miles: unknown as of 2026-09-20.
  • 188 locations and $1.73 million provisional BEAD award sit with VitaLink, not proven QCOL legal perimeter, 2026-04-27.

Salsgiver, Inc. / Salsgiver Telecom

Independent operator screen | P2: selective

Current ownership

Local telecom group. FCC records identify Loren M. Salsgiver as president; beneficial ownership and public/private status were not established in the reviewed primary sources.

Commercial fit (inference)

Potential Omni-adjacent tuck-in, enterprise-fiber extension or construction/operations partner around Indiana County and the Alle-Kiski Valley. Asset mix may be more enterprise, transport and managed services than a pure residential FTTH platform.

Possible transaction or partnership

Direct president/owner dialogue after separating Salsgiver Inc., Salsgiver Telecom and any affiliate assets; test sale, minority capital, network lease or joint construction.

Constraints

Opaque corporate perimeter and scale, mixed service lines, grant/operating obligations, unknown title to route assets and residential-versus-enterprise mix.

Countercase

The FCC resolved late disconnected-number reporting through a 2025 consent decree, compliance plan and $14,000 voluntary contribution. The order did not question basic license qualifications, so this is a diligence item rather than an operating disqualification.

Geographic clusters

  • Indiana County: West, South and North Mahoning townships, Smicksburg, Cherryhill Township and Center Township under the 2023 Phase I contract.
  • Western Pennsylvania operating base: Freeport/Sarver and the Alle-Kiski Valley; current residential passings by county are unknown.

Construction evidence

  • Indiana County approved a $2,315,395 Phase I contract on 2023-01-11 for Salsgiver to construct, operate and maintain fiber in targeted unserved and underserved areas plus four public Wi-Fi locations.
  • The July 2024 Pennsylvania challenge summary records Salsgiver FTTP enforceable commitments in Indiana County at advertised 10 Gbps symmetrical service. Completion of the full 2023 contract perimeter was not independently verified.

Reported scale and date

  • $2,315,395 contracted build cost across named Indiana County routes and four public Wi-Fi locations, 2023-01-11.
  • Company-wide passings, subscribers, fiber miles and revenue: unknown as of 2026-09-20.

Tri-Co Connections

Cooperative / municipal partner | P2: selective

Current ownership

Broadband subsidiary of member-owned Tri-County Rural Electric Cooperative.

Commercial fit (inference)

Strong rural deployment comparator and possible wholesale, operations or financing partner. It is not a credible control-M&A target absent cooperative governance action and member benefit.

Possible transaction or partnership

Co-op board and management partnership, grant financing, middle-mile/interconnection or shared services, not conventional sponsor-backed acquisition outreach.

Constraints

Member ownership, mission orientation, rural density, grant obligations and north-central geography outside Omni's disclosed western Pennsylvania counties.

Countercase

Construction is already complete to all co-op members, reducing greenfield funding need and making a sale less aligned with the cooperative mission.

Geographic clusters

  • Tioga, Potter, Bradford, Lycoming, McKean, Cameron and Clinton counties across the cooperative's approximately 5,000-square-mile electric service territory.

Construction evidence

  • Tri-County reported in 2025 that construction was complete and every Pennsylvania electric member had access to fiber internet.
  • The completed network was described as almost 3,000 fiber miles.

Reported scale and date

  • Almost 3,000 fiber miles complete, 2025 annual meeting.
  • More than 8,500 members connected, 2025 annual meeting.
  • Nearly 17,000 electric members across seven counties and 5,000 square miles, live company page accessed 2026-09-20.
