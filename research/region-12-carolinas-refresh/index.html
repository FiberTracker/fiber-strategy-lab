Ripple Fiber

Committed or integrated platform | P3: partner / monitor

Current ownership

Eaton Fiber agreed to acquire Ripple in July2026, with completion expected before year-end2026. Ripple shareholders Platform Investment Partners and KLT are expected to retain exposure through Eaton alongside Tillman and Bain. Closing is not verified.

Commercial fit (inference)

Relevant carrier wholesale precedent and potential interconnection counterparty. The pending Eaton transaction supersedes an independent whole-company target thesis.

Possible transaction or partnership

Track the Eaton/Verizon transaction and post-close wholesale or boundary opportunities; do not assume a separate Ripple sale is available.

Constraints

Pending signed transaction, lender and regulatory consents, Verizon exclusive-retail arrangements and a split of customer/network assets.

Countercase

A signed parent-platform transaction and future Verizon retail arrangements constrain separate negotiations. Test specific wholesale or boundary opportunities only after ownership, exclusivity and network/customer perimeters are established.

Geographic clusters

  • Charlotte and Union County, NC
  • Statesville and Iredell County, NC
  • Outer Banks, NC
  • Harnett County, NC
  • Lexington and Greenville, SC offices

Construction evidence

  • Completed: on July 14, 2026, Ripple reported completion of a $20 million initial Outer Banks project reaching more than 20,000 homes and businesses across Kitty Hawk, Southern Shores, Duck, Kill Devil Hills and Corolla.
  • In progress: on December 4, 2025, Ripple said more than 5,000 Statesville homes were fiber-ready, with an 18,000-premise project target and active construction on Salisbury Road and West Downtown.
  • Platform: as of May 28, 2025, Ripple reported more than 170,000 deployed fiber passings across seven states, plus more than 100,000 passings and 13,000 subscribers added over the preceding 12 months. These are company-reported totals, not a Carolinas-only perimeter.

Reported scale and date

  • More than 170,000 deployed fiber passings across seven states, 2025-05-28, company-reported
  • More than 20,000 homes and businesses reached in the completed Outer Banks initial phase, 2026-07-14
  • $350 million debt capacity, 2025-05-28

TruVista Fiber

Scaled strategic platform | P2: selective

Current ownership

Current iCON Infrastructure portfolio company. iCON's current investment page lists TruVista; the exact fund, ownership percentage and management ownership are not public in the reviewed current sources.

Commercial fit (inference)

[I] Strong sponsor-backed platform with a compact South Carolina Midlands base and demonstrated acquisition capability. The Carolinas fit is good, but Georgia expansion creates a moderate geographic penalty and may be central to the owner's thesis rather than non-core.

Possible transaction or partnership

[I] Sponsor-level dialogue on exit timing, recapitalization or a buy-and-build plan is the clearest path. Near-term add-on sourcing in adjacent South Carolina and Georgia rural systems is also credible.

Constraints

Unknown: No current revenue, EBITDA, leverage, subscriber count or sponsor hold-period plan was publicly verified.; The company-reported 2025 scale statement does not define whether 56,914 locations are incremental, total available locations or activated customers.

Countercase

[I] Recent acquisitions and the April 2026 Commerce announcement may indicate continued investment rather than sale readiness.

Geographic clusters

  • Chester County, SC
  • Fairfield County, SC
  • Kershaw County, SC
  • parts of Richland County, SC
  • north and south Georgia clusters

Construction evidence

  • Company-reported actual: the current TruVista history says 2025 fiber coverage expanded by 5,751 miles and extended availability to 56,914 homes and businesses. The wording does not disclose whether both figures are gross additions or resulting footprint.
  • Closed acquisitions: PlantTel was completed by 2022 and SlyTel was completed in December 2025. TruVista also announced a City of Commerce telecom-network acquisition in April 2026, but the reviewed source did not establish closing.

Reported scale and date

  • 5,751 miles of fiber coverage expansion, 2025, company-reported
  • 56,914 homes and businesses with extended availability, 2025, company-reported
  • More than 270 employees, current company page accessed 2026-09-20

Comporium

Independent operator screen | P2: selective

Current ownership

Privately held. Comporium's 2019 history described fifth-generation Barnes family leadership, while its 2026 project release still described the company as privately held. No public evidence reviewed establishes a sale process or a change of control.

Commercial fit (inference)

[I] Attractive Carolinas density, recognizable local brand and proven grant-matching support a strategic-expansion thesis. Distance is moderate because the Rock Hill core, Midlands and Transylvania are separate operating clusters rather than one continuous footprint.

Possible transaction or partnership

[I] Prioritize family and management dialogue around growth financing, grant-match capital, refinancing and selective partnerships or tuck-ins. Do not premise outreach on a sale.

Constraints

The company serves a mixed broadband perimeter, so customer count and square miles are not FTTH passings.; Unknown: Public revenue, EBITDA, fiber subscribers and current FTTP passings were not verified.

Countercase

[I] A 130-year family-controlled operating history and visible self-funded builds argue for patience and partnership, not assumed control availability.

Geographic clusters

  • York, Lancaster and Chester counties, SC
  • Lexington, Saluda, Edgefield, Orangeburg, Calhoun and Aiken counties, SC
  • Transylvania County, NC

Construction evidence

  • Completed: on March 26, 2026, Comporium said 64 miles of fiber and $4.7 million of combined private, county and GREAT funding reached 517 homes and businesses in Transylvania County.
  • Completed: on December 12, 2024, Comporium said 156 miles and nearly $7.3 million reached 1,442 homes and businesses in Lexington and Aiken counties.

Reported scale and date

  • More than 150,000 customers, undated current company page accessed 2026-09-20
  • More than 4,500 square miles of service area, undated current company page accessed 2026-09-20
  • Nearly 1,000 employees on current about page; more than 900 in the 2024 project release

RiverStreet

Cooperative / municipal partner | P2: selective

Current ownership

Member-owned cooperative rooted in Wilkes Telephone Membership Corporation. RiverStreet is the unified operating name for Wilkes Communications and RiverStreet Networks.

Commercial fit (inference)

Consider project-specific completion capital or operating transfers, subject to cooperative and public-partner consent. The West Piedmont project lost RiverStreet as provider in August2026 following a funding shortfall; this is not proof of a statewide exit or company sale.

Possible transaction or partnership

[I] Cooperative merger, adjacent rural-system acquisition, RUS or bank debt, grant bridge financing and shared-services combinations are more credible than a sponsor buyout.

Constraints

Cooperative governance, documented project-level financing shortfall and public obligations. The status and remaining cost of each retained project must be verified separately.

Countercase

[I] The operating dispersion may outweigh scale benefits for a buyer seeking a compact Carolinas platform.

Geographic clusters

  • Wilkes and neighboring western NC counties
  • Stokes County, NC
  • Beaufort, Hyde and Washington counties, NC
  • Camden and Currituck project areas, NC
  • multiple rural Virginia counties
  • Southside Virginia
  • West Piedmont historical award area
  • Dinwiddie and eastern project counties

Construction evidence

  • Current build: RiverStreet says its multi-phase Stokes County plan is designed to reach more than 6,770 homes and businesses, with areas available, under construction and in future engineering as of the page reviewed.
  • Awarded, not completed: NCDIT announced a 2025 CAB award for 921 Camden County homes and businesses.
  • Awarded for 2026 delivery, not verified complete: NCDIT awarded $959,828.18 for 306 locations across Camden, Currituck, Stokes and Wilkes counties in March 2026.
  • The August 2025 VATI ledger showed 8,167 of 24,641 East-project passings, 31 of 10,056 West-project passings and zero of 5,009 Dinwiddie project passings with access; all three were flagged high risk.
  • RiverStreet's current project page says BEAD expansion required redesign and refinancing and acknowledges delays since March 2025.
  • On 2026-08-05, the West Piedmont commission terminated RiverStreet's West-project role after it could not secure remaining capital and contracted with ZiTEL for the remaining scope.

Reported scale and date

  • Roughly 30,000 customers across rural North Carolina and Virginia, current company page accessed 2026-09-20
  • More than 6,770 planned Stokes County homes and businesses, page dated 2025-11-23
  • 17 North Carolina serviceable counties listed on the current company site
  • RiverStreet's November 2024 Virginia BEAD letter claimed approximately 14,300 Virginia customers and 36,900 North Carolina customers. These are applicant-reported customers, not independently audited state counts.
  • The same letter described more than 6,300 planned Virginia route miles and more than 74,500 target locations across nine counties. These are build-plan figures, not completed network scale.

Sandhill Telephone Cooperative and Sandhill Connextions

Cooperative / municipal partner | P2: selective

Current ownership

Member-owned telephone cooperative serving its historical and adjacent South Carolina communities under the Sandhill brand.

Commercial fit (inference)

[I] Compact Pee Dee geography and visible grant-backed conversion produce a low distance penalty and a sensible adjacency candidate. Small scale and cooperative governance reduce conventional fee paths.

Possible transaction or partnership

[I] Member-approved combination with an adjacent cooperative, capital-credit-aware financing, grant bridge or shared-services arrangement is more realistic than a sponsor acquisition.

Constraints

Cooperative governance and universal-service obligations constrain control and capital structure.; Unknown: Exact FTTP passings, subscribers and financials remain unverified.

Countercase

[I] The platform may be too small for a standalone transaction and may create more value as an adjacency partnership or tuck-in perimeter than as a control target.

Geographic clusters

  • Chesterfield County
  • Bethune area of Kershaw County
  • Marlboro County
  • Cheraw and parts of Darlington County

Construction evidence

  • Company-reported actual: in July 2025 Sandhill said it invested $5.7 million in the preceding year and had brought fiber to more than 75% of the people relying on its service.
  • Government award scope: the February 2024 SC Broadband Office summary listed two Chesterfield County FTTP projects totaling $4.154694 million and 556 locations, with construction completion targets of December 29, 2025. Completion was not independently verified here.
  • Company-reported cumulative investment: on July 1, 2026, Sandhill said grant funding and its own contributions had invested more than $25 million in Marlboro County during the prior decade.

Reported scale and date

  • More than 75% of people relying on the service reached by fiber, 2025-07-01, company-reported
  • $5.7 million invested in the preceding year, 2025-07-01
  • More than $25 million invested in Marlboro County during the prior decade, 2026-07-01, company-reported

SkyLine Membership Corporation and SkyBest Communications

Cooperative / municipal partner | P2: selective

Current ownership

SkyLine is a member-owned cooperative; SkyBest Communications is its wholly owned subsidiary for deregulated broadband and related services.

Commercial fit (inference)

[I] Strong fiber capability and an established subsidiary make SkyLine/SkyBest a credible cooperative expansion partner. The footprint is geographically compact, but mountain terrain, storm-restoration exposure and distance from the Piedmont and Midlands create a meaningful construction and integration penalty.

Possible transaction or partnership

[I] Member-approved merger, subsidiary-level partnership, grant-supported build or shared network and back-office services are the plausible routes.

Constraints

Member governance limits a conventional sale.; [I] Mountain terrain and storm-restoration requirements can raise build and operating complexity.; Unknown: Current revenue, leverage and total FTTP passings were not verified.

Countercase

[I] A compact High Country system can be strategically attractive, but difficult terrain and limited adjacency to central Carolinas clusters may depress synergy value.

Geographic clusters

  • Alleghany, Ashe, Avery and Watauga counties, NC
  • Johnson County, TN
  • limited upstate SC expansion

Construction evidence

  • Actual and in progress: on October 1, 2025, SkyBest said a new Riverside remote would serve more than 300 grant recipients and pass additional locations in Avery County.
  • Award scope: the first two Avery CAB rounds totaled nearly $10 million and more than 1,700 eligible locations, according to the same company release.
  • Awarded, not completed: on April 29, 2025, NCDIT listed a Watauga County award for 178 homes and businesses.

Reported scale and date

  • Five-county, 840-square-mile cooperative area, current company page accessed 2026-09-20
  • More than 1,700 Avery eligible locations across two CAB rounds, 2025-10-01

Lumos

Committed or integrated platform | P3: partner / monitor

Current ownership

Acquired on April 1, 2025 by a joint venture owned 50% by T-Mobile and 50% by EQT Infrastructure VI. Lumos now operates as a wholesale network platform, with T-Mobile the anchor tenant and owner of residential and small-business customer relationships.

Commercial fit (inference)

[I] Strategically important infrastructure partner, but low-priority acquisition target because the control transaction closed recently and committed capital is already in place. Distance penalty is high as expansion now spans numerous states.

Possible transaction or partnership

[I] Pursue wholesale, carrier, build-partner or local tuck-in opportunities, not a near-term control sale. Any financing dialogue should recognize T-Mobile's disclosed additional capital commitment for 2027 to 2028.

Constraints

Closed 50/50 strategic JV with an anchor tenant and defined wholesale model.; Public build figures mix homes reached, customers, route miles and forward targets; none are interchangeable.

Countercase

[I] The platform's capital plan, strategic anchor and recent acquisition materially reduce near-term advisory openness despite its scale.

Geographic clusters

  • Triad and central NC legacy footprint
  • Durham and Orange counties, NC
  • Johnston and Harnett counties, NC
  • New Hanover County, NC
  • Greenville, Spartanburg, Richland and Lexington counties, SC
  • Hampton Roads
  • Roanoke-Lynchburg corridor
  • Shenandoah Valley and Charlottesville
  • Alleghany Highlands

Construction evidence

  • Announced and under construction: Lumos announced nearly $100 million and 775 miles for Greenville County in October 2023; announced $56 million and 655 miles in New Hanover County in April 2023, then said construction was beginning in February 2024.
  • Awarded for 2026 delivery, not verified complete: NCDIT awarded Lumos $3.965221 million for 402 Orange County locations in March 2026.
  • Target, not actual: the JV stated a goal of reaching 3.5 million homes by year-end 2028.
  • The current Virginia service page lists operating or marketed communities including Virginia Beach, Chesapeake, Portsmouth, Roanoke, Lynchburg, Bedford, Charlottesville, Harrisonburg, Staunton and Waynesboro.
  • The Botetourt County VATI project reported 1,901 of 1,901 project passings with access and 269.73 fiber miles through August 2025. This is a grant-project perimeter, not the Virginia or company footprint.

Reported scale and date

  • 475,000 homes with connectivity and 7,500 network miles at the joint-venture close on April1,2025, companywide.
  • Approximately97,000 fiber customers transferred to T-Mobile at the April1,2025 close, companywide.
  • 3.5million homes passed by end-2028 is an announced target, not an actual.

Segra

Scaled strategic platform | P2: selective

Current ownership

Standalone fiber company owned by Cox Communications. Segra and Cox-owned Unite Private Networks completed their brand combination in November 2024.

Commercial fit (inference)

[I] High strategic relevance for enterprise backhaul, subsea, data-center and wholesale partnerships, but poor comparability to residential FTTH operators. Geographic distance penalty is high at the corporate level, although the Myrtle Beach to Florence to Charlotte and Raleigh corridor is directly relevant.

Possible transaction or partnership

[I] Focus on enterprise-route partnerships, dark fiber, data-center connectivity and potential non-core route or customer-perimeter opportunities. A control transaction is unlikely while Cox is actively integrating and investing.

Constraints

Strategic ownership by Cox and recent UPN integration reduce acquisition availability.; Route miles and connected enterprise locations cannot be compared with residential passings or subscribers.

Countercase

[I] Including Segra in a residential platform screen can overstate scale and distract from the more relevant enterprise partnership thesis.

Geographic clusters

  • Charlotte, NC
  • Raleigh, NC
  • Myrtle Beach and Florence, SC
  • broader Carolinas enterprise routes

Construction evidence

  • Completed: on May 18, 2026, Segra announced completion of a new 600G inland route from the Myrtle Beach cable landing station through Florence to Charlotte and Raleigh, with 4 Tbps of C-band capacity available on day one.
  • Corporate integration: the November 2024 Segra and UPN brand merger created a then-reported 44,000-route-mile, 20,000-connected-location network across 24 states.

Reported scale and date

  • 45,000 fiber route miles, 30,000 connected customer locations and 24 states, 2026-05-18
  • 600 Gb/s wavelength capability and 4 Tbps day-one C-band capacity on the Myrtle Beach inland route, 2026-05-18
