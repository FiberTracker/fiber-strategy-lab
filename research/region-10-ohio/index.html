altafiber

Independent operator screen | P2: selective

Current ownership

Privately owned. Macquarie Infrastructure Partners V completed its $2.9 billion take-private of Cincinnati Bell on 2021-09-07. altafiber is the operating brand in Ohio, Kentucky, and Indiana.

Commercial fit (inference)

High-priority carrier benchmark and potential future strategic counterparty. Its incumbent fiber depth, public-private partnership record, and central and southwest Ohio edge-outs compete with Omni around Wilmington, London, Eaton, and adjacent corridors. It also shows that a larger carrier can fund county-scale builds, which can pressure Omni's cost-of-capital and scarcity story.

Possible transaction or partnership

Strategic partnership, wholesale or interconnection, or potential buyer universe inference only. altafiber is not a plausible Omni bolt-on and no current sale process was found.

Constraints

Company-wide address and investment figures include non-Ohio markets unless specifically labeled. The Adams-Brown-Clermont figure is addressable locations, not completed passings or subscribers. Macquarie ownership does not establish acquisition appetite for Omni.

Countercase

Geographic overlap with Omni is selective rather than statewide, and altafiber's large incumbent perimeter and Hawaii exposure could make a small-market Ohio combination strategically unnecessary.

Geographic clusters

  • Greater Cincinnati
  • Dayton and Montgomery-Greene corridor
  • Central Ohio
  • Adams-Brown-Clermont rural project

Construction evidence

  • On 2026-02-17, fiber was available to 70,000 residential and business addresses in the Dayton region, including Montgomery and Greene Counties; the completed project target was $200 million and 2,300 fiber route miles.
  • On 2024-08-16, the company and BroadbandOhio described a $110 million build to approximately 38,000 single-family and multi-dwelling addressable locations in Adams, Brown, and Clermont Counties, including 13,263 unserved or underserved addresses. The state share was $50 million and altafiber's share was $60 million. The stated completion deadline was 2026-12-31, so this remains a target until completion is verified.

Reported scale and date

  • 70,000 residential and business addresses with fiber in the Dayton region as of 2026-02-17.
  • $200 million and 2,300 fiber route miles when the Dayton-region build is complete, a forward completed-project perimeter as of 2026-02-17.
  • Approximately 38,000 addressable locations in the Adams-Brown-Clermont project, a construction target disclosed 2024-08-16.

Glo Fiber / Horizon, owned by Shenandoah Telecommunications Company

Public-company operating brand | P3: partner / monitor

Current ownership

Public-company owned. Nasdaq-listed Shentel completed its acquisition of Horizon Telcom on 2024-04-01 and said it would rebrand Horizon's commercial and residential fiber businesses to Glo Fiber.

Commercial fit (inference)

Top strategic alternative because Horizon supplies both Ohio commercial fiber and FTTH beachheads. It can compete for enterprise, carrier, dark-fiber, and residential economics, so Omni should not pitch itself as uniquely carrier-ready without proving routes, buildings, and contracted wholesale demand.

Possible transaction or partnership

Potential strategic buyer, interconnection, or wholesale counterparty inference. Shentel is public and already executed the Horizon acquisition; no evidence shows it is seeking Omni or divesting Ohio.

Constraints

Latest SEC scale is consolidated across eight states. Ohio build announcements are targets or community-level counts and are not reconciled to the 2026 SEC passings table by state.

Countercase

Glo's principal Ohio clusters are south-central and southeast of much of Omni's northern build, so overlap may be lower than the statewide map suggests. Public-company leverage and growth targets may also constrain another large acquisition.

Geographic clusters

  • Ross County incumbent market
  • Chillicothe-Circleville-Lancaster-Washington Court House
  • Zanesville-Hillsboro-Jackson-Johnstown-Greenfield expansion corridor
  • Shenandoah Valley
  • Central Virginia and Campbell County
  • Virginia expansion markets within an eight-state company footprint

Construction evidence

  • On 2024-04-17, Glo announced engineering work for more than 40,000 additional Ohio homes and businesses in Zanesville, Hillsboro, Jackson, Johnstown, and Greenfield, while reporting service in Chillicothe, Circleville, Lancaster, and Washington Court House.
  • On 2025-02-03, Glo said more than 4,000 Lancaster homes and businesses already had service and construction would add approximately 10,000 more, with expected completion in the second half of 2025. Completion was not independently verified here.
  • Shentel reported completion of a more than $20 million Campbell County project reaching more than 4,100 previously unserved homes on 2026-05-19.
  • The 2025 Form 10-K reports a $5 million Virginia FTTH business acquisition closed on 2025-07-09, adding approximately 1,500 passings and 700 customers. This was an acquired retail operating asset, not a cooperative operating partnership.

Reported scale and date

  • 475,677 Glo Fiber expansion-market homes and businesses passed as of 2026-06-30 across the multi-state Shentel perimeter.
  • 19,847 fiber route miles as of 2026-06-30 across Shentel's integrated multi-state network.
  • 100,155 Glo Fiber expansion-market Residential and SMB RGUs as of 2026-06-30; this is not Ohio-only and should not be treated as subscribers.
  • 475,677 Glo expansion passings and 100,155 Glo RGUs at 2026-06-30, companywide.
  • 728,736 total company passings and 19,847 route miles at 2026-06-30 across eight states.
  • Glo construction substantially complete by end-2026 is management guidance, not an actual.

Armstrong / MCTV transition

Independent operator screen | P2: selective

Current ownership

Family owned and operated within Armstrong Group. No sale process was identified in the reviewed primary sources.

Commercial fit (inference)

Strategically important Omni-adjacent competitor and potential boundary-rationalization or partnership counterparty. Full-company M&A would be much larger and less actionable than a small tuck-in, with no public seller signal found.

Possible transaction or partnership

Senior family-owner dialogue; test appetite for asset swaps, edge-market partnerships, grant-build financing or a broader strategic combination only after exact overlap and corporate perimeter work.

Constraints

Scale, family control, six-state perimeter, mixed legacy cable and FTTH definitions, and likely competitive overlap with Omni. BEAD award is provisional and should not be treated as constructed locations. MCTV should not be screened as an unencumbered independent seller. Customer pages show integration; exact legal closing date is not established by the reviewed releases.

Countercase

Armstrong is investing and building at meaningful scale. Public evidence supports an active operator, not a motivated seller.

Geographic clusters

  • Butler County: completed southern-county FTTH upgrade in Cranberry, Gibsonia, Mars, Seven Fields and Valencia; Zelienople was a 2025 target.
  • Fayette County: Connellsville project announced for more than 4,500 homes and businesses, slated for early 2025; completion was not verified.
  • Western Pennsylvania overlap: current local pages and the Omni plan indicate competitive adjacency across Butler, Fayette, Lawrence, Mercer and Westmoreland, but this screen did not calculate location overlap.
  • Medina County
  • Mahoning County and Poland Township
  • Ashland and Richland-area communities
  • eastern and northeastern Ohio
  • Massillon and Stark County
  • Wooster and Wayne County
  • eastern Ohio and Ohio-West Virginia border communities

Construction evidence

  • Completed and activated 970 miles of fiber for more than 44,000 homes and businesses in southern Butler County as of 2025-01-10.
  • Announced Connellsville expansion to more than 4,500 homes and businesses on 2025-02-04; target status only.
  • Provisionally awarded 3,425 BEAD fiber locations and $19,898,209 as of 2026-04-27; county allocation and contract execution were not shown in the statewide summary.
  • The Supreme Court of Ohio reporter published a 2025-07-21 appellate decision stating that Armstrong's 2024 Poland Township, Mahoning County project was a multi-million-dollar transition to fiber. The court record says Armstrong had completed more than 50% of the permitted work by September 2024.
  • Armstrong's current company page says the broader six-state network reaches more than 400,000 homes. It does not say all are Ohio FTTH passings.
  • The 2026-02-17 transaction announcement said MCTV had made significant FTTH investments and was nearing completion of its network transformation.
  • MCTV's current Excellerate page says FTTH is being rolled out across its service area on a neighborhood-by-neighborhood basis. The page does not publish county completion percentages.

Reported scale and date

  • More than 400,000 homes reached across six states, company live page accessed 2026-09-20.
  • 970 fiber miles activated and more than 44,000 homes and businesses addressed in the southern Butler project, 2025-01-10.
  • More than $150 million invested in FTTH, company statement dated 2025-01-10.
  • More than 400,000 homes reached across six states on the current company page accessed 2026-09-20; technology mix and Ohio share are undisclosed.
  • More than 50% of the permitted Poland Township fiber upgrade completed by September 2024, according to a court decision dated 2025-07-21; no passing count was disclosed.
  • More than 96,000 passings across 13 counties in eastern Ohio and West Virginia, disclosed in the 2026-02-17 transaction announcement.
  • More than 60,000 homes and businesses served in Ohio and West Virginia on MCTV's current service-area page accessed 2026-09-20. This company phrasing is not labeled subscribers.

Buckeye Broadband

Independent operator screen | P2: selective

Current ownership

Wholly owned by privately held Block Communications, Inc., a Toledo-headquartered diversified media and telecommunications company.

Commercial fit (inference)

Direct competitive threat in Omni's Toledo, Perrysburg, Rossford, Sandusky, and Huron markets. Buckeye's local brand, cable base, and parallel FTTH rebuild can raise Omni's acquisition cost and lower expected penetration. Block's adjacent commercial telecom assets also make Buckeye a carrier-grade alternative rather than only a residential cable incumbent.

Possible transaction or partnership

Possible future strategic counterparty or asset-swap inference only. No public sale process or stated acquisition interest was found; family-controlled Block ownership can support long-duration investment.

Constraints

Buckeye does not publish a current primary-source total for FTTH passings. Marketing references to a fiber-optic network do not prove every address is FTTH.

Countercase

A legacy cable conversion can carry different economics and architecture from greenfield FTTH, and Buckeye's tight northwest Ohio focus may limit fit outside direct overlap counties.

Geographic clusters

  • Toledo and Lucas County
  • Wood County suburbs including Perrysburg and Rossford
  • Erie County including Sandusky and Huron
  • southeast Michigan

Construction evidence

  • On 2023-03-30, Buckeye said construction had begun on a 270-mile fiber build in Huron and Sandusky, both in Erie County, with year-end 2023 completion targeted.
  • Its current construction page says Buckeye is rebuilding its entire cable footprint to FTTH over ten years and expects to build hundreds of fiber miles each year. This is a program target, not evidence that the full footprint is already FTTH.

Reported scale and date

  • 270 route miles of fiber construction in Huron and Sandusky announced 2023-03-30.
  • Ten-year full-footprint FTTH rebuild and hundreds of fiber miles per year on the current construction page accessed 2026-09-20; no current passing count was published in reviewed primary sources.

Bascom Communications

Cooperative / municipal partner | P2: selective

Current ownership

Locally headquartered telecommunications cooperative. The operating perimeter includes Bascom Mutual Telephone Company and related subsidiaries.

Commercial fit (inference)

Direct local threat around Omni's Tiffin and Fostoria markets and a rural construction benchmark. Bascom's cooperative pole-access relationship and public support can make it a valuable interconnection or wholesale partner, but its governance reduces confidence in a conventional whole-company acquisition thesis.

Possible transaction or partnership

Prefer partnership, transport, pole-access, joint build, or selective asset arrangement. A cooperative conversion or sale would require governance and member diligence; no availability evidence was found.

Constraints

The 1,400 figure is homes and businesses targeted by the project, not subscribers. The 238 figure is addresses passed, not connected customers. Project completion at 2026-06-30 was not independently verified.

Countercase

Small rural density and grant dependence may limit standalone returns. Bascom's local governance and seven-county mixed fiber and wireless perimeter may make integration more complex than the map suggests.

Geographic clusters

  • Seneca County
  • northwest Crawford County
  • parts of Hancock, Wood, Sandusky, Huron, and Wyandot Counties

Construction evidence

  • Bascom's October 2025 project update said Phase 1 of its North Central Electric partnership included 148 miles of new fiber, was scheduled for completion by 2026-06-30, and would deliver fiber to more than 1,400 homes and businesses. It reported 238 addresses passed and another 200 Attica-area locations expected by the end of September 2025.
  • The same page describes an estimated $26 million broader project supported in part by a $3 million BroadbandOhio grant. Bascom's 2026 FAQ describes the build as connecting North Central Electric members without fiber and expanding Bascom's footprint in Seneca County and surrounding counties.

Reported scale and date

  • 148 miles of new fiber in Phase 1 and more than 1,400 target homes and businesses, October 2025 update.
  • 238 addresses passed at the October 2025 update, plus 200 Attica-area locations forecast for completion by September 2025. The page's timing should be refreshed because the update was posted after the stated month-end target.
  • $26 million estimated broader project and $3 million BroadbandOhio grant, as reported on the company project page.

NKTelco

Owned operating brand | P3: partner / monitor

Current ownership

Part of family-owned Schurz Communications' broadband portfolio since 2021.

Commercial fit (inference)

Useful west-central Ohio adjacency and operating benchmark near Omni's Greenville and Eaton markets. Schurz ownership also identifies a sophisticated regional broadband owner that could partner, compete, or evaluate a future combination, but it is not evidence NKTelco itself is available.

Possible transaction or partnership

Discuss a specifically defined Schurz-owned operating or wholesale perimeter; NKTelco is not an independent owner-led seller.

Constraints

The more-than-3,000 figure aggregates several recent western Ohio projects and is not a total company passing count. The 1,600 Versailles wording mixes businesses and residents and should not be converted into homes passed.

Countercase

Current evidence points to continued owner-backed investment, not an exit. Its footprint is smaller and farther west than Omni's densest northern clusters, so strategic fit may depend on exact network adjacency.

Geographic clusters

  • Auglaize County
  • Mercer County
  • Shelby County
  • Miami County
  • Darke County
  • Marysville and Union County network lease

Construction evidence

  • On 2026-02-18, NKTelco said it had completed FTTH throughout Arcanum in Darke County and that recent western Ohio projects had expanded service to more than 3,000 homes and businesses in predominantly rural communities.
  • On 2024-10-01, NKTelco reported completion of a $2 million fiber expansion in Versailles, Darke County, making service available to more than 1,600 businesses and residents. The unit is company-reported premises or people language, not a formal subscriber count.

Reported scale and date

  • More than 3,000 homes and businesses reached by several recent western Ohio fiber investments as of 2026-02-18.
  • More than 1,600 businesses and residents reached by the completed Versailles build as of 2024-10-01, within a stated $2 million investment.
  • Five named west-central Ohio counties served as of 2025-07-15.
