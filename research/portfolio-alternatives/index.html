Public portfolio alternatives: Omni operating base versus hypothetical Omni + Socket + IdeaTek

As of September 20, 2026. Public sources only. Case A is Omni Fiber’s confirmed operating base, including integrated Lit operations. Citizens is an announced extension with final regulatory approval and legal closing unresolved, and is excluded from that confirmed base. Its potential post-close contribution is considered separately. Case B is an analytical scenario that combines Omni with the existing Socket and IdeaTek platform. No reviewed source announces that combination, a sale process, or willingness by any owner to transact. Homes, premises, locations, communities, customers, subscribers and fiber miles retain their reported definitions. Forward targets are not actuals, and incompatible figures are not summed.

Citizens status as of September 20, 2026. Omni announced the transaction on June 1, 2026. A July 11 Pennsylvania transfer-of-control notice shows applications for approval; no final approval or consummation notice was located. Final legal closing remains unresolved in the reviewed public record.

Decision view

Case A is the cleaner near-term platform case. It concentrates management and capital on optimizing the integrated Lit operations, completing current builds and creating density in western Pennsylvania. Citizens integration would follow a verified closing. Case B adds meaningful reported scale and a Missouri-Kansas operating core, but it also introduces Pamlico, TPG Peppertree and founder interests, more brands and systems, active expansion obligations, and carrier-channel questions. Public evidence does not yet show that the expanded state map forms a continuous or economically integrated network.

The key ownership correction is decisive. IdeaTek is no longer accurately described as merely Kansas-owned with no institutional investor. In May 2025, the FCC granted transfer of IdeaTek control to Last Dance Intermediate III and said its upstream parent was primarily owned and controlled by Oak Hill- and Pamlico-affiliated funds. Oak Hill's July 2025 release says TPG Peppertree sold its existing stake and reinvested from a newer fund alongside Oak Hill and Pamlico. Socket and IdeaTek were combined as a Midwest platform while retaining distinct brands and local management. [FCC; Oak Hill]

A. Omni confirmed operating base

Oak Hill formed Omni in 2022 with a $250 million commitment. Omni and Lit Fiber announced their merger in November 2024, and Brownsville's fiscal 2025 financial statements describe Omni as the surviving entity without a change to the city's contractual arrangements. Omni announced the Citizens Fiber transaction in June 2026; final regulatory approval and legal closing remain unresolved. The Citizens announcement identifies a residential and business footprint in Westmoreland County but discloses no price, subscribers, passings, route miles, revenue or EBITDA. [Oak Hill; Omni-Lit; Citizens; Brownsville ACFR, Note24 printed157-158 / PDF164-165]

The strongest public strategy is western Pennsylvania densification. Omni lists live or developing markets across Mercer, Lawrence and Fayette clusters, proposes to add Citizens in Westmoreland subject to the unresolved final close, and announced nearly 200,000 additional locations across Fayette, Indiana, Lawrence, Mercer, Washington and Westmoreland counties. This is a plan, not completed construction. The investment case requires address-level proof that the builds share transport, crews, marketing and support economics. [Omni service areas; Citizens release]

Lit contributes a more differentiated capability than ordinary retail FTTH. Brownsville combines a city-funded middle mile, private last-mile investment and an operator role that includes maintenance, operations and wholesale commercialization. That pattern could support municipal origination and carrier-ready infrastructure. It does not prove that every Lit advisory engagement transferred to Omni or that the contracts produce attractive returns. It also does not prove installed MDU units, anchor carriers or contracted wholesale revenue.

Omni reported about 60 communities with customers and about 25 under construction in December 2025, while targeting about 340,000 locations by year-end. By June 2026 it said more than $500 million had been invested. The later release does not reconcile whether the 340,000-location target was achieved. The case is falsified if actual passings, take rate and margins materially lag underwriting, the proposed Citizens addition supplies little economical adjacency after a verified close, or key Lit rights did not transfer or cannot be assigned. [December 2025 release; June 2026 release]

B. Hypothetical Omni + Socket + IdeaTek

Socket and IdeaTek should be treated as one sponsor-coordinated platform, not two independent targets. Oak Hill and Pamlico announced their investments on July 8, 2025, appointed Jim Gleason executive chairman of the combined company, and retained the two local brands and management teams. The companies target more than 500,000 Midwest homes passed by 2028. That target is neither an actual nor additive to Omni's location targets. [Socket release; IdeaTek release]

Socket's current website says it delivers fiber to more than 175,000 homes across three states and lists communities in Missouri, Illinois and Tennessee. It separately dates more than 45,000 customers to 2025 and says 75,000 new homes in Missouri and Illinois gained access in 2026. Those are different units. Socket also acquired Fastwyre's Missouri network. The signing release described 16 markets serving more than 25,000 households; transaction adviser Houlihan Lokey says the deal closed April 30, 2026. Fastwyre Missouri is therefore part of Socket's current perimeter, not a separate opportunity. Socket called those service areas contiguous to existing communities, supporting Missouri density but not adjacency to Omni. [Socket About; Fastwyre signing; closing]

IdeaTek's current page reports 75,000-plus customers, 120-plus communities and 8,500-plus fiber miles. Its service page is Kansas-heavy but lists Hooker and Miami, Oklahoma. It closed the Velocity acquisition in March 2026, adding fiber and fixed wireless in south-central Kansas, and announced a $300 million Tucson build in July. The same public page says IdeaTek sold its earlier fiber-to-tower business and cross-state route in 2015, so that historic backbone cannot be attributed to today's company. [IdeaTek About; service areas; Velocity; Tucson]

Public scale reporting needs reconciliation before any combination case is underwritten. Pamlico's current portfolio page says Socket and IdeaTek together serve more than 65,000 residential and commercial subscribers, while IdeaTek's current page alone says more than 75,000 customers. These are lower bounds, so they do not mathematically contradict each other. Their dates and customer definitions still require reconciliation before using them as comparable scale measures. No reliable combined subscriber total follows from the public record. [Pamlico; IdeaTek]

The strategic upside is broader than state count. Socket adds local construction, locating, business services and a denser Missouri network. IdeaTek adds rural fiber, fixed wireless and subsidy-program experience. A combined group could test shared procurement, engineering standards, NOC functions, carrier coverage and construction resources. The public record does not establish route adjacency from Kansas and Missouri to Omni's Ohio/Pennsylvania/Michigan clusters or Brownsville. State presence alone cannot support network synergy.

The operating burden is material. Socket and IdeaTek remain distinct brands and local teams even within their present platform. Adding Omni creates at least three operating brands, different products and likely different billing, OSS/BSS, plant standards and field models. Socket/IdeaTek's 2028 target, Fastwyre integration, IdeaTek's Tucson plan and Omni's ongoing builds compete for the same capital and leadership attention. Case B fails if a reconciled route and customer bridge shows limited incremental density, owner or lender consents are costly, system conversion overwhelms savings, or existing construction consumes integration capacity.

Carrier angle and conflict

There are confirmed sponsor and carrier precedents, but none includes Omni, Socket or IdeaTek. T-Mobile and Oak Hill announced a definitive 50/50 JV for GoNetspeed and Greenlight, expected to close in the first half of 2027. T-Mobile separately announced a definitive 50/50 JV with Wren House for i3 Broadband, expected to close in the second half of 2026. The release says the GoNetspeed/Greenlight footprint includes Pennsylvania and i3's current markets include Missouri and Illinois. It also describes wholesale models in which T-Mobile acquires substantially all existing residential fiber customers. [T-Mobile]

This precedent shows that carrier-backed customer separation is executable. It also creates potential conflict for Case B. Socket's Missouri and Illinois markets may overlap i3, and Omni's Pennsylvania markets may overlap GoNetspeed/Greenlight. Public state lists do not establish address overlap, exclusivity or a contractual conflict. The decisive work is address-level mapping and review of customer ownership, wholesale pricing, minimum volumes, build obligations and channel restrictions. Common Oak Hill sponsorship is not evidence that T-Mobile wants either case.

IdeaTek's July 21, 2026 Tucson release reports 240,000 premises and more than 130 Kansas communities. These are company-reported premises, not subscribers. The $300 million Tucson plan is a prospective investment requirement, not proof of completed service or a fully contracted liability. IdeaTek Tucson release

A continuous owned route is not necessary to create procurement, systems or distribution benefits. The combination test is incremental value after transport, integration, governance and funding costs, compared with each platform's standalone plan.

Missing inputs and falsification tests

Both cases require the same core evidence before a valuation or financing view: same-date actual passings and connected customers; address and route overlap; owned versus leased fiber; take rate, churn, revenue, EBITDA, maintenance capex and free cash flow; debt and undrawn capacity; grants and change-of-control obligations; OSS/BSS and plant standards; and carrier/MDU contracts with pricing and exclusivity.

Case A is falsified by weak western Pennsylvania adjacency, missed build economics, non-transferable Lit rights or immaterial carrier and MDU demand. Case B is falsified by limited route synergy, incompatible owners or covenants, expensive systems conversion, conflicted carrier channels, or too much simultaneous build and integration risk. Until those tests are passed, Case A is the more supportable public strategy and Case B remains a scale option rather than an actionable combination thesis.

Public facts retain their disclosed dates and units. Analytical deal structures do not establish seller willingness or buyer interest.
