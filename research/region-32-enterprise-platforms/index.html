Uniti Group Inc. (post-Windstream perimeter)

Scaled strategic platform | P1: investigate

Current ownership

Public fact: the merger closed on 2025-08-01. Legacy Uniti and Windstream became indirect wholly owned subsidiaries of the listed parent, Uniti Group Inc. Legacy Uniti holders received approximately 62% of the combined common stock at closing and legacy Windstream holders approximately 38%, before convertible securities and warrants. Uniti trades on Nasdaq as UNIT. This is a public-company control situation, not a sponsor exit. [S1, S2]

Commercial fit (inference)

Analytical inference: Uniti is the most capable national wholesale counterpart and the least credible conventional bolt-on. A combined RightFiber/Great Plains platform could explore reciprocal transport, wavelength, dark-fiber, colocation, or customer-backed joint builds where actual endpoints justify them. Uniti's Tier 2 and Tier 3 strategy and Midwest/Southeast access may be commercially adjacent, but no route overlap or customer synergy is established here. The post-merger company reported no single customer or related group at 10% or more of 2025 consolidated revenue, which reduces the old Windstream concentration issue at the consolidated level, but segment-level and contract-level concentration remain undisclosed. [S2]

Possible transaction or partnership

Priority route: wholesale or customer-backed joint build first. Second route: a narrow asset carveout only if Uniti identifies non-core assets and title, debt collateral, easements, operating systems, customers, and transition services can be separated. Management said on 2026-07-30 that it may monetize non-core assets, but did not identify a process or perimeter. Full acquisition would require public-company control, refinancing or assumption of a highly levered capital structure, and integration of consumer, managed-services, copper, wholesale, and enterprise operations. It is a transformational transaction far beyond a normal regional add-on. [S3]

Constraints

Public fact: Q2 2026 net loss was $155.9 million, notes and other debt were $10.65 billion net at 2026-06-30, and 2026 interest-expense guidance was $805 million. Uniti is simultaneously funding Kinetic fiber construction and Fiber Infrastructure expansion and had used nearly $3 billion of asset securitizations by July 2026. After quarter-end, the issuers accepted $480.236 million aggregate principal amount of secured notes for purchase under asset sale offers on 2026-08-21; this is a debt-reduction event, not evidence that a new operating-asset sale process is open. Indebtedness can constrain flexibility, while securitization, debt repayment, and possible asset monetization expand financing options. Any carveout must clear debt documents and ABS collateral boundaries. Customer, ROW, pole, IRU, lease, and regulatory transferability are unknown until diligence. [S3, S16]

Countercase

Countercase: scale is also the strategic argument. Uniti owns, operates, or leases a nationwide network, offers 10 to 400 Gbps waves, spectrum, dark fiber, Ethernet, internet, colocation, enterprise managed networking, and wireless backhaul, and has no disclosed consolidated customer above 10% of revenue after the merger. If a buyer wanted a national platform rather than a regional adjacency, Uniti would be uniquely broad. That thesis still has to overcome leverage, public-company execution, integration risk, copper and legacy-service exposure, and the absence of a verified sale process. [S2, S3]

Geographic clusters

  • National wholesale backbone and optical transport, Kinetic local access concentrated in the Midwest and Southeast, and enterprise fiber markets across the Southeast. [S2]
  • Current named growth nodes include Jackson, Birmingham, Huntsville, Montgomery, Tulsa, and South-Central long-haul routes. In 2026 Uniti disclosed a 69-route-mile Birmingham metro build, a Tulsa 50-rack colocation agreement, and a broader 1,100-route-mile South-Central expansion or upgrade program. The 1,100 miles are an announced program, not all confirmed completed miles. [S4]

Construction evidence

  • Public fact: Uniti is executing customer-aligned high-count long-haul, metro-lateral, dark-fiber, optical-wave, and colocation expansion. At 2026 Q2, Fiber Infrastructure recorded about $2.2 million of new monthly recurring revenue bookings, its reported record, and $77.2 million of quarterly capital expenditure before $9.7 million of customer upfront payments. The company also disclosed a 20-year, $500 million customer contract in May 2026, without naming the customer. These facts show active build and contracting, not asset availability. [S3, S4]

Reported scale and date

  • Public fact: at 2025-12-31 Uniti reported approximately 240,000 fiber route miles, 11.2 million fiber strand miles, 282,000 copper route miles, more than 1.0 million customers, more than 500,000 residential fiber customers, and approximately 1.9 million fiber-equipped households. The property disclosure says the group 'own[s] or lease[s]' the fiber miles and does not publish an owned-versus-leased route-mile split. Operating lease liabilities were $483.1 million at 2025-12-31, but that balance is not a fiber-mile measure. Uniti sells or grants dark fiber through leases and IRUs and also operates owned builds. A data-center count is not disclosed; the 10-K instead describes on-net colocation facilities and key data-center corridors. [S2] At 2026-06-30 the company reported $12.98 billion of assets, $608.9 million of cash, and $10.65 billion of notes and other debt, net. [S3]

FirstLight Fiber / Antin Infrastructure Partners

Scaled strategic platform | P1: investigate

Current ownership

Public fact: Antin agreed to acquire FirstLight from Oak Hill on 2018-02-21, with closing then expected in the second half of 2018. Antin's 2025 Universal Registration Document still lists FirstLight in Flagship Fund III and III-B, investment year 2018, confirming the current fund perimeter at 2025-12-31. FirstLight is private. No reviewed primary source announced a current sale process. [S8, S9]

Commercial fit (inference)

Analytical inference: FirstLight could add a coherent Northeast enterprise, carrier, dark-fiber, cloud, managed-services, and colocation region. For a combined RightFiber/Great Plains platform, that is a geographic extension rather than a proven infill. A partnership could provide Northeast reach and Canadian gateways without acquiring a large, noncontiguous operating company. More than 15,000 businesses reportedly rely on FirstLight, but private-company revenue, EBITDA, top-customer concentration, churn, and contract tenure are not disclosed. The named hyperscaler award proves capability, not diversification or concentration. [S5, S7]

Possible transaction or partnership

Priority route: wholesale interconnection, resale, protected wave, or route-specific dark-fiber cooperation tied to customer demand. Full acquisition is conceivable only as a sponsor-led exit from Antin Fund III, but holding vintage is not evidence of availability. It would be a large platform acquisition with six-state operations, data centers, cloud, voice, unified communications, security, and managed services, not an easy bolt-on. Asset carveout is less natural because FirstLight describes an integrated owned and operated network. A route or data-center carveout would require proof that customers, transport, regeneration sites, systems, employees, and permits can be separated without damaging the remaining network. No such public offer was found. [S5, S8, S9]

Constraints

Unknown: FirstLight does not publish debt, liquidity, capex, EBITDA, leverage, or fund-level asset valuation. Antin's funds are closed-ended and FirstLight has been held since 2018, which creates eventual monetization logic as a general fund-life inference, but no exit timetable or process is public. The current 20,000-versus-25,000 route-mile conflict is a diligence issue. Other diligence priorities are owned-versus-IRU routes, data-center title and power commitments, customer concentration, carrier and hyperscaler contract terms, pension and labor exposure, network rationalization, and the capital required for high-count route upgrades.

Countercase

Countercase: the platform is operationally coherent, already sells the full enterprise and wholesale stack, has a dense six-state route system, more than 16,000 stated on-net locations, and a tangible data-center estate. Those attributes could justify full-platform value for a buyer deliberately pursuing a multi-region strategy. The contrary evidence is geographic separation, unreported leverage and earnings, inconsistent public scale data, a mature sponsor owner that may require a full value realization, and no public indication that a whole-company or asset sale is available. [S5, S7, S8]

Geographic clusters

  • Dense Northeast and northern Mid-Atlantic corridors, including Albany-Boston, New York State, northern New England, Pennsylvania, and Canadian connectivity. Albany is described as a regeneration and colocation point on the 240-route-mile Albany-Boston hyperscaler route announced 2026-09-17. [S5]
  • The current data-center page names five featured locations and nine additional location names, with Manchester appearing in both lists. It therefore supports 13 unique named locations, not a clean 14-facility count, and does not disclose ownership versus leased colocation at every site. [S7]

Construction evidence

  • Public fact: FirstLight announced a major hyperscaler agreement on 2026-09-17 for a 240-route-mile high-fiber-count Albany-Boston route. The company completed a Brunswick, Maine data-center expansion in February 2025 and selected a new network system of record in August 2026. These are operating and modernization signals. They do not establish that FirstLight is for sale or that a particular route is separable. [S5, S7]

Reported scale and date

  • Material conflict: the newest dated company release says 20,000 route miles across six states and the current site footer says the same, connecting more than 16,000 on-net locations. A 2025 company white paper and multiple current data-center cards instead say approximately 25,000 route miles; the white paper adds nearly 15,000 lit locations and access to another 125,000 and calls the network exclusively owned and operated. [S5, S6, S7] Use 20,000 as the latest dated stated perimeter, but do not treat the discrepancy as resolved. The 'exclusively owned and operated' claim is marketing-level evidence, not a route-by-route title schedule, and no leased fiber, IRU, pole, conduit, or facility mix is published. Featured data-center sizes range from 3,260 square feet at Rockland to 52,000 square feet at Brunswick; a total facility-area figure is not disclosed. [S7]

SummitIG

Scaled strategic platform | P1: investigate

Current ownership

Public fact: SDC Capital Partners has been SummitIG's majority owner since 2019, reaffirmed in the 2026 DF&I acquisition release. In 2021 Harrison Street acquired a significant minority interest and Columbia Capital retained a minority interest. The latest primary release does not restate those minority positions, so their current percentages and continuing status are unknown. SDC's current portfolio page labels SummitIG 'Partially Realized' while still listing it as an investment. That label is consistent with prior partial monetization but is not a current sale-process announcement. [S10, S13, S14]

Commercial fit (inference)

Analytical inference: SummitIG is the strongest specialist route to data-center and hyperscale ecosystems, but the weakest conventional enterprise bolt-on. Columbus and Chicago could be commercially relevant to a broader Midwestern strategy, yet no location overlap with Great Plains or RightFiber is established. A customer-backed dark-fiber, metro cross-connect, or joint-build relationship could let the combined platform connect enterprise customers into SummitIG's data-center routes while preserving each operator's core. SummitIG's custom hyperscale focus creates possible customer-concentration risk, but neither concentration nor contract economics are public. That risk must be labeled unknown, not assumed high. [S10, S11]

Possible transaction or partnership

Priority route: route-specific wholesale, IRU, lateral, or joint-build cooperation where named customer endpoints create demand. Full acquisition would require negotiating with a majority sponsor and potentially multiple minority holders, funding a scaled data-center fiber platform, and absorbing active construction and DF&I integration. It is not an easy bolt-on. Asset carveout is strategically counter to SummitIG's current density and corridor-integration program unless an owner affirmatively offers a non-core geography. The SierraIG precedent proves SummitIG will use a JV structure, but it does not imply willingness to form a U.S. JV with these buyers. No public sale process was found. [S10, S13, S15]

Constraints

Unknown: private-company debt, cash, leverage, EBITDA, maintenance capex, committed build capital, and customer concentration. Public evidence points to capital access, not unlimited capacity: SDC reported approximately $12 billion of AUM in the June 2026 release, the platform has institutional minority backing, and it completed DF&I while funding multi-market expansion. Constraints include integrating acquired records and customers, finishing committed construction, permit and ROW execution, serving high-count custom contracts, and avoiding stranded speculative routes. Minority-holder rights and SDC's 'Partially Realized' designation require direct diligence. [S10, S14]

Countercase

Countercase: SummitIG's owner-operator model, underground high-count infrastructure, Northern Virginia density, Columbus build, Chicago expansion, and DF&I corridor can be more valuable as a specialist partner or standalone platform than as part of a regional enterprise operator. Recent acquisition activity and active expansion are evidence of a growth posture, not a divestiture posture. Conversely, SDC's partial realization history means a future ownership event is plausible in principle. Neither point establishes current availability, valuation, or process. [S10, S11, S14]

Geographic clusters

  • Northern Virginia is the anchor and densest cluster. SummitIG reported more than 1,100 miles of Virginia infrastructure before integrating nearly 200 miles of DF&I conduit and dark fiber, including nearly 60 miles of distinct Maryland reach to Baltimore, on 2026-06-30. [S10]
  • The current company site reports more than 1,200 operational miles across Northern Virginia, Greater Virginia, Columbus, Chicago, and other new markets. It separately states more than 200 newly constructed miles in Columbus, more than 175 long-haul miles from Northern Virginia toward Richmond, and more than 60 Chicago miles with 100 miles under development. The site does not provide a reliable numeric data-center or on-net-building count in accessible static text. [S11, S12]

Construction evidence

  • Public fact: SummitIG completed the acquisition of DF&I from Blue Owl-managed funds on 2026-06-30 and said it was integrating customers, operations, network records, and remaining active construction. It also described rapid expansion across five core U.S. markets. The network is purpose-built for data-center, hyperscaler, cloud, carrier, large-enterprise, and government connectivity. [S10] The current site calls the more than 1,200-mile network newly built, high-count, 100% underground, fully owned, operated, and maintained by SummitIG. [S11] Those claims support control of the physical network but do not disclose conduit leases, ROW terms, laterals, or customer IRUs.

Reported scale and date

  • Public fact: current company materials state more than 1,200 operational route miles, 100% underground. The 2026-06-30 transaction release instead says the pre-acquisition Virginia footprint exceeded 1,100 miles and DF&I added nearly 200 miles of conduit and dark-fiber assets. These figures should not be added mechanically because geography, operating status, and overlap are not reconciled. [S10, S11] The disclosed product is predominantly dark fiber and custom infrastructure rather than a broad lit enterprise suite. No revenue, EBITDA, capex, debt, customer count, fiber-pair inventory, route utilization, or data-center count is publicly disclosed. SierraIG's 750 km target and $50 million investment target remain announced plans from 2024. [S15]
