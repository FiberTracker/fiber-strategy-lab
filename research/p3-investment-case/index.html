Lit's municipal model: a useful operating capability with a contract-specific return

The strongest version of the P3 pitch is an operator that can develop last-mile service and commercialize public infrastructure. Brownsville provides evidence of live service and municipal economic participation. The investable asset is the private network and its contractual rights, with public ownership, fees and obligations explicitly separated.

What the contracts change

Brownsville's FY2025 financial report distinguishes a 20-year maintenance agreement from an initial five-year operating agreement. Each has up to three successive ten-year renewal options. The report does not resolve who controls renewal or its conditions. An underwriter therefore cannot substitute the maintenance term or optional renewals for secured operating revenue duration. Brownsville ACFR, Note 24, printed pages 157–158, PDF pages 164–165

The same note authorizes custom wholesale commercialization and records $61,995 of FY2025 middle-mile user fees. This is a municipal disclosure, not Omni revenue, EBITDA or a recurring run rate. A July2026 city statement separately says Omni shares revenue with the city per connected customer. The fee allocation, sharing formula and retained operator economics need reconciliation before assigning a value to the rights. ACFR City partnership update

Brownsville describes city middle-mile assets and private last-mile investment. Medina explicitly identifies its Port Authority as middle-mile owner. Ownership of a public network must not become assumed Omni collateral because Omni maintains or commercializes it. Executed agreements, acceptance documents, title schedules and assignment consents determine the transferable perimeter. Medina announcement Brownsville update

Operational evidence and remaining work

Brownsville announced its first Lit customer in August2024. Its FY2025 report says the arrangements and obligations continued when Lit merged into Omni. That supports continuity for this specific relationship. It does not establish that every Lit advisory engagement, municipal contract or planned network transferred into the same legal perimeter. First customer ACFR

The July2026 city update describes approximately $20million of public investment, more than 100miles of middle mile and approximately $80million of private last-mile investment. These later approximate descriptions differ from the earlier financial report and should remain dated project statements. Neither the investment narrative nor the first-customer milestone proves every planned last-mile address is complete. City update

Medina's November2019 first-phase release announced three communities and approximately $8million of secured phase-one funding. A further $50million for county expansion was prospective. Use the release to establish the development model; require subsequent construction and operating evidence for completed scale. Medina announcement

The carrier proposal

Offer selected municipal markets as a carrier access and development package. Price the carrier's customer rights and wholesale service separately from private plant, public-network access and future builds. The operator may contribute local execution and municipal relationships; the carrier may contribute distribution and contracted demand. Neither contribution guarantees a return without the actual wholesale tariff, service-level obligations and minimum commitments.

The proposal fails if municipal revenue sharing or maintenance consumes the available margin, renewal is outside operator control, assignment is unavailable, or the carrier can obtain equivalent coverage more cheaply. The first diligence request is a contract-and-asset schedule, followed by monthly retained cash flow, completion capex and consent requirements for each market.

Public facts retain their disclosed dates and units. Analytical deal structures do not establish seller willingness or buyer interest.
