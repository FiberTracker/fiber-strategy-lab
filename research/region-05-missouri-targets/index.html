Bluebird Fiber

Scaled strategic platform | P1: investigate

Current ownership

Macquarie Infrastructure Partners-backed. Completed acquisition of substantially all Everstream assets and operations on 2026-03-06; no current sale announcement found.

Commercial fit (inference)

Highest strategic corridor value. Adds business-only enterprise, carrier, dark-fiber, wave and data-center capabilities and alternate Missouri paths beyond GPC's Kansas City-St. Louis route. It is transformational, not a bolt-on.

Possible transaction or partnership

Priority commercial route is reciprocal wholesale, protected waves, dark fiber or an IRU. An acquisition would require a sponsor-to-sponsor, platform-scale discussion with Macquarie after the Everstream integration.

Constraints

Bluebird reports more than 36,000 route miles. Rightfiber reports a 28,000-mile fiber network without defining route versus strand miles, so the two figures are not directly comparable. Everstream integration is fresh; asset ownership includes a historical OpCo/PropCo structure; route overlap and lease obligations require diligence.

Countercase

Bluebird may create more overlap and integration risk than incremental last-mile reach, and Macquarie may prefer to harvest the Everstream combination independently.

Geographic clusters

  • Kansas City
  • St. Louis
  • Columbia and Jefferson City
  • Rolla
  • Springfield
  • Carthage and Joplin
  • southeast Missouri
  • multi-state Midwest corridors

Construction evidence

  • Completed Everstream asset purchase in March 2026. Separately, its awarded Missouri NTIA middle-mile design covers 325 miles across 21 counties, plus a Missouri-Oklahoma route; the award and RFP prove planned funded routes, not completed construction.

Reported scale and date

  • More than 36,000 fiber route miles, more than 400,000 on-net or near-net buildings, and more than 450 POPs in 12 states, company-reported 2026-03-06. The 325-mile Missouri NTIA project is an awarded/planned route perimeter, not operating miles.

Socket / IdeaTek Midwest platform

Scaled strategic platform | P2: selective

Current ownership

Oak Hill and Pamlico-backed Midwest platform; TPG Peppertree reinvested alongside them in 2025. Socket and IdeaTek retain distinct brands and local management. Socket acquired Fastwyre Missouri in April2026.

Commercial fit (inference)

The Missouri-Kansas platform offers residential scale and a hypothetical combination with Omni. Physical connection, incremental customers and enterprise corridor synergies require route-level diligence; state presence does not establish them.

Possible transaction or partnership

Monitor for sponsor-level strategic combination, selected market swaps or reciprocal wholesale. Near-term whole-company acquisition is unlikely while Oak Hill and Pamlico fund growth and integrate Fastwyre.

Constraints

Active sponsor-backed consolidator, current Fastwyre integration, potentially meaningful overlap, and a stated 2028 build target. Exact Missouri passings, subscribers, churn and fiber-versus-legacy plant for acquired exchanges are undisclosed. IdeaTek is already part of the Socket platform, not an independent Kansas-owned seller.

Countercase

Socket may be a better-funded Missouri competitor than a seller, and buying overlapping FTTH could destroy value if penetration is low or upgrade capital remains high.

  • Moberly and Mexico
  • Hannibal and Kirksville
  • St. Louis western suburbs
  • Sedalia and Warrensburg
  • Nevada
  • Hickory County
  • Webb City and southwest Missouri
  • central Kansas
  • Wichita region
  • Dodge City
  • Garden City
  • statewide Freestate corridor

Construction evidence

  • Socket passed more than 100,000 premises as of 2025-07-08 and announced expansion into Webb City, Odessa, Eureka, Nevada and Harrisonville. The announced Fastwyre Missouri perimeter added 16 markets; current Socket pages list former Fastwyre towns as Socket service areas.
  • IdeaTek: Reports 8,500-plus fiber miles and 120-plus communities on an undated current company page accessed 2026-09-20; route-versus-strand basis is not disclosed.
  • IdeaTek: Partner on the planned state-owned Freestate open-access network, described by IdeaTek on 2025-06-06 and by NTIA's current award page accessed 2026-09-20.

Reported scale and date

  • Socket reported more than 100,000 premises passed on 2025-07-08. Socket and IdeaTek target more than 500,000 homes passed by 2028. The acquired Fastwyre Missouri business was described at signing as 16 markets serving more than 25,000 households; this is not a verified subscriber count.
  • IdeaTek: 75,000-plus customers, current page accessed 2026-09-20
  • IdeaTek: 8,500-plus fiber miles, undated current company page accessed 2026-09-20; route-versus-strand basis unspecified.
  • Kansas state-owned Freestate project: planned 682-mile open-access middle-mile project, described by IdeaTek on 2025-06-06 and on NTIA's current award page accessed 2026-09-20; $42.5 million federal award and $69.7 million total project cost; separate public asset, not IdeaTek-owned mileage.

Kingdom Networks, formerly Kingdom Telephone and Phynx Fiber

Cooperative / municipal partner | P2: selective

Current ownership

Member-owned Missouri telephone cooperative. The 2025 Kingdom Networks change unified brands under the same cooperative board and team; no sale or outside-control transaction found.

Commercial fit (inference)

Compact central-Missouri FTTH adjacency to Socket and statewide transport routes. Attractive for backhaul aggregation and rural expansion, but more a cooperative partner than a conventional bolt-on.

Possible transaction or partnership

Pursue backhaul, managed core, construction support, shared grant bids or long-term transport. Any merger would require cooperative governance and likely regulatory and USDA consent.

Constraints

Member ownership, regulated ILEC perimeter, active USDA grant obligations and incomplete public subscriber economics.

Countercase

Small scale and grant restrictions may make direct ownership uneconomic; its best strategic value may be traffic and route density rather than acquired EBITDA.

Geographic clusters

  • Auxvasse
  • Fulton and Callaway County
  • Mexico and Audrain County
  • Montgomery City
  • Centralia
  • Moberly and Randolph County
  • rural Boone County

Construction evidence

  • USDA awarded $23.007 million for a 464.7-square-mile FTTP project covering 1,955 households, 4,941 people, 611 farms and 84 businesses in Audrain, Boone, Callaway and Montgomery counties. A company update showed active ReConnect 3 construction and scheduled first-home activations in June 2025; it did not prove the full project complete.

Reported scale and date

  • ReConnect 3 funded service area: 1,955 households across 464.7 square miles, USDA award data dated 2022-10-27. Do not substitute people, farms, businesses or the company's later 'more than 2,200 locations' description for households.

Wisper Internet

Independent operator screen | P1: investigate

Current ownership

Latest located FCC disclosure dated 2024-10-22: Nathan Stooke 60% and E8 Topco 40%, with E8 supported by Digital Alpha funds. No later ownership change found.

Commercial fit (inference)

Useful rural demand aggregation and tower-backhaul partner with selective FTTH tuck-ins. It is not a clean fiber bolt-on because most geographic reach is fixed wireless and some backhaul is leased.

Possible transaction or partnership

Start with tower backhaul, wholesale internet, and selective fiber-area diligence. A control investment or fiber carve-out is more plausible than valuing the whole footprint as FTTH.

Constraints

Mixed FWA and FTTH technologies, CAF/RDOF obligations, leased backhaul, lender and minority-investor rights, and no Missouri-only subscriber or fiber-passings disclosure.

Countercase

BEAD-funded fiber overbuilds can pressure an FWA-heavy base, while universal-service obligations and tower replacement capex may absorb cash.

Geographic clusters

  • broad rural Missouri fixed-wireless coverage
  • Sedalia and Smithton fiber
  • Richland
  • St. Robert and Waynesville
  • Osage Beach
  • Joplin
  • selected eastern and northern Missouri areas

Construction evidence

  • Wisper reported more than 70 new towers across Missouri and Illinois over the prior year as of 2026-06-16. Its current construction page identifies live fiber in Cook Station, Richland, Sedalia, Smithton and St. Robert, with construction or cabinet work in Brumley and Waynesville.

Reported scale and date

  • More than 21,000 customers across six states, company-reported 2026-06-16. FCC's 2024 record described approximately 20,000 mixed residential, commercial and enterprise subscribers and 74,358 modified CAF II deployment obligations across all states. Neither figure is Missouri-only or fiber-only.

Gateway Fiber and WANRack

Scaled strategic platform | P2: selective

Current ownership

Majority-owned by a CBRE Investment Management fund. Gateway, WANRack and KWIKOM combined in 2025 and were operating under a unified Gateway platform by 2025-07-29.

Commercial fit (inference)

Adds eastern-Missouri FTTH plus an enterprise and E-Rate capability that Rightfiber could use for schools and municipalities. Geography is more complementary to GPC's St. Louis reach than to Rightfiber's Poplar Bluff entry.

Possible transaction or partnership

Wholesale, E-Rate teaming, reciprocal off-net access or a sponsor-level combination. A whole-company acquisition is unlikely immediately after the CBRE-backed merger.

Constraints

Recently integrated national platform, CBRE control, dispersed school-network assets that may not form a contiguous backbone, and no disclosed Missouri unit economics.

Countercase

National E-Rate scale can look larger than the physically interconnected Missouri network; not all WANRack fiber is useful for general carrier traffic.

Geographic clusters

  • St. Charles County
  • Lincoln County
  • Warren County
  • Franklin County
  • Wentzville
  • Troy
  • Warrenton
  • Washington
  • Union
  • national K-12 private WAN projects

Construction evidence

  • Gateway is a 100% FTTP builder for residential and commercial customers in eastern Missouri. WANRack adds private lit and dark fiber WANs for schools. The 2025 merger explicitly targeted more commercial and wholesale opportunities.

Reported scale and date

  • Combined platform operates in 25 states as described in the 2025 merger announcement. Missouri-specific route miles, passings and subscribers are not publicly disclosed in the cited sources.

i3 Broadband, including Big River Broadband and Circle Fiber

Committed or integrated platform | P3: partner / monitor

Current ownership

Wren House portfolio company. T-Mobile agreed on 2026-04-28 to invest about $700 million for 50% of a JV that will acquire i3 and substantially all existing residential fiber customers; latest primary disclosure located says closing expected in second half 2026, not yet confirmed.

Commercial fit (inference)

Strategically useful St. Louis and southeast-Missouri FTTH and commercial fiber, including direct relevance to Rightfiber's Poplar Bluff market. The pending T-Mobile wholesale JV makes future open-access collaboration plausible but acquisition unavailable.

Possible transaction or partnership

Defer M&A until the T-Mobile/Wren House closing is confirmed. Then pursue wholesale access, enterprise interconnection or a legacy Big River commercial-perimeter discussion if the JV focuses on residential customers.

Constraints

Pending change of control, likely integration restrictions, direct Poplar Bluff overlap, target-versus-actual ambiguity, and no public split between residential FTTH and Big River enterprise assets.

Countercase

T-Mobile's capital and retail channel may make i3 a stronger competitor, while a 50/50 JV can complicate governance and any carve-out.

Geographic clusters

  • west St. Louis County
  • St. Charles County
  • Cape Girardeau
  • Perryville
  • Ste. Genevieve
  • Farmington and St. Francois County
  • Dexter and Scott County
  • Poplar Bluff and Butler County

Construction evidence

  • i3 closed Big River and Circle Fiber on 2023-05-31 and announced an additional $100 million southeast-Missouri investment. It had separately announced a $300 million St. Louis and Metro East program in 2022. These are announced investment plans, not audited capex or completed passings.

Reported scale and date

  • Target of approximately 500,000 households passed by year-end 2026 across Missouri, Illinois and Rhode Island, per T-Mobile on 2026-04-28. This is a target, not actual passings. Missouri-specific subscribers and route miles are unknown.

Sho-Me Technologies

Cooperative / municipal partner | P1: investigate

Current ownership

Wholly owned subsidiary of Sho-Me Power Electric Cooperative; no acquisition or sale process found.

Commercial fit (inference)

Best actionable statewide diversity partner. Its rural rings and utility rights-of-way can connect Rightfiber FTTH markets to GPC's national backbone while providing paths distinct from conventional telco corridors.

Possible transaction or partnership

Pursue master transport, reciprocal interconnection, IRUs, protected Ethernet and waves, and dark fiber where available. Treat acquisition as off-strategy unless Sho-Me Power initiates a structural review.

Constraints

Cooperative strategic asset, utility operational requirements, dark fiber only in some areas, no residential base, and unknown spare-strand inventory or change-of-control flexibility.

Countercase

A wholesale agreement may add vendor dependence without exclusive economics, and utility-route diversity must be tested span by span against Rightfiber and GPC paths.

Geographic clusters

  • statewide rural Missouri
  • Kansas City
  • St. Louis
  • Columbia
  • Jefferson City
  • Rolla
  • Springfield
  • Joplin
  • Cape Girardeau
  • Poplar Bluff
  • Sedalia
  • Lake of the Ozarks
  • southeast Missouri

Construction evidence

  • Current company pages report an optical network built along electric transmission lines, continual expansion, ring topology, more than 120 POPs, and more than 200 Ethernet nodes. No dated recent-mile build figure was located.

Reported scale and date

  • More than 4,700 fiber route miles and more than 120 POPs, current company-reported live-page figures accessed 2026-09-20. Business and carrier transport only, not residential passings.
