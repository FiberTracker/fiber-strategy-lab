Rightfiber / GPC perimeter audit

As of: 2026-09-20

Decision

Group Ritter Communications and Great Plains Communications inside Rightfiber. Do not yet group Fastwyre Nebraska into the closed base. Grain and Rightfiber announced the Ritter/GPC combination closed on September 2, one day after the Nebraska PSC approved C-5716, the indirect transfer of Great Plains Communications LLC, Great Plains Broadband LLC and Great Plains Communications Long Distance LLC to Rightfiber Holdings LLC. [P]S1S3

Treat Rightfiber as the platform and go-forward brand, not as evidence that every regulated operating company has been renamed. GPC's website remains active and now says GPC is Rightfiber, while existing services and local support continue during systems integration. The public sources reviewed do not fully bridge Rightfiber Holdings, Great Plains Communications Holdings and the legacy Ritter holding entities. [P]S1S3

Fastwyre Nebraska remains a pending add-on as of September 20. Nebraska approved C-5709 on August 18 and required ongoing reporting. The FCC then granted HunTel Cablevision's international Section 214 transfer to GPC on September 18, but that same FCC action expressly says the separate domestic application in WC Docket 26-142 remains pending. GPC's news archive has no Fastwyre closing release through the as-of date. [P]S8S9S17

Closed Rightfiber perimeter and reported scale

The latest company closing release reports more than 300,000 FTTH passings, a 28,000-mile fiber network, more than 400 communities across 20 states, and a $1.6 billion credit facility led by Fifth Third Bank for continued organic growth and potential M&A. [P]S1

The units matter:

  • 300,000-plus is FTTH passings, not subscribers. The June announcement used the looser description of 300,000 homes and businesses, while the September closing release explicitly says FTTH passings. Use the later definition. [P]S1S2
  • 28,000 is company-reported network miles. The closing release does not define owned versus leased, or route miles versus strand miles. Do not refine the unit without supporting data. [P]S1
  • $1.6 billion is a credit facility, not equity, cash on hand or necessarily fully drawn debt. Terms, maturity, pricing, covenants, lender group and drawn balance are unknown. GPC previously announced a $500 million financing in December 2024, but no source reviewed explains whether the new facility refinances it. Do not add the two facilities into $2.1 billion of capital. [P]S1S18
  • The release says potential M&A, but does not link the facility to Fastwyre's purchase price or disclose that price. Any funding connection is inference, not fact.

Retail states versus 20-state network

The current Rightfiber consumer service page lists six currently served states: Arkansas, Kentucky, Louisiana, Missouri, Tennessee and Texas. That page reflects the legacy Ritter retail web estate, not the full corporate perimeter. It omits the GPC markets and also omits Oklahoma, which the June merger release included in Ritter's seven-state description. [P]S2S4

The June release describes local legacy operations in eleven unique states: Ritter in Arkansas, Tennessee, Texas, Missouri, Kentucky, Louisiana and Oklahoma; GPC in Colorado, Indiana, Iowa, Kentucky and Nebraska. Kentucky overlaps. By contrast, GPC's carrier page says its 20,000-plus-mile network reaches 13 states. A 2023 GPC primary source enumerated Colorado, Illinois, Indiana, Iowa, Kansas, Kentucky, Minnesota, Missouri, Nebraska, Ohio, South Dakota, Wisconsin and Wyoming. [P]S2S5S6

Therefore, the 20-state Rightfiber figure should be read as a broader network, enterprise, carrier and hyperscaler footprint, not as 20 retail FTTH states. The exact current 20-state list is unknown. The published legacy GPC and Ritter lists yield 18 unique states after Kentucky and Missouri overlap, so two additional states or a changed definition are not disclosed in the primary sources reviewed.

GPC acquisitions already inside Rightfiber

These are completed and already embedded in GPC's current network. Do not add their historical metrics to Rightfiber's 28,000 miles or 300,000-plus passings.

  • Pinpoint Network Solutions / Pinpoint Broadband: completed February 11, 2016 and announced February 17. It added 1,200 fiber miles, including 135 miles in greater Omaha. This makes the requested acquisition list non-exhaustive. [P]S16
  • InterCarrier Networks: completed August 6, 2019. ICN contributed 2,037 route miles across Illinois, Indiana, the St. Louis metro and Kentucky. It was carrier and enterprise transport infrastructure, not a retail-passings acquisition. [P]S12
  • Enhanced Telecommunications Corporation: completed September 17, 2020. ETC served more than 14,000 business, wholesale and residential customers in southeastern Indiana. Customers are not passings. [P]S13
  • USA Communications: legally completed September 30, 2021 and announced October 1. It added residential and business fiber assets in Nebraska and Colorado. Acquired miles, passings and customers were not disclosed; financial terms were undisclosed. [P]S14
  • Wood River Network: closed December 31, 2021 and announced January 4, 2022. The 10.6-mile Nebraska network was expected to enable service to approximately 500 homes and 60 businesses. Those premises were an addressable plan at closing, not verified passings or subscribers. [P]S15

Fastwyre Nebraska: separate, not closed

The May 19 announcement covered only Fastwyre's Nebraska business, not Fastwyre's multi-state company. The 26 named communities are Arlington, Bassett, Belden, Bellevue, Blair, Carroll, Emerson, Fort Calhoun, Fremont, Herman, Homer, Kennard, Laurel, Lyons, Macy, Meadow Grove, Newport, Oakland, Osmond, Pender, Rosalie, Tekamah, Thurston, Walthill, Wayne and Winnebago. GPC said it expected to welcome more than two dozen Nebraska employees and later upgrade and integrate the network. Those upgrades are targets, not completed construction. No target passings, subscribers, miles, revenue, EBITDA or consideration were disclosed. [P]S7

The legal perimeter is all outstanding equity in HunTel, giving GPC indirect control of Arlington Telephone Company, The Blair Telephone Company, Eastern Nebraska Telephone Company, Rock County Telephone Company and HunTel Cablevision Inc., each doing business as Fastwyre Broadband. [P]S8S9

The chronology is decisive:

  1. On April 1, the FCC granted domestic authority for an earlier proposed transfer of the Fastwyre Alaska and Nebraska licensees to Anacoco. That was authorization, not proof of closing. [P]S11
  2. On May 14, American Broadband and HunTel signed a separate stock purchase agreement with GPC. GPC announced it on May 19 as subject to customary conditions and regulatory approvals. [P]S7S9
  3. On July 14, Nebraska granted withdrawal of C-5687, the Anacoco application. Do not use C-5687 as evidence that GPC bought Fastwyre. [P]S10
  4. On August 18, Nebraska unanimously granted C-5709 with ongoing reporting. [P]S8
  5. On September 18, the FCC granted the international authorization to GPC, while expressly preserving the separate, pending domestic review in WC Docket 26-142. [P]S9

The September 2 Ritter/GPC merger is also separate. Because Fastwyre's domestic FCC approval was still pending after that date and the 300,000-passings / 28,000-mile figures are unchanged from the June Rightfiber announcement, the best-supported inference is that Fastwyre is excluded from those reported totals.

Commercial read-through

Rightfiber is a credible adjacent-market buy-and-build platform, with scale across FTTH and enterprise transport plus disclosed financing capacity. Fastwyre Nebraska fits strategically as a dense GPC adjacency, but the public record is not sufficient to underwrite economics. The next diligence gate is documentary: obtain the WC Docket 26-142 domestic grant and a company consummation notice before moving Fastwyre into the closed perimeter or changing Rightfiber's reported scale.
