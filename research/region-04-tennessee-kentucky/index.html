Aeneas Communications, LLC d/b/a Aeneas Internet and Telephone

Cooperative / municipal partner | P2: partnership

Current ownership

Cooperative-controlled through Southwest Tennessee Electric Membership Corporation. STEMC board materials describe its completed Aeneas acquisition; current legal-entity chain and exact percentage still require confirmation.

Commercial fit (inference)

West Tennessee operating proximity supports partnership diligence. It is not a conventional founder-owned control target, and acquiring an ISP would not automatically transfer the electric cooperative network.

Possible transaction or partnership

Wholesale, operating services, co-build or a consented ISP/asset perimeter; cooperative and grant obligations must be defined.

Constraints

Cooperative control, electric-network ownership, public funding and change-of-control consents. Exact current cap table and asset boundaries remain unverified.

Countercase

STEMC may obtain sufficient scale through its existing operator without selling. A conventional whole-company acquisition should not lead the approach.

Geographic clusters

  • Jackson and Madison County core
  • Fayette, Tipton, Lauderdale, Haywood, Madison, Chester and Hardeman counties under the 2024 middle-mile award
  • Bolivar/Hardeman, Dancyville/Haywood, Ripley/Lauderdale and the Southwest Tennessee Electric service area through utility partnerships

Construction evidence

  • status: completed; date: 2024-07-22; evidence: Aeneas said its fiber expansion with Southwest Tennessee Electric was completed one year ahead of schedule across the electric cooperative's 11-county service area. This establishes a completed utility-partnership network, not Aeneas ownership of the electric system.
  • status: completed; date: 2025-07-22; evidence: Tennessee reported the Dancyville project had delivered broadband access to 1,144 locations. Locations are not subscribers.
  • status: awarded_not_verified_complete; date: 2024-04-30; evidence: Tennessee awarded $7.189 million for middle-mile work across seven West Tennessee counties, with program projects due by 2026-12-31.

Reported scale and date

  • More than 30,000 customers reported on a current company page accessed 2026-09-20; the page states growth from 20,000 customers in 2020 by more than 50% over five years. Customer mix and exact milestone date are not disclosed.

West Kentucky & Tennessee Telecommunications Cooperative d/b/a WK&T

Cooperative / municipal partner | P2: selective

Current ownership

Not-for-profit, member-owned telephone cooperative headquartered in Mayfield, Kentucky.

Commercial fit (inference)

Best immediate geographic partner. WK&T surrounds Rightfiber's Paducah/McCracken beachhead and extends south toward Rightfiber's West Tennessee markets, offering a corridor thesis without requiring Rightfiber to duplicate an established cooperative network.

Possible transaction or partnership

Lead with reciprocal transport, interconnection, enterprise referrals and joint grant or edge-build arrangements. Explore a JV, operating partnership or member-supported strategic combination only after board, member, RUS, grant and regulatory feasibility is established.

Constraints

Member ownership, nonprofit purpose, ILEC and carrier-of-last-resort obligations, public funding, possible RUS liens and two-state approvals make a conventional acquisition difficult. Current sale willingness is unknown.

Countercase

WK&T already has an all-fiber network, meaningful scale and public funding. It may need little from Rightfiber beyond selective transport, and overlapping Paducah-area competition could make cooperation less attractive.

Geographic clusters

  • Western Kentucky exchanges across Calloway, Carlisle, Graves, Hickman, Marshall and McCracken counties, including Mayfield-area communities, Hardin, Hazel, Sedalia, Wingo and New Concord
  • Northwest Tennessee exchanges and expansion areas in Henry and Weakley counties, including Puryear, Cottage Grove, Martin and Dresden

Construction evidence

  • status: actual_network; date: Current page accessed 2026-09-20; evidence: WK&T describes a 100% FTTH network and more than 3,100 route miles.
  • status: awarded_completion_not_reverified; date: 2022-06-20; evidence: Kentucky awarded $3.366 million, matched to a $6.731 million total project, targeting 843 unserved households and businesses in Calloway and Marshall counties.
  • status: awarded_not_verified_complete; date: 2024-04-30; evidence: Tennessee awarded $6.610 million of middle-mile funding for Henry and Weakley counties, with program projects due by 2026-12-31.

Reported scale and date

  • As of the 2024-12-10 Kentucky PSC application: approximately 14,825 Kentucky broadband customers and 7,549 Tennessee broadband customers, or 22,374 in total across the stated two-state perimeter. The company history page separately says more than 18,000 connections and 3,100 route miles without a date; those different units and vintages are not reconciled.

United Communications

Cooperative / municipal partner | P2: selective

Current ownership

Broadband subsidiary of Middle Tennessee Electric, a member-owned, not-for-profit electric cooperative. MTE acquired majority ownership in 2018; current MTE materials identify United as its subsidiary.

Commercial fit (inference)

Largest and most capable Tennessee partner, but not an easy bolt-on. United would create a major Middle Tennessee position and Nashville interconnection point, although it is not contiguous with Rightfiber's publicly listed West Tennessee residential markets.

Possible transaction or partnership

Pursue wholesale transport, enterprise/carrier interconnection, route diversity and coordinated grant edges. Any equity route should be framed as a mission-preserving cooperative strategic option, not a sponsor-style sale assumption.

Constraints

MTE cooperative governance, strategic smart-grid value, current organic investment and BEAD obligations. Large scale may make the asset expensive and strategically core to MTE.

Countercase

United is well funded, expanding and tied to the second-largest U.S. electric cooperative. Rightfiber has no demonstrated residential adjacency to its core counties, weakening near-term acquisition synergies.

Geographic clusters

  • Franklin and Williamson County
  • Murfreesboro/Rutherford, Lebanon/Wilson and Nashville/Davidson
  • Shelbyville/Bedford, Lewisburg/Marshall, Columbia-area Maury, Winchester/Franklin, Lynchburg/Moore, Giles and Lincoln counties

Construction evidence

  • status: actual_network; date: 2026-03-10; evidence: United reported more than 6,100 fiber-route miles serving more than 120,000 homes, businesses and community institutions across Middle Tennessee. These are served locations, not subscribers or passings.
  • status: awarded_future_build; date: 2026-03-10; evidence: $18.1 million BEAD award targeting approximately 1,800 unserved or underserved homes and businesses in Maury, Rutherford, Trousdale, Williamson and Wilson counties; completion required by 2028-12-31.

Reported scale and date

  • More than 6,100 fiber-route miles and more than 120,000 served locations as of 2026-03-10. MTE's current page lists an 11-county United service perimeter.

Brandenburg Telephone Company and affiliate Brandenburg Telecom

Independent operator screen | P2: selective

Current ownership

Privately held corporate group. Brandenburg Telephone is wholly owned by Brandenburg Communications Corporation; Brandenburg Telecom is another wholly owned subsidiary. Ultimate parent shareholders were not disclosed in the 2025 audited statements.

Commercial fit (inference)

Most conventional Kentucky corporate target in the tested set, but a second-stage rather than immediate adjacency play. It could add a central Kentucky/Elizabethtown corridor after Rightfiber consolidates western Kentucky or confirms former GPC route adjacency.

Possible transaction or partnership

Approach at Brandenburg Communications Corporation for a whole-group transaction or structured partnership covering both regulated ILEC and competitive affiliate assets. First map KentuckyWired, bank-related-party and affiliate arrangements and confirm change-of-control requirements.

Constraints

Geographically distant from Paducah; ultimate owners unknown; regulated ILEC and related-party structure; no verified sale process; subscriber and fiber-passing counts unavailable.

Countercase

A profitable, conservatively capitalized private company may have no need to sell. Without confirmed Rightfiber backbone adjacency, the deal would create an isolated operating island rather than a bolt-on.

Geographic clusters

  • Brandenburg/Meade County, Vine Grove/Hardin County and Irvington/Breckinridge County ILEC territory
  • Elizabethtown, Clarkson, Sonora, Upton and Muldraugh competitive markets through Brandenburg Telecom

Construction evidence

  • status: actual_network_program; date: 2015 launch; current service page accessed 2026-09-20; evidence: The group began BBTELConnect FTTH implementation in 2015 and currently markets fiber plans. No source reviewed establishes that the full perimeter is all-fiber.
  • status: actual_capex; date: 2025-12-31; evidence: Audited 2025 purchases of property and equipment were $2.467 million; telephone plant in service was $85.591 million. Capex is company-wide, not identified as fiber-only.
  • status: completed_contract; date: 2017-10-31; evidence: Brandenburg is a KentuckyWired dark-fiber IRU counterparty, demonstrating an existing state middle-mile relationship.

Reported scale and date

  • For Brandenburg Telephone's audited 2025 perimeter: $13.275 million operating revenue; $9.486 million nonregulated gross income before $4.546 million of related expenses, producing $4.939 million of nonregulated net income; $7.725 million total net income; $85.591 million telephone plant in service; and $2.467 million of property/equipment purchases. Net income is not EBITDA. Subscriber and FTTH-passing counts were not found.

Ben Lomand Connect

Cooperative / municipal partner | P2: selective

Current ownership

Member-owned, nonprofit telephone cooperative. Members receive patronage capital subject to board retirement decisions.

Commercial fit (inference)

Substantial south-central Tennessee partner with grant execution capability. It is not contiguous with Rightfiber's listed West Tennessee residential markets, so the strongest logic is route sharing and future Middle Tennessee corridor development.

Possible transaction or partnership

Propose middle-mile interconnection, reciprocal enterprise referrals and joint grant edges around Franklin/Moore. Consider a JV or shared-services model only if it protects member economics and cooperative control.

Constraints

Member ownership, nonprofit purpose, board/member governance, grant obligations and a large active self-build program make outright M&A structurally difficult.

Countercase

Ben Lomand is already scaling toward an all-fiber network and has secured material public funding. Geographic distance and cooperative autonomy reduce near-term transaction need.

Geographic clusters

  • McMinnville/Warren, Sparta/White, Tracy City/Grundy, Spencer/Van Buren, Manchester/Coffee and Crossville/Cumberland
  • Franklin, Moore and Marion county expansion areas, with legacy/service presence also cited in Cannon, DeKalb, Putnam, Rutherford and Bedford

Construction evidence

  • status: actual_network; date: 2023 annual meeting; evidence: Ben Lomand reported close to 30,000 broadband connections, 83% on fiber, and said it was approaching 100% FTTH. Connections are not unique passings or subscribers.
  • status: awarded_not_verified_complete; date: 2024-04-30 and 2024-10-30; evidence: Tennessee awarded $14.683 million of middle-mile funding for Franklin and Moore counties and later approved $4.671 million of last-mile funding for Moore County.

Reported scale and date

  • Close to 30,000 broadband connections, of which 83% were fiber, reported at the 2023 annual meeting. No newer connection count was found in the primary sources reviewed.

DeKalb Telephone Cooperative, Inc. d/b/a DTC Communications

Cooperative / municipal partner | P2: selective

Current ownership

Member-owned telephone cooperative. DTC-branded services are provided by the cooperative and wholly owned subsidiary Advantage Cellular Systems, Inc.

Commercial fit (inference)

Useful central Tennessee operating and grant partner, but smaller and less geographically immediate than United or Ben Lomand. The Rutherford/Wilson edges could matter if Rightfiber extends east from West Tennessee.

Possible transaction or partnership

Lead with wholesale transport, rural edge construction or joint grant support. Treat any business combination as member- and board-driven, with RUS and regulatory review, rather than a normal bilateral acquisition.

Constraints

Cooperative ownership, mixed legacy technologies, unknown scale, public-funding obligations and no evidence of sale willingness.

Countercase

The network is not publicly proven to be all-fiber, scale is unknown and the footprint is not contiguous with Rightfiber's currently listed Tennessee residential markets.

Geographic clusters

  • Alexandria/Smithville and DeKalb County
  • Woodbury/Cannon, Smith, Wilson and Rutherford county edges

Construction evidence

  • status: awarded_completion_not_verified; date: 2023-06-12; evidence: USDA awarded a $1.587 million ReConnect grant to deploy FTTP benefiting 787 people, nine businesses and 56 farms in Rutherford, Wilson and Cannon counties. The source is an award notice, not proof of completion.
  • status: actual_mixed_network; date: Current page accessed 2026-09-20; evidence: DTC markets fiber up to 5 Gbps, but its network disclosure still identifies ADSL, VDSL and fiber. The full operating perimeter should not be called all-fiber.

Reported scale and date

  • Current customer and route-mile counts were not published in the primary materials reviewed. The bounded 2023 ReConnect project targets 787 people, nine businesses and 56 farms; these are beneficiaries, not customers or passings.

Twin Lakes Telephone Cooperative Corporation

Cooperative / municipal partner | P2: selective

Current ownership

Member-subscriber-owned Tennessee telephone cooperative operating on a nonprofit basis.

Commercial fit (inference)

High-quality Upper Cumberland network but lowest immediate adjacency to Rightfiber's listed Tennessee/Kentucky residential markets. Strategic value rises only in a broader eastward Tennessee build or statewide wholesale design.

Possible transaction or partnership

Maintain as a wholesale, route-diversity and future interconnection partner. A cooperative operating alliance or shared-services arrangement is more credible than acquisition.

Constraints

Member ownership, nonprofit bylaws, mature all-fiber network, limited current geographic adjacency and no public transaction catalyst.

Countercase

Twin Lakes has meaningful scale, an all-fiber system and strong local control. Those strengths reduce its need for Rightfiber and make a sale unlikely absent a member-led strategic shift.

Geographic clusters

  • Gainesboro/Jackson, Livingston/Overton, Cookeville South and Baxter/Putnam
  • Celina/Clay, Byrdstown/Pickett, Jamestown and Clarkrange/Fentress
  • Additional Upper Cumberland exchanges include Chestnut Mound, Crawford, Granville, Highland, Moss, North Springs and Rickman

Construction evidence

  • status: actual_network; date: Current page accessed 2026-09-20; evidence: Twin Lakes reports a 100% fiber network serving more than 40,000 connections in the Upper Cumberland. Connections are not necessarily customers or passings.
  • status: awarded_not_verified_complete; date: 2024-04-30; evidence: Tennessee awarded $1.055 million of middle-mile funding for Putnam County, with program projects due by 2026-12-31.

Reported scale and date

  • More than 40,000 connections on a 100% fiber network, per the current company page accessed 2026-09-20. The page does not define connections as subscribers or passings.
