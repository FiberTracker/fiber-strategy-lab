ALLO Communications

Scaled strategic platform | P2: selective

Current ownership

Private with SDC, Nelnet and founder/management. Nelnet held 27% economic and 20% voting rights at 2025 year-end; SDC percentage undisclosed.

Commercial fit (inference)

[I] Sponsor combination, market swap or leased-network partner, not bolt-on.

Possible transaction or partnership

Sponsor-to-sponsor merger, Nebraska market swap, joint leased-network build or minority recap.

Constraints

Leverage; Nebraska overlap; Multiple investors; No proven Kansas residential FTTH cluster

Countercase

Large debt and duplicate Nebraska plant can make a premium transaction dilutive.

Geographic clusters

  • 40 Nebraska communities
  • Lincoln
  • Omaha suburbs
  • Grand Island
  • Kearney
  • Kansas City business market
  • Colorado: ten communities reported on June 12, 2025, concentrated in Weld, Adams, Morgan, Boulder and Summit counties; Greeley construction completed in June 2025 and Erie construction remained active after a July 2025 stop-work order was lifted.
  • Arizona: City of Yuma retail FTTP plus Yuma County middle mile. The county owns the middle-mile network and ALLO operates and markets it to carriers.
  • Boulder, Colorado: planned citywide last mile is no longer a build cluster. ALLO withdrew on July 30, 2026 after beginning limited work in 2025.

Construction evidence

  • Completed 342 Nebraska fiber miles to more than 1,450 addresses over 18 months, announced 2026-03-17.
  • Greeley's citywide all-fiber build, begun August 2021, was declared complete June 12, 2025.
  • Erie construction began October 2022; the Town lifted its latest stop-work order on July 8, 2025.
  • Yuma County reports completed western phases and an eastern middle-mile segment at 76% overall on the current page, but the table has no visible update date.
  • Boulder reported ALLO would not perform the 2025 lease requiring 80% coverage by 2028 and 97% by 2030.

Reported scale and date

  • approximately 157,000 residential customers and 74,000 business lines at 2025-12-31
  • approximately $260 million 2025 revenue
  • $1.41 billion debt at 2025-12-31
  • 157,000 residential customers and approximately 74,000 business lines nationwide at December 31, 2025.
  • $260 million nationwide revenue for calendar 2025 and $1.41 billion debt outstanding at December 31, 2025.
  • $20.7 million of Yuma County ARPA funding for phase one and a $10 million Arizona broadband grant for phase two; these fund the county-owned middle-mile perimeter, not ALLO corporate equity or all Arizona FTTP.
  • $500 million gross financing completed in June 2025 at ALLO Holdings.

Wyyerd Fiber

Scaled strategic platform | P2: selective

Current ownership

Carlyle-backed private FTTP platform. Wyyerd acquired Zona Communications in 2019 and acquired Arizona fiber assets from Ting Fiber and Conterra Networks on May 21, 2025.

Commercial fit (inference)

[Inference] Wyyerd is a scaled, sponsor-backed consolidator with strong Arizona metro density, not an obvious seller. It is outside RightFiber's core. Its combination logic is statewide Arizona, not RightFiber adjacency.

Possible transaction or partnership

[Inference] Sponsor dialogue, financing, selective fiber-asset acquisitions or a future combination with remaining Southern Arizona Simply Bits operations are the plausible routes. A direct RightFiber deal is low probability.

Constraints

Carlyle ownership and a recently expanded debt facility favor continued growth.; Tucson commitment is a five-year target rather than a completed network.; Dark-fiber leaseback obligations to Conterra support school districts in Greater Tucson and Rio Rico.; Acquired backbone or institutional fiber does not by itself prove residential passings.

Countercase

[Inference] Rapid acquisition and deployment may create integration and construction risk. Public sources do not provide take rates or unit economics to validate the return on the new debt and Tucson commitment.

Geographic clusters

  • Maricopa County: West Valley base in Surprise, Peoria, Buckeye, Goodyear and Sun City West, with Avondale service launched June 30, 2026; current listings also include Phoenix, Glendale, El Mirage, Chandler and Gilbert.
  • Pima County: acquired Tucson, Marana and Oro Valley fiber in May 2025; Marana construction began June 2025 and countywide construction was underway by August 15, 2026.
  • Yavapai and Santa Cruz counties: acquired infrastructure in Prescott Valley, Nogales and Rio Rico was described as a foundation for expansion, not proof of completed residential FTTP service.

Construction evidence

  • Acquired fiber across seven Arizona municipalities from Ting and Conterra in May 2025.
  • Avondale moved from construction in December 2025 to available service in June 2026.
  • Tucson approved an FTTP license and right-of-way agreement in July 2026; Wyyerd committed more than $200 million of private investment over five years.
  • Pima County construction was announced as underway on August 15, 2026.

Reported scale and date

  • $435 million debt facility as of August 7, 2025, for Arizona and California FTTP expansion.
  • More than $200 million planned private investment in Tucson over five years from July 2026. This is a target, not spent capital.
  • No reviewed primary source disclosed Arizona subscribers, passings or total owned route miles.

Highline

Scaled strategic platform | P2: selective

Current ownership

The latest reviewed primary ownership description identifies Highline as a subsidiary of ITC Broadband Holdings, with Atlantic Engineering Group described in 2021 as a construction partner and part-owner. Highline acquired Rye Telephone, RTC Broadband and SPT Broadband in August 2021. [Unknown] A current 2026 cap table was not found.

Commercial fit (inference)

[Inference] Highline provides the strongest existing rural Colorado operating complement to Maverix, but it is an acquisitive multi-state platform and outside RightFiber's core. Its legacy ILEC and grant perimeters may be harder to separate than a stand-alone overbuilder.

Possible transaction or partnership

[Inference] A Colorado asset swap, carve-out or Highline acquisition of a smaller independent is more credible than Highline selling to RightFiber. Direct dialogue should first confirm current ownership and which Colorado assets sit in regulated incumbent entities.

Constraints

Current cap table is unverified.; Some Colorado assets derive from acquired telephone companies and may include regulated obligations.; Available-home figures and construction claims are not a statewide passing count.; No Arizona footprint was identified.

Countercase

[Inference] Highline's current acquisition activity has focused on Michigan and Texas, while the newest Colorado construction evidence located is materially older. Colorado may be a mature or lower-capital-priority cluster rather than an active sale candidate.

Geographic clusters

  • Pueblo County and southern Colorado: Pueblo, Pueblo West, Colorado City and Rye, with current service also listed in Walsenburg and Kim.
  • Park County and mountain corridor: Bailey, Como, Fairplay, Guffey, Hartsel, Jefferson, Lake George and Pine.
  • Mesa County: Grand Junction; the operator reported construction to more than 1,500 homes by February 2023.

Construction evidence

  • Highline's current Colorado page markets symmetrical FTTP service across 16 named places.
  • The current availability page says more than 3,000 Pueblo West homes are available and construction north and south of Highway 50 is in progress, but gives no update date.
  • Grand Junction had more than 1,500 constructed homes by February 2023, with then-current work around Fruitvale and north of Patterson Road.
  • Highline is the ISP. AEG is an engineering and construction partner, not a separate retail operator in this screen.

Reported scale and date

  • More than 1,500 Grand Junction homes reached by construction as of February 2023.
  • More than 3,000 Pueblo West homes described as service-available on the current undated page.
  • No current Colorado aggregate passings, subscribers, revenue or grant total was disclosed in reviewed primary sources.

Maverix Broadband

Independent operator screen | P2: selective

Current ownership

Privately held Colorado operator founded by David Lindauer. [Unknown] No primary source reviewed disclosed the current equity holders, debt or sponsor backing.

Commercial fit (inference)

[Inference] Maverix is the best smaller independent in the screen for a relationship or sale-readiness check. It offers founder-led local execution, but the network is dispersed, grant-dependent and outside RightFiber's core geography.

Possible transaction or partnership

[Inference] Founder dialogue, minority growth capital, grant-compliant sale, or a combination with Highline or another Colorado operator. Any deal must separate active customer networks from awarded but incomplete projects and confirm change-of-control consent.

Constraints

Ownership and leverage are unknown.; Multiple public grants may carry build, affordability, reporting and change-of-control obligations.; Website service geography does not reconcile cleanly with all grant perimeters.; No Arizona operations identified.

Countercase

[Inference] Grant wins create backlog and apparent scale, but they may also consume management capacity and private match capital. Without take-rate, cost-to-complete and equity data, Maverix cannot yet be underwritten as a clean tuck-in.

Geographic clusters

  • Douglas and Elbert counties: active FTTP in Parker, Elizabeth and Kiowa. Franktown is labeled coming soon.
  • Boulder and Gilpin counties: Nederland, Gilpin County and unincorporated Boulder County are a grant-funded build perimeter, not confirmed complete service.
  • Chaffee and Saguache counties: public award references identify another FTTH project, but current construction status was not confirmed from an accessible primary project update.
  • Livermore is labeled coming soon on Maverix's site despite the site's Douglas and Elbert county positioning, creating a perimeter inconsistency that requires address-level confirmation.

Construction evidence

  • Maverix markets a 100% direct-fiber network and current service in Parker, Elizabeth and Kiowa.
  • Colorado's Office of Information Technology reported the first Capital Projects Fund connections in Elizabeth and Kiowa on November 25, 2024; it described fiber between the towns as a middle-mile network with residential extension.
  • A 2024 $8,667,692 Capital Projects Fund award supports fiber to 2,725 previously unserved or underserved addresses in Nederland, Gilpin County and parts of unincorporated Boulder County. This is an award and target perimeter, not proof all locations are built.

Reported scale and date

  • $8,667,692 awarded in 2024 for the Nederland, Gilpin and Boulder perimeter, targeting 2,725 addresses in the current Town document.
  • First grant-funded customer connection in the Kiowa and Elizabeth project reported November 25, 2024.
  • No current total passings, subscribers, revenue, debt or private matching-capital figure was confirmed.

Loveland Pulse and municipal-system route

Cooperative / municipal partner | P2: selective

Current ownership

Pulse is a City of Loveland-owned municipal communications utility. It is not privately owned acquisition inventory. Timnath funds its local construction under an intergovernmental agreement, while Pulse owns and operates that network and provides service.

Commercial fit (inference)

[Inference] Pulse has no conventional M&A fit for RightFiber. It demonstrates a municipal operating model that could be replicated through an IGA, wholesale or managed-service partnership, but Colorado remains outside RightFiber's core.

Possible transaction or partnership

[Inference] Public-private operating agreement, transport, wholesale access, network management, or adjacent municipal expansion. Do not frame Pulse, Connexion or NextLight as ordinary sale targets without an explicit public process.

Constraints

Municipal ownership and public-purpose mandates.; IGA economics, revenue sharing and public capital in Timnath.; Public procurement, voter and council considerations.; Potential confusion between system ownership and operating responsibility.

Countercase

[Inference] Strong municipal take rates and completed builds reduce the need for an equity partner. The partnership model is commercially relevant, but the transaction fee path is narrower and slower than private-company M&A.

Geographic clusters

  • Larimer County: Loveland citywide network, completed in 2023.
  • Larimer and Weld counties: Timnath expansion, with a live first connection and a multi-year phased rollout.
  • Municipal comparables: Fort Collins Connexion and Longmont NextLight remain city-owned operators; they are partnership comparables, not Pulse acquisition targets.

Construction evidence

  • Loveland declared its core build complete on November 16, 2023 after four years.
  • The Timnath IGA provides that Pulse will own and operate the network, Timnath will fund construction in phases, and Timnath receives 25% of gross wired-data revenue.
  • Timnath reported its first Pulse connection live on the current page; the overall rollout remains phased.
  • Longmont reported more than 43,000 premises passed and more than 28,000 served at July 2025. Fort Collins reported a 43% residential take rate in Q1 2025. These figures describe separate municipal systems.

Reported scale and date

  • Loveland press release: nearly $110 million overall investment, 631 miles of conduit and more than 1,316 miles of fiber at construction completion in November 2023.
  • Timnath: $20 million initial Town capital-improvement funding announced in August 2023; expected full payback about 26 years and full construction then estimated at four to five years.
  • The nearly $110 million press figure is not reconciled to narrower city financial-report project-cost figures in this review.

Ting Internet, including Simply Bits

Scaled strategic platform | P2: selective

Current ownership

Ting Fiber is a wholly owned Tucows subsidiary. Tucows began a strategic review in Q4 2025 and continued it as of August 6, 2026. The review includes possible asset sales, partnership structures and other transactions. Tucows completed a July 27, 2026 exit transaction with Generate that canceled approximately $150 million of Series A preferred units, but Ting's secured-note and operating obligations remain relevant.

Commercial fit (inference)

[Inference] Ting is the highest-priority event-driven name because a live strategic review exists and Colorado offers a separable cluster. It remains outside RightFiber's core, and much of the Colorado footprint depends on municipal or utility leases rather than fully owned backbone.

Possible transaction or partnership

[Inference] Explore a Colorado carve-out, partnership or asset purchase from Tucows. Treat Simply Bits as a separate FWA tuck-in or migration opportunity, not as fiber. The strategic review and July 2026 capital cleanup create a credible dialogue route, but do not prove a sale will occur.

Constraints

Strategic review is announced, but no transaction is announced or closed.; Secured financing and network lease obligations may restrict asset separation.; Tucows does not disclose Colorado-specific subscribers, passings or EBITDA.; Simply Bits is FWA-led and excluded from Ting's disclosed serviceable-address and account figures.

Countercase

Ting's Q2 2026 revenue and gross-profit trends improved, and the Generate exit removed the asserted preferred-return breach. [Inference] Improved performance and cleaner capital may reduce urgency to sell, even while the strategic review continues.

Geographic clusters

  • Colorado: Centennial, Greenwood Village, Thornton and Colorado Springs, plus a Western Slope/Roaring Fork presence. Greenwood Village first lit service in March 2024. Colorado Springs operates through a 25-year lease with Colorado Springs Utilities signed January 7, 2022.
  • Arizona: Ting sold fiber assets in Tucson, Marana, Oro Valley, Prescott Valley, Nogales and Rio Rico to Wyyerd in May 2025.
  • Arizona retained perimeter: Simply Bits continues to market fixed-wireless internet across Southern Arizona. This is not FTTP and must not be combined with Ting fiber counts.

Construction evidence

  • Ting continued selective FTTH investment, including Colorado, during 2025 and 2026 while slowing broader expansion.
  • Greenwood Village was live in March 2024; Centennial uses a 20-year municipal dark-fiber lease, while Colorado Springs uses a 25-year utility-network lease.
  • Wyyerd acquired specified Arizona fiber assets in May 2025.
  • Simply Bits' current product states that a home antenna connects to nearby towers and advertises 50/15 and 100/30 Mbps plans, confirming FWA rather than FTTP for the current retail offer.

Reported scale and date

  • 126,000 owned-infrastructure serviceable addresses, 109,000 partner-infrastructure serviceable addresses and 54,000 active Ting accounts nationwide at December 31, 2025, excluding Simply Bits.
  • $68.2 million Ting segment revenue for 2025.
  • $238.5 million of 2023 term notes and $63.0 million of 2024 term notes issued through Ting's securitized financing facilities; these are nationwide Ting financing, not Colorado-only debt.
  • Simply Bits added approximately 4,500 customers and 1,100 square miles of fixed-wireless network at its 2021 acquisition. Current comparable figures were not disclosed.

Wecom Fiber

Scaled strategic platform | P2: selective

Current ownership

Searchlight-controlled private operator. The FCC described Wecom LLC as wholly owned by Searchlight WCM Holdco after the approved transaction, with Searchlight Fiber Alliance holding the controlling interest upstream. Wecom's 2026 company disclosures continue to describe Searchlight backing.

Commercial fit (inference)

[Inference] Wecom is the strongest rural Arizona platform and a logical complement to Wyyerd's metro clusters, but Searchlight backing, BEAD awards and large funded builds make it a buyer or standalone growth platform. It is not a practical RightFiber add-on and is geographically non-core.

Possible transaction or partnership

[Inference] Sponsor dialogue, construction or project financing, future recapitalization, or a large Arizona platform combination. Any sale or merger must preserve BEAD and CPF obligations and distinguish grant assets, private builds and legacy FWA.

Constraints

Nearly $196 million of BEAD obligations and a large cost-to-complete program.; CPF pole-permit delays and unverified completion after the April 2026 extended deadline.; Mixed FTTP and fixed-wireless technology perimeter.; Searchlight control and no public subscriber or financial metrics.

Countercase

[Inference] Wecom's large awards and private builds produce scale but also execution, matching-capital and compliance risk. A statewide Wyyerd-Wecom thesis is strategically coherent, but sponsor appetite, valuation and overlapping planned areas are unknown.

Geographic clusters

  • Western Arizona: active or grant-funded fiber in Mohave and La Paz counties, including Kingman, Golden Valley, Yucca, Meadview, Parker, Quartzsite, Bouse, Brenda and Ehrenberg.
  • Yavapai County: active or under construction in Prescott Valley, Prescott, Cordes Lakes, Spring Valley, Rimrock, Mayer, Paulden, Dewey-Humboldt and other rural communities.
  • Coconino County: Flagstaff active; Williams, Parks, Grand Canyon and Forest Lakes shown as planning.
  • Pinal County: Casa Grande active; Arizona City, Apache Junction, San Tan Valley, Maricopa, Eloy and Superior shown under construction on the current map.
  • BEAD planning also covers Navajo, Gila and Maricopa counties.

Construction evidence

  • Arizona's 2025 CPF report says the Mohave/La Paz AZ-EXTEND project had reached 6,754 locations, had 80% design completion and active construction in four zones; the deadline was extended to April 15, 2026 because of pole-permit delays. Current final completion is unverified.
  • Prescott Valley's $35 million privately financed FTTP build targets 25,000 homes and businesses with completion within 18 months of June 2025.
  • Wecom's current map separates active, in-construction and planning locations.
  • Wecom operates both FTTP and fixed wireless. Legacy or current wireless coverage must not be counted as fiber passings.

Reported scale and date

  • $195 million of approved Arizona BEAD funding announced December 8, 2025, with private match, targeting more than 66,000 homes and businesses across 17 project areas in eight counties. This is an award and target, not completed passings.
  • AZ-EXTEND: $10 million CPF award plus $30,444,979 match, targeting more than 2,000 total fiber miles, 13,682 households, 390 businesses and 124 community anchor institutions in Mohave and La Paz counties; 2025 report status was 6,754 locations reached.
  • Prescott Valley: $35 million private project targeting 25,000 homes and businesses, announced June 27, 2025.
  • No current companywide subscriber, revenue or EBITDA figure was found in reviewed primary sources.
