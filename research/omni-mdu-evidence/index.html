Omni / Lit: prove the MDU economics property by property

Omni has a documented MDU sales and access process, plus one qualified property-manager listing that markets fiber availability. The reviewed public record does not yet establish a signed MDU portfolio, a bulk-contract base or superior property economics. The pitch should make those claims conditional on the property book. [P] M01 [P] M02 [P] M03

Omni and integrated Lit operations are the subject of this analysis. Socket and IdeaTek remain a separate sponsor-platform case. A property listing, blank agreement or municipal right-of-way is not a verified contract, installed-unit schedule or customer base.

The defensible pitch today

The strongest proposition is an operating process that could turn property access into recurring service revenue. Omni markets a community-development channel and publishes a long-term right-of-entry agreement. A property manager also lists Omni availability at an identified Mansfield apartment. Those are tangible capability signals; they do not measure execution across a portfolio. [P] M01 [P] M02 [P] M03

Present three separate commercial structures: retail resident sales under signed access agreements; property-paid bulk service under an executed billable-unit schedule; and carrier wholesale service under a separately negotiated customer-ownership, capex and service-level contract. The public materials do not establish an existing Omni bulk or carrier-MDU book. [P] M01 [P] M02

  • Value contracted, ready and billable units separately. Accessible units are not automatically customers or revenue.
  • Tie any MDU premium to assignable rights, installation conversion, retention and property contribution after the full cost of serving the property.
  • Use municipal P3 capability as a separate origination and operating credential. It does not convey private building access. [P] M06 [P] M09

Underwrite the executed agreements

The form currently linked from Omni’s MDU page has a 20-year initial term, annual renewals, and 60-day nonrenewal notice. It grants installation, maintenance, access and resident-marketing rights, with equipment owned by Omni and a covenant running with the land. The older July-path form contemplates perpetual access. Both are blank templates: neither establishes the terms of any executed property contract, and the newer link does not amend earlier contracts. [P] M01 [P] M02 [P] M04

The current blank form does not specify an executed property, certified unit count, bulk billing, property compensation, minimum billable units, service levels, deployment budget or property economics. Treat those as separate diligence requests. The form does not establish exclusive access. [P] M02

  • Obtain the signed agreement and all schedules for each property: legal owner, term, renewal, assignment, termination, access, marketing and equipment rights.
  • For property payments and access restrictions, obtain counsel review of the executed agreement and applicable provider classification. The reviewed federal common-carrier rule addresses exclusive access and certain compensation arrangements; exclusive marketing has disclosure requirements and is not categorically banned. [P] M12 [P] M13 [P] M15
  • Separate city revenue sharing from property-owner compensation. Brownsville’s municipal operating arrangements do not disclose an apartment bulk contract. [P] M06 [P] M07

Carrier precedents: safe claims and Omni proof required

These public precedents show how carriers have divided network ownership, retail customers, construction and wholesale payments. They do not establish carrier interest in Omni, an Omni contract, or proposed transaction terms. Acquisition dollars, customers, passings and contracted or bulk-billed units remain separate denominators.

The base-case rule is simple: give no credit for carrier revenue, reimbursement, customer-book value or exclusivity until the applicable rights, units, rates, address election and closing status are documented. Unsigned opportunities may be shown only as contingent scenarios. [P] CR01 [P] CR02 [P] CR14 [P] CR15

Illustrative economics: contribution can coexist with poor cash recovery

These three deliberately hypothetical retail-access cohorts are not estimates for Omni, Lit or any other operator. They show why direct contribution alone is insufficient: slower conversion can leave simple capital recovery far beyond an attractive holding period. Cohorts have different sizes and cost assumptions; they are scenarios, not measured peers.

Annual direct contribution equals subscriber revenue less property payments and direct service/building costs. It excludes corporate overhead and is not EBITDA. Initial capex includes all accessible-unit in-building costs plus installation cost per activated subscriber.

Five-year cash recovery solves subscriber-dependent installation cost explicitly: required accounts = (five years of fixed property/building cost + in-building capex) divided by (five years of contribution per subscriber before fixed costs minus installation cost per subscriber). Required accounts are rounded upward. The model assumes accounts are present from day one and does not include ramp, churn, replacement capex, tax or financing.

The six files that turn the hypothesis into a pitch

  • Executed property agreements and a certified unit schedule, with versions and assignment requirements.
  • A dated bridge from signed units to accessible, ready-for-service, bulk-billed and active retail units; reconcile property addresses and double counting.
  • Monthly revenue, property fees, service costs, bad debt and retention by property cohort, separating recurring contribution from allocated EBITDA.
  • Itemized feeder, entrance, riser, unit-drop, ONT, gateway and remediation capex, including who pays and who owns each component.
  • For bulk or carrier proposals: billable-unit definitions, vacancy, minimum commitments, customer ownership, SLAs, capex funding and termination liabilities.
  • Evidence of route serviceability and in-building readiness for each named property; a city-level build announcement or FCC location record is insufficient.
