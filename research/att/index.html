AT&T / Gigapower challenge on Omni Fiber and Lit

As of: 2026-09-20
Evidence: Primary company, sponsor, county, FCC and SEC sources only. No private transcripts.

Conclusion

Lead with selected-market wholesale access to AT&T, not a sale to Gigapower. Keep an AT&T-anchored, ring-fenced build as the second route. Treat a full-company acquisition as low-probability near term.

That ranking is not a statement of known buyer interest. No public AT&T, Gigapower, Forged Fiber, BlackRock or Omni source reviewed through September 20 disclosed an Omni/Lit discussion, mandate, offer or process. It is an inference from AT&T's actual structures, current capital priorities and exact FCC overlap.

The strongest counterevidence to a whole-company pitch is current. On September 10, AT&T's CFO said the Lumen fiber and EchoStar spectrum transactions give the company optionality, but the opportunity now is to execute and monetize those assets and AT&T does not need to do anything in the near term. AT&T is still integrating Lumen customer operations, and the latest 10-Q still says AT&T plans to sell a controlling interest in Forged Fiber. A second fiber acquisition must therefore beat a credible do-nothing case. Sources: S05: AT&T at Citi Global TMT Conference, official investor-relations transcript, 2026-09-10; S06: AT&T at Goldman Sachs Communacopia + Technology Conference, official investor-relations transcript, 2026-09-09.

What AT&T actually bought from Lumen

AT&T closed the Lumen transaction on February 2, 2026. It bought a specific Mass Markets FTTP carve-out, not Lumen as a whole: more than 1 million fiber subscribers and more than 4 million fiber customer locations in Arizona, Colorado, Florida, Idaho, Iowa, Minnesota, Nebraska, Nevada, Oregon, Utah and Washington. Ohio, Michigan and Texas were not included. Lumen retained enterprise and wholesale fiber customers and its copper consumer business. Sources: S01: AT&T Completes Acquisition of Lumen's Mass Markets Fiber Business, 2026-02-02; S04: Lumen Form 8-K: completion of Mass Markets fiber-to-the-home sale, 2026-02-02.

The structure also matters. The 2025 purchaser labeled “NetworkCo” was Forged Fiber 37, LLC. By June 30, 2026, AT&T reported the physical network assets in wholly owned Forged Fiber 37 Services, LLC, while the acquired customer relationships sat in AT&T's Advanced Connectivity segment. AT&T still intended to sell a controlling interest in Forged Fiber to an equity partner and classified the network business as held for sale and discontinued operations. No primary-source partner announcement or deconsolidation close was found through September 20. Sources: S02: AT&T Form 8-K: Lumen purchase agreement and NetworkCo perimeter, 2025-05-21; S03: AT&T Form 10-Q for the period ended June 30, 2026, 2026-07-23; reporting period ended 2026-06-30.

Do not turn AT&T's reported $5.756 billion cash payment, including purchase-price adjustments, into a clean enterprise value comp. The deal included customer relationships, network assets, construction capability and transition/access arrangements while Lumen retained other assets and customers. AT&T's preliminary purchase accounting included about $3.4 billion of PP&E, $900 million of customer relationships and $800 million of goodwill. Those figures and the cash consideration describe a particular carve-out, not a reusable Omni valuation multiple. Sources: S03: AT&T Form 10-Q for the period ended June 30, 2026, 2026-07-23; reporting period ended 2026-06-30.

Gigapower is a precedent, not the presumed buyer

Gigapower is separate from Forged Fiber. AT&T reported a 50% interest at 2025 year-end, with joint governance by AT&T and a BlackRock infrastructure fund. Gigapower owns and operates a commercial wholesale open-access network; AT&T is the anchor ISP and retains retail customer relationships. Its launch thesis was selected metro construction beyond AT&T's traditional service areas. No source reviewed says Gigapower buys operating retail ISPs. Sources: S07: AT&T 2025 Annual Report, 2025-12-31 reporting date; published 2026; S08: Gigapower Joint Venture from AT&T and BlackRock Launches, 2023-05-11.

The more relevant precedent is AT&T's direct 2024 wholesale agreements with Boldyn, Digital Infrastructure Group, PRIME FiBER and Ubiquity. AT&T said it evaluates specific new service areas without existing fiber options. The same announcement named initial Boldyn builds at Fort Bliss and San Antonio, even though AT&T has extensive Texas operations. This directly rebuts the idea that statewide incumbent presence is a veto. Sources: S09: AT&T Strengthens Fiber Leadership Through New and Expanded Third-Party Relationships, 2024-09-09.

Exact Ohio, Michigan and Texas overlap

The test uses the FCC's December 31, 2025 state FTTP files. It retains technology 50 and residential-capable R/X records, deduplicates location_id for AT&T provider 130077 and Omni provider 480067, and intersects the two sets. These are reported availability locations where a standard installation could be performed. They are not subscribers, passings, housing units, construction completions or legal ILEC territory. Sources: S16: FCC BDC: How to Format Fixed Broadband Availability Location Lists, updated 2025-07-31; S17: FCC D25 Ohio State FTTP Location Coverage file 1789784, availability as of 2025-12-31; file published 2026-09-15; S18: FCC D25 Michigan State FTTP Location Coverage file 1789771, availability as of 2025-12-31; file published 2026-09-15; S19: FCC D25 Texas State FTTP Location Coverage file 1789792, availability as of 2025-12-31; file published 2026-09-15.

Ohio's overlap is concentrated: Wood County has 3,632 shared locations out of 11,792 Omni locations; Trumbull 3,953 of 11,159; Summit 1,780 of 8,643; and Stark 33 of 1,971. The other 23 Omni-reporting Ohio counties in D25 have zero exact AT&T FTTP overlap. Michigan's D25 Omni filing covers Midland and Saginaw Counties and has no exact AT&T overlap. Texas's measured Omni perimeter is Cameron County.

State presence is insufficient because it has no common address denominator. Copper, fixed wireless, ILEC territory, county adjacency and announced construction are different evidence classes. AT&T can have millions of FTTP locations in a state while lacking fiber at most Omni locations. Conversely, Omni's current 2026 service-area page includes build-stage communities not captured in D25. A live proposal needs the later FCC vintage when public plus Omni's current serviceability and build-date file.

Route comparison

1. Selected-market access: best supported. Omni retains the physical network; AT&T buys wholesale access at a defined non-overlap address list and owns the retail relationship. Start with D25-clean Michigan, Cameron County after removing 1,100 duplicate locations, and Ohio clusters outside the Wood, Trumbull and Summit overlap pockets. The gating items are wholesale product readiness, OSS/BSS integration, install and repair service levels, brand/channel rules, municipal and lender consents, and market-level contribution economics.

2. Anchor build: plausible, unproven. Ring-fence new Omni construction and seek an AT&T commitment with fixed locations, milestones, term, rates, minimum economics and remedies. Lit's Medina history shows an open-access residential model tied to a county-owned middle-mile network. Omni's Brownsville relationship shows a municipal partnership and adjacent smart-city/private-5G use cases. That proves capability, not AT&T appetite. Sources: S10: Omni Fiber and Lit Fiber announce merger to accelerate fiber-to-the-home expansion, 2024-11-20; S14: Medina County: Announcing Medina Fiber, an Open Access Residential Fiber Network, 2019-11-26; S15: Omni Fiber Brings High-Speed Internet and Smart Connectivity to Brownsville, 2025-05-27. Kill the route if the case depends on all announced locations becoming serviceable or if Omni cannot give AT&T sufficient delivery and operating control.

3. Full company: low near-term probability. The positive case is real: Lumen shows AT&T will buy underpenetrated fiber customer relationships, a network and a build engine to increase broadband penetration and wireless convergence. The countercase is stronger today. Omni is smaller and geographically scattered, retains sponsor and minority-shareholder complexity, has active construction and municipal relationships, and raised fresh capital for stand-alone growth. AT&T is integrating Lumen and has not publicly completed the Forged Fiber partner transaction. Sources: S05: AT&T at Citi Global TMT Conference, official investor-relations transcript, 2026-09-10; S06: AT&T at Goldman Sachs Communacopia + Technology Conference, official investor-relations transcript, 2026-09-09; S11: Omni Fiber raises $200 million from Oak Hill Capital, Stonepeak Credit, and Oak Hill Advisors, 2025-12-01; S12: Omni Fiber Service Areas, current company page as accessed 2026-09-20; S13: Lit Communities Announces Majority Investment from Oak Hill Capital, 2023-05-04.

Constraints and kill tests

  1. Refresh D25 to the latest common FCC vintage and reconcile it to Omni's serviceable-address file. Kill any market that misses AT&T's return hurdle after exact duplication and planned AT&T builds are removed. The hurdle is unknown.
  2. Require a complete wholesale operating package: ordering, provisioning, installs, repair, outage, cybersecurity, performance credits and customer ownership. Retail-only capability kills access and anchor routes.
  3. Do not route the opportunity to Gigapower without explicit AT&T and BlackRock-fund authority. If its mandate remains greenfield wholesale builds outside AT&T's traditional areas, direct AT&T access can survive while the Gigapower route fails.
  4. Do not place assets into Forged Fiber without knowing the equity partner, closing status, geographic mandate, leverage policy and acquisition rights. Its disclosed 11-state perimeter excludes the target states.
  5. Reconcile Omni ownership, debt, working-capital facility, liens, change-of-control terms, municipal contracts, subsidies and taxes. The reported $150 million incremental debt, $50 million equity and $10 million working-capital facility are financing events, not enterprise value.
  6. Build economics from serviceable locations, subscribers, ARPU, churn, installation cost, maintenance capex and committed construction capex. Kill any case that substitutes FCC locations for subscribers or announced construction for completed passings.
  7. Require an AT&T executive mandate for a full-company route. Generic strategic fit is not buyer appetite, and September management commentary favors execution of existing assets.

Gaps

The June 30, 2026 FCC download was unavailable in the bounded check, so D25 remains the measured overlap vintage. Gigapower's JavaScript-dependent live location and FAQ pages did not render, so no exact current market count is used. Brownsville's FY2025 ACFR distinguishes an initial five-year operating agreement from a 20-year maintenance agreement; each has up to three successive ten-year renewal options. The report does not establish who controls renewal or the applicable conditions. Assignment, transferability, customer economics and retained operator cash flow remain unresolved. Brownsville ACFR, Note 24, printed pages 157-158, PDF pages 164-165 Omni subscribers, revenue, EBITDA, take rate, current net debt, maintenance capex, municipal contract economics and wholesale readiness are not public in the sources reviewed.
