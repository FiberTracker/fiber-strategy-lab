Joink Holdings / Joink / CTI

Scaled strategic platform | P1: investigate

Current ownership

Private sponsor-backed platform. The 2022 FCC record shows Joink Holdings at 66% DIF CIF 2 US LLC and 34% J K Business Services. CVC acquired a majority stake in DIF's manager in 2024, but no later Joink change-of-control filing was found. The FCC granted the CTI transfer effective September 11, 2022.

Commercial fit (inference)

Best strategic combination in the screen because it adds both FTTP and enterprise capabilities across two target states while preserving a regional operating model.

Possible transaction or partnership

Sponsor-to-sponsor merger, fund-level sale with management rollover, or a commercial partnership around wholesale transport and off-net last mile. A one-market bolt-on is less consistent with Joink's existing platform ownership.

Constraints

Sponsor control, CTI integration, mixed fiber and fixed-wireless plant, unknown current consolidated scale, and no measured asset overlap with Rightfiber.

Countercase

Joink is itself a buy-and-build platform with fresh Indiana extension awards. Its owner may prefer continued standalone growth, and the absence of public scale data prevents confident synergy sizing.

Geographic clusters

  • Terre Haute and western Indiana
  • eastern Illinois
  • CTI's Christian and Montgomery County central Illinois markets

Construction evidence

  • Joink says it continues extending fiber across eastern Illinois and western Indiana. Indiana awarded Joink extensions covering 18 addresses in Round 15, 10 in Round 16 and 11 in Round 17; these are awards and not proof of completed service.

Reported scale and date

  • CTI served approximately 7,154 subscribers in Christian and Montgomery counties at the July 2022 FCC filing. Joink does not publish a current consolidated route-mile, passing or subscriber count. Company pages currently offer enterprise packages up to 10 Gbps.

ImOn Communications

Scaled strategic platform | P1: investigate

Current ownership

Acquired by Goldman Sachs Asset Management's infrastructure investing business on April 22, 2022. No subsequent sale or control change was found in reviewed primary sources.

Commercial fit (inference)

Best immediate Iowa partner and a credible future sponsor-to-sponsor combination if Goldman Sachs seeks an exit.

Possible transaction or partnership

Near-term reciprocal transport, CLEC last mile, data-center connectivity or joint enterprise pursuit. Longer-term whole-company combination or selected-market structure if ownership timing aligns.

Constraints

Sponsor-owned growth platform, active expansion outside Iowa, likely meaningful valuation, and possible duplication that requires address- and route-level diligence.

Countercase

ImOn has demonstrated organic execution and acquisition capability, including Danville Telecom in 2025, so it may be more natural as an independent counterparty or competing consolidator than a seller.

Geographic clusters

  • Cedar Rapids
  • Iowa City
  • Dubuque
  • Clinton-Camanche
  • Marshalltown
  • Sioux City-Siouxland
  • southeast Iowa

Construction evidence

  • Actual 2025 construction totaled 532 fiber miles across 17 communities, with more than $100 million invested. Five southeast Iowa networks were completed by February 2026, reaching nearly 30,000 homes and businesses after construction began in March 2025.

Reported scale and date

  • 532 fiber miles placed during 2025. Nearly 30,000 serviceable homes and businesses in Burlington, West Burlington, Fort Madison, Mount Pleasant and Keokuk as of February 19, 2026. These are not subscribers.

Metro Communications / Conxxus

Independent operator screen | P1: investigate

Current ownership

The January 2026 FCC record identifies Zachary Horn with 97.5% equity and 100% voting control of MCC. Metro acquired MTCO effective January 2, 2026 and acquired former Clearwave assets after FCC approval on March 12, 2026; acquired Clearwave assets were in the securitization collateral by June 23, 2026.

Commercial fit (inference)

Most consequential Illinois strategic partner or combination candidate, with enterprise, tower, transport and residential capabilities.

Possible transaction or partnership

Carrier interconnect, IRU, dark-fiber or enterprise channel partnership first. A later merger or recapitalization is more plausible than a near-term bolt-on while Metro integrates two 2026 acquisitions.

Constraints

Active integration, securitized assets, universal-service obligations at acquired ILECs, founder voting control and potentially complex carve-out perimeters.

Countercase

Metro is an active buyer with recent financing, not a demonstrated seller. Combining two consolidators before systems and field integration stabilize could destroy near-term execution capacity.

Geographic clusters

  • central Illinois
  • southern Illinois
  • Marseilles and Metamora exchanges
  • St. Louis-area routes

Construction evidence

  • Current growth evidence is acquisition-led. The Clearwave transaction added assets covering approximately 4,000 route miles plus customers in Illinois and around St. Louis. MTCO added about 10,000 residential customers and fiber-overbuilt ILEC markets.

Reported scale and date

  • Approximately 4,000 route miles of Clearwave assets in the 2025 application, approved March 12, 2026. MTCO served about 10,000 customers when the acquisition closed January 2, 2026. These perimeters should not be summed into a current Metro total without eliminating duplication.

New Lisbon Telephone Company / New Lisbon Broadband and Communications

Independent operator screen | P2: selective

Current ownership

NLBC is a subsidiary of New Lisbon Telephone Company within New Lisbon Holdings. Reviewed primary sources did not identify current ultimate beneficial owners or ownership percentages.

Commercial fit (inference)

Most plausible small Indiana tuck-in if owner intent, current customer count and plant mix validate.

Possible transaction or partnership

Bilateral whole-company dialogue or operating partnership. Separate valuation for regulated ILEC, CLEC, Pennsylvania Telephone and Taylored Systems perimeters may be required.

Constraints

Unknown ultimate ownership, stale scale disclosure, mixed technologies, multiple subsidiaries and possible non-core managed-services operations.

Countercase

New Lisbon acquired Taylored Systems in 2021, signaling buyer behavior. There is no current public sale indication and no quantified route adjacency to Rightfiber.

Geographic clusters

  • Henry County
  • Wayne County
  • Randolph County
  • New Castle
  • Richmond
  • Winchester and surrounding east-central Indiana communities

Construction evidence

  • State awards in 2021 targeted 28 Henry County locations and an 82-household Whitewater project in Wayne County. Awards are targets, not proof of completed construction. The current site offers fiber and fixed wireless but no dated 2025-2026 route-mile or passing count.

Reported scale and date

  • A 2021 Indiana grant summary said NLTC and NLBC served more than 3,000 customers. Current scale is unknown. Current service-area page lists 25 named east-central Indiana communities.

Surf Internet

Scaled strategic platform | P2: selective

Current ownership

Private sponsor-backed platform. Macquarie Capital led a January 2026 investment alongside Bain Capital and Future Standard. Public materials do not disclose control percentages.

Commercial fit (inference)

Commercial partner or large strategic combination, not a conventional bolt-on.

Possible transaction or partnership

Transport, dark-fiber, off-net last mile or enterprise channel partnership. Consider a future sponsor-led combination only after route and passing overlap is measured.

Constraints

Substantial sponsor capital, secured ABS structure, three-state build plan, likely high valuation and potentially significant but unquantified duplicate markets.

Countercase

Surf is funded to keep building and is positioned as a scaled super-regional platform. It is more likely to compete with or partner with Rightfiber than sell as a small add-on.

Geographic clusters

  • northern and central Indiana
  • northern Illinois
  • Michigan
  • Livingston and Ingham counties
  • Fowlerville and rural Livingston townships
  • Hartford/South Haven in Van Buren County
  • Sparta/Kent County
  • Emmet and Charlevoix counties

Construction evidence

  • Surf reported service available to more than 300,000 homes and businesses across the three-state region on May 26, 2026. Its January plan targeted more than 330,000 total passings during 2026; the target is not an actual. It closed a $407 million ABS on March 4, 2026 to support continued expansion.
  • Livingston ROBIN build was 50% complete as of 2025-08-05 and was expected to reach about 4,500 unserved homes by mid-2026; separate Fowlerville build targeted about 960 locations.
  • An $11.4 million privately funded northern Michigan project announced 2025-10-21 targeted 8,965 homes and businesses in Charlevoix, Petoskey, Boyne City, East Jordan and Harbor Springs.
  • Hartford construction targeted nearly 1,000 locations by December 2025.

Reported scale and date

  • More than 300,000 serviceable homes and businesses as of May 26, 2026, across Indiana, Illinois and Michigan; more than 330,000 passings is a 2026 target. More than 150 school systems served. These are not subscriber counts.
  • More than 200,000 serviceable fiber passings across three states as of 2025-05-13, actual company milestone.
  • Target to surpass 330,000 total passings in 2026, announced 2026-01-28; target, not actual.
  • $407 million ABS closed 2026-03-04 through a wholly owned financing subsidiary.

Zayo, including former Intelligent Fiber Network

Committed or integrated platform | P3: partner / monitor

Current ownership

IFN ceased to be independent when Zayo closed its acquisition on June 1, 2021. Zayo is privately held by a consortium led by DigitalBridge and EQT. Zayo closed its acquisition of Crown Castle's Fiber Solutions business on May 1, 2026.

Commercial fit (inference)

Wholesale and route-diversity partner only. IFN should not remain on an independent target list.

Possible transaction or partnership

IRU, wavelength, dark fiber, NNI, off-net enterprise access and data-center interconnection.

Constraints

Very large global platform, sponsor ownership, recent Crown integration and limited residential synergy.

Countercase

Any acquisition thesis based on buying IFN separately is obsolete. IFN has been integrated into Zayo since 2021.

Geographic clusters

  • Indianapolis
  • Fort Wayne
  • Columbus
  • Terre Haute
  • South Bend
  • major Midwest metros and long-haul corridors

Construction evidence

  • The 2021 IFN acquisition added more than 5,000 route miles and nearly 1,000 on-net enterprise buildings. At announcement, combined Indiana reach exceeded 7,500 route miles and 2,000 buildings, data centers and cell sites. The May 2026 Crown Fiber closing added a much larger national metro portfolio.

Reported scale and date

  • Former IFN: more than 5,000 route miles and nearly 1,000 on-net enterprise buildings as of June 1, 2021. Zayo's March 2025 pre-Crown disclosure was 146,000 route miles globally; current post-close network perimeter is far larger than a regional bolt-on.

Endeavor Communications

Cooperative / municipal partner | P2: selective

Current ownership

Member-owned Clay County Rural Telephone Cooperative operating as Endeavor Communications. Members govern the cooperative and receive or reinvest capital credits.

Commercial fit (inference)

Coherent smaller partnership alternative, especially for wholesale access or joint build, but structurally poor as an acquisition target.

Possible transaction or partnership

Wholesale, joint-build, reciprocal last-mile or business-services partnership that preserves cooperative ownership.

Constraints

Member governance, cooperative mission, regulated service obligations, undated scale and no disclosed route diversity.

Countercase

A sale would conflict with the current member-owned structure and capital-credit model. Partnership is materially more realistic than M&A.

Geographic clusters

  • Cloverdale
  • Putnam County
  • Hendricks County
  • Montgomery County and rural central-west Indiana

Construction evidence

  • Endeavor currently markets gigabit fiber and dedicated business internet up to 10 Gbps. Reviewed sources did not provide a dated 2025-2026 route-mile or passing build total.

Reported scale and date

  • More than 17,000 customers, company website accessed September 20, 2026. The page is undated and does not state route miles or passings.
